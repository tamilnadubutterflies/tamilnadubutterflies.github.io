Field guide notes for Pieridae · Pierinae (Whites, Orange-tips & Jezebels)

Family overview (Pieridae, Whites and yellows): Medium-to-small butterflies dominated by white, yellow, orange, and black pigments, ranging from garden grass yellows and emigrants to hill-dwelling clouded yellows and forest albatrosses.

Subfamily traits (Pierinae): Includes Delias jezebels, Cepora gulls, Appias albatrosses/puffins, Colotis/Ixias orange-tips, Hebomoia, and Prioneris.

Typical habitats: Spans dry scrub plains (Colotis), urban avenues (Delias), and wet evergreen hills (Appias, Prioneris).

Field photography tip: Capture forewing tip markings and underside hindwing vein patterns. Capture the closed-wing underside when possible, spot counts in Eurema and wingbar markings in Appias and Ixias are key underside clues.

தமிழ் அறிமுகம் & அடையாள சுருக்கம்:

இதன் தமிழ்ப் பெயர் மஞ்சள் எழிலி. Common Jezebel (Delias eucharis) என்பது வெள்ளையன்கள் (வெள்ளையன்கள் மற்றும் ஆரஞ்சு நுனிச்சிறகிகள்) குடும்பத்தைச் சேர்ந்த தமிழ்நாடு பட்டாம்பூச்சியாகும். iNaturalist தமிழ்நாடு திட்டத்தில் இதுவரை 546 பதிவுகள் பதிவாகியுள்ளன.

விரிவான இறக்கை அடையாளக் குறிப்புகள் ஆங்கிலத்தில்:

Striking white butterfly with bright yellow and scarlet-spotted underside

Identification marks

Upperwing: White wings crisply outlined with black veins and a black outer border framing white marginal spots.

Underwing: Unmistakable bright saffron-yellow hindwing framed by bold brick-red heart-shaped spots inside a black border.

Lookalikes: Only Painted Sawtooth (Prioneris sita) approaches its red-and-yellow underside pattern, but Painted Sawtooth is restricted to wet hills and has red spots closer to the margin.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Avenues, gardens, orchards, dry and moist forests where mistletoe parasitizes host trees.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Very common across Tamil Nadu plains, cities, and hills.

Seasonality: Year-round, noticeably abundant in winter and dry months.

Flight & behaviour

Flies slowly around tree canopies and glides gracefully down to Lantana and garden flowers.

Field note: Its bright red underside warns insectivorous birds of toxic compounds sequestered from mistletoe leaves.
