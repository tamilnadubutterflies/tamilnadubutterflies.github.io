Field guide notes for Hesperiidae · Coeliadinae (Awls, Awlets & Awlkings)

Family overview (Hesperiidae, Skippers): Compact, fast-darting butterflies (83 checklist species) such as awls, flats, angles, aces, bobs, demons, swifts, and grass darts.

Subfamily traits (Coeliadinae): Large, swift skippers active particularly at dawn, dusk, and shaded forest interiors; several possess metallic-green or bright orange markings.

Typical habitats: Dense hill forests, bamboo patches, shaded ravines, and coastal scrub.

Field photography tip: Focus on the underside hindwing white/blue bands and dark tornal spots. Photograph both the upperwing hyaline spots and the underside hindwing spot arcs, essential for distinguishing swifts and aces.

தமிழ் அறிமுகம் & அடையாள சுருக்கம்:

Plain Banded Awl (Hasora vitta) என்பது தாவிகள் (பெருந்தாவிகள் மற்றும் நாமத்தாவிகள்) குடும்பத்தைச் சேர்ந்த தமிழ்நாடு பட்டாம்பூச்சியாகும். iNaturalist தமிழ்நாடு திட்டத்தில் இதுவரை 7 பதிவுகள் பதிவாகியுள்ளன.

விரிவான இறக்கை அடையாளக் குறிப்புகள் ஆங்கிலத்தில்:

Purplish-glossed forest awl with an outwardly blurred white hindwing band

Identification marks

Upperwing: Dark bronzy-brown showing at least one tiny pale subapical dot in males (without a discal sex brand) and two larger yellowish discal spots plus subapical dots in females.

Underwing: Rich dark purplish-brown crossed by a broad creamy-white hindwing band whose inner edge is crisp while its outer margin blurs diffusely into the ground colour above a dark tornal lobe.

Lookalikes: Diffused outer edge of the white hindwing band and presence of a subapical forewing dot in both sexes separate it from Hasora chromus and Hasora taminatus.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Wet evergreen and semi-evergreen hill forests, stream corridors, and tall woody climber thickets.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Western Ghats hills of Tamil Nadu including Anamalai, Valparai, Nilgiris, Siruvani, and Kalakkad-Mundanthurai.

Seasonality: Mainly June to December during southwest and northeast monsoons.

Flight & behaviour

Fast, darting twilight flier that visits sunlit forest clearings in early morning, settles closed-winged beneath leaves, and occasionally takes moisture from damp sand or bird droppings.

Field note: Southern Indian populations belong to subspecies Hasora vitta indica; far scarcer in Tamil Nadu than the ubiquitous Hasora chromus.
