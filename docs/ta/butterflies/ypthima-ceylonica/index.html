Field guide notes for Nymphalidae · Satyrinae (Browns, Bushbrowns, Treebrowns & Rings)

Family overview (Nymphalidae, Brush-footed butterflies): Tamil Nadu’s largest butterfly family (97 checklist species), encompassing tigers, crows, pansies, eggflies, sailers, barons, rajahs, browns, and Tamil Nadu’s state butterfly, the Tamil Yeoman.

Subfamily traits (Satyrinae): Shade-loving brown butterflies marked with concentric underside eyespots (ocelli) that frequently exhibit wet- and dry-season forms.

Typical habitats: Grasslands, forest understorey, bamboo groves, palm groves, and shaded gardens.

Field photography tip: Underside ocelli sizes, brand marks, and discal line shape (straight vs curved) are crucial for species ID. Record both basking upperside and resting underside views, especially for bushbrowns, treebrowns, sailers, and oakleaf butterflies.

தமிழ் அறிமுகம் & அடையாள சுருக்கம்:

இதன் தமிழ்ப் பெயர் வெள்ளை நால்வளையன். White Four-ring (Ypthima ceylonica) என்பது வரியன்கள் (பழுப்பு மற்றும் நால்வளையன் சிறகிகள்) குடும்பத்தைச் சேர்ந்த தமிழ்நாடு பட்டாம்பூச்சியாகும். iNaturalist தமிழ்நாடு திட்டத்தில் இதுவரை 443 பதிவுகள் பதிவாகியுள்ளன.

விரிவான இறக்கை அடையாளக் குறிப்புகள் ஆங்கிலத்தில்:

Ubiquitous lawn and scrub ringlet flashing a bright white hindwing patch in flight

Identification marks

Upperwing: Dark brown forewing bearing one large bi-pupilled yellow-ringed subapical ocellus, paired with a striking pure white posterior half on the hindwing marked by 1-3 small dark submarginal spots. Sexes look closely alike, with females slightly paler.

Underwing: White finely striated with pale rufous-brown lines; forewing repeats the large bi-pupilled ocellus, while hindwing carries 4 yellow-ringed ocelli (one larger apical and three smaller linear postdiscal-tornal spots, the anal spot often bi-pupilled), reduced in dry forms.

Lookalikes: Unmistakable in Tamil Nadu by the stark white lower half of the hindwing upperwing, separating it immediately from Common Four-ring (Ypthima huebneri) and Common Five-ring (Ypthima baldus).

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Urban lawns, road verges, coastal scrub, wetland bunds, cultivated fields, and Ghats foothills.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Statewide from Chennai urban parks and coastal scrub to Coimbatore, Madurai, Trichy, Erode, Salem, and Western and Eastern Ghats foothills.

Seasonality: Abundant throughout the year across Tamil Nadu plains and lower hills.

Flight & behaviour

Low, fluttering grass-dweller active even on overcast days; visits small ground herbs like Tridax procumbens and Phyla nodiflora and rests near turf roots.

Field note: TNBS authors Hari Theivaprakasham, Hari Ramanasaran & Appavu Pavendhan documented its first complete immature stages from India and recorded Cynodon dactylon as a larval host plant in the Journal of Threatened Taxa (2021).
