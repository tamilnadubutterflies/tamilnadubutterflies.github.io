Field guide notes for Lycaenidae · Theclinae (Silverlines, Oakblues, Royals, Flashes & Yamfly)

Family overview (Lycaenidae, Blues and hairstreaks): Small, jewel-like butterflies (96 checklist species) including grass blues, pierrots, lineblues, ceruleans, silverlines, oakblues, royals, flashes, and sunbeams.

Subfamily traits (Theclinae): Often tailed hairstreaks with metallic silver bars (Cigaritis), deep violet-blue upperwings (Arhopala), or swift red-and-slate flashes (Rapala).

Typical habitats: Scrub jungles, forest canopies, mistletoe-bearing trees, and rocky hilltops.

Field photography tip: Capture the hindwing tornal lobes, tail count, and arrangement of underside bands. Sharp macro shots of the hindwing underside bands and spot alignments are usually far more diagnostic than upperside blue.

தமிழ் அறிமுகம் & அடையாள சுருக்கம்:

Redspot (Zesius chrysomallus) என்பது நீலன்கள் (வெள்ளிவரையன்கள் மற்றும் அரசநீலன்கள்) குடும்பத்தைச் சேர்ந்த தமிழ்நாடு பட்டாம்பூச்சியாகும். iNaturalist தமிழ்நாடு திட்டத்தில் இதுவரை 54 பதிவுகள் பதிவாகியுள்ளன.

விரிவான இறக்கை அடையாளக் குறிப்புகள் ஆங்கிலத்தில்:

Coppery-red male hairstreak whose caterpillars shelter inside weaver-ant nests

Identification marks

Upperwing: Strongly dimorphic: males are glowing pale coppery-red finely veined and narrowly edged in dark brown with silver-blue rimmed black anal spots, whereas females are dull rufous-brown richly glossed with pale metallic violet-blue over the basal and discal areas.

Underwing: Silvery grey-white marked with pale cinnamon-red to ochreous spots and wavy bands finely rimmed in dark brown and white, highlighted by a bold reddish spot near the forewing base and orange-crowned black tornal spots; males have two equal tails while females carry an extra third short tail at vein 3.

Lookalikes: Males cannot be confused with Indian Red Flash (Rapala iarbus) or Cornelian (Deudorix epijarbas) once the pale grey underside spotted with dark-rimmed red-brown bars and two tails are seen; females differ from Arhopala oakblues by their silvery-grey spotted underside.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Wooded plains, dry and moist deciduous forests, coastal groves, and orchards holding weaver-ant colonies.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Widespread in Tamil Nadu across Chennai coastal groves, Coimbatore, Kallar, Mudumalai, Erode, Pachamalai, Sirumalai, and Tirunelveli plains.

Seasonality: Flies nearly year-round across plains and foothills, peaking from August to February.

Flight & behaviour

Swift, dashing flight around canopy trees; males perch aggressively on sunlit outer branches and females search closely for weaver-ant nests before laying eggs.

Field note: Exhibits one of India's tightest butterfly-ant symbioses: larvae often rest inside silken leaf-nests built by weaver ants (Oecophylla smaragdina), which aggressively guard them.
