Field guide notes for Papilionidae · Papilioninae (True Swallowtails & Birdwings)

Family overview (Papilionidae, Swallowtails): Large to very large, strong-flying butterflies including birdwings, roses, mimes, Mormons, jays, swordtails, and banded peacocks. Many species carry prominent hindwing tails and feed actively while fluttering their wings at flowers.

Subfamily traits (Papilioninae): Includes Graphium jays and swordtails, Pachliopta roses, Papilio swallowtails, and the Southern Birdwing (Troides minos).

Typical habitats: Forest edges, hill streams, gardens, scrub, and evergreen shola margins across Tamil Nadu.

Field photography tip: Note hindwing tail shape, body colour (red vs black), and submarginal spot patterns. Both upperwing (dorsal) and underwing (ventral) photographs help separate mimics, mimes, and closely related dark Papilio species.

தமிழ் அறிமுகம் & அடையாள சுருக்கம்:

இதன் தமிழ்ப் பெயர் மயில் அழகி. Common Banded Peacock (Papilio crino) என்பது அழகிகள் (அழகிகள் மற்றும் பறவையிறகிகள்) குடும்பத்தைச் சேர்ந்த தமிழ்நாடு பட்டாம்பூச்சியாகும். iNaturalist தமிழ்நாடு திட்டத்தில் இதுவரை 415 பதிவுகள் பதிவாகியுள்ளன.

விரிவான இறக்கை அடையாளக் குறிப்புகள் ஆங்கிலத்தில்:

Emerald swallowtail & emblem of The Nature and Butterfly Society

Identification marks

Upperwing: Velvety black dusted uniformly with sparkling emerald-green scales and crossed by a bright metallic blue-green median band across both wings.

Underwing: Dark brown dusted with pale ochre scales and a series of orange-ringed blue lunules along the hindwing margin.

Lookalikes: Distinguished from Paris Peacock (Papilio paris) by the continuous median blue-green band across forewing and hindwing rather than a single isolated blue hindwing patch.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Deciduous and dry evergreen forests, wooded foothill gardens, scrub jungles, and hill streams.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Widespread across Tamil Nadu hills and plains, including Kallar, Siruvani, Coimbatore, Pachamalai, and Salem foothills.

Seasonality: Active throughout the year with peaks during and after monsoon months.

Flight & behaviour

Strong, rapid flier that hovers briefly while nectaring at Lantana and Clerodendrum flowers, and frequently mud-puddles along damp forest paths.

Field note: Recommended by TNBS members during state butterfly discussions (Pattampoochi Q2 2019) and depicted at the heart of the official TNBS society emblem.
