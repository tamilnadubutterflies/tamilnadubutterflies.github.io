Field guide notes for Lycaenidae · Theclinae (Silverlines, Oakblues, Royals, Flashes & Yamfly)

Family overview (Lycaenidae, Blues and hairstreaks): Small, jewel-like butterflies (96 checklist species) including grass blues, pierrots, lineblues, ceruleans, silverlines, oakblues, royals, flashes, and sunbeams.

Subfamily traits (Theclinae): Often tailed hairstreaks with metallic silver bars (Cigaritis), deep violet-blue upperwings (Arhopala), or swift red-and-slate flashes (Rapala).

Typical habitats: Scrub jungles, forest canopies, mistletoe-bearing trees, and rocky hilltops.

Field photography tip: Capture the hindwing tornal lobes, tail count, and arrangement of underside bands. Sharp macro shots of the hindwing underside bands and spot alignments are usually far more diagnostic than upperside blue.

தமிழ் அறிமுகம் & அடையாள சுருக்கம்:

Tufted White Royal (Pratapa deva) என்பது நீலன்கள் (வெள்ளிவரையன்கள் மற்றும் அரசநீலன்கள்) குடும்பத்தைச் சேர்ந்த தமிழ்நாடு பட்டாம்பூச்சியாகும். iNaturalist தமிழ்நாடு திட்டத்தில் இதுவரை 4 பதிவுகள் பதிவாகியுள்ளன.

விரிவான இறக்கை அடையாளக் குறிப்புகள் ஆங்கிலத்தில்:

Mistletoe royal whose males conceal a long black hair tuft beneath the forewing

Identification marks

Upperwing: Males are shining pale mauve-blue with broad jet-black apical and outer borders on the forewing and a rounded glandular costal patch on the hindwing; females are paler blue with dark-marked veins, narrower borders, and no costal patch.

Underwing: Creamy-white to pale greyish-white marked only by a broken series of thin dark postdiscal dashes, two orange-crowned black tornal spots with metallic blue scaling, and two delicate white-tipped tails; males carry a long black hair tuft along the bowed forewing inner margin.

Lookalikes: Separated from Peacock Royal (Tajuria cippus) and Plains Blue Royal (Tajuria jehana) by its paler mauve-blue male upperside with huge black apical area, broken underside dash line, and the male's prominent black forewing hair tuft.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Moist deciduous and evergreen hill forests, wooded valleys, and mistletoe-draped canopy trees.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Western Ghats ranges of Tamil Nadu including Nilgiris, Mudumalai, Anamalais, Palani Hills, and KMTR, plus wooded Eastern Ghats hills like Shervaroys and Pachamalai.

Seasonality: Recorded across most months in hill forests, peaking between September and March.

Flight & behaviour

Rapid canopy flyer that perches high on sunlit mistletoe clumps and outer tree branches, occasionally descending to hilltops or flowering shrubs in morning hours.

Field note: Represented in southern India by Pratapa deva deva; caterpillars mimic mistletoe buds and shoots while feeding unobtrusively high in host crowns.
