Field guide notes for Papilionidae · Papilioninae (True Swallowtails & Birdwings)

Family overview (Papilionidae, Swallowtails): Large to very large, strong-flying butterflies including birdwings, roses, mimes, Mormons, jays, swordtails, and banded peacocks. Many species carry prominent hindwing tails and feed actively while fluttering their wings at flowers.

Subfamily traits (Papilioninae): Includes Graphium jays and swordtails, Pachliopta roses, Papilio swallowtails, and the Southern Birdwing (Troides minos).

Typical habitats: Forest edges, hill streams, gardens, scrub, and evergreen shola margins across Tamil Nadu.

Field photography tip: Note hindwing tail shape, body colour (red vs black), and submarginal spot patterns. Both upperwing (dorsal) and underwing (ventral) photographs help separate mimics, mimes, and closely related dark Papilio species.

தமிழ் அறிமுகம் & அடையாள சுருக்கம்:

இதன் தமிழ்ப் பெயர் மலபார் அழகி. Malabar Rose (Pachliopta pandiyana) என்பது அழகிகள் (அழகிகள் மற்றும் பறவையிறகிகள்) குடும்பத்தைச் சேர்ந்த தமிழ்நாடு பட்டாம்பூச்சியாகும். iNaturalist தமிழ்நாடு திட்டத்தில் இதுவரை 11 பதிவுகள் பதிவாகியுள்ளன.

விரிவான இறக்கை அடையாளக் குறிப்புகள் ஆங்கிலத்தில்:

Black-and-white red-bodied swallowtail endemic to the Western Ghats

Identification marks

Upperwing: Deep soot-black forewings pale-streaked between outer veins, paired with black hindwings bearing a large creamy-white central patch and deep crimson submarginal crescents; thorax and abdomen sides bright red.

Underwing: Mirrors the upperwing pattern closely with sharper red marginal lunules and crimson abdominal warning markings.

Lookalikes: Much larger than the widespread Common Rose (Pachliopta aristolochiae), with an almost continuous pale white central hindwing band extending closer to the inner margin.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Heavy wet evergreen hill forests, riparian shola edges, and shaded forested ghats.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Restricted to moist Western Ghats sections of Tamil Nadu: Anamalai (Valparai, Topslip), Siruvani, Nilgiri slopes, and Tirunelveli-Kanyakumari hills.

Seasonality: Active year-round in wet evergreen belts, peaking between July and December.

Flight & behaviour

Floats majestically with slow, measured wingbeats along sunlit openings over forest streams and occasionally joins mixed swallowtail nectaring clusters on Mussaenda.

Field note: Named after the historic Pandya region of southern India and prized by visitors to wet evergreen Western Ghats trails.
