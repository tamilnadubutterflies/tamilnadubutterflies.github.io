Field guide notes for Pieridae · Coliadinae (Yellows, Emigrants & Clouded Yellows)

Family overview (Pieridae, Whites and yellows): Medium-to-small butterflies dominated by white, yellow, orange, and black pigments, ranging from garden grass yellows and emigrants to hill-dwelling clouded yellows and forest albatrosses.

Subfamily traits (Coliadinae): Yellow, pale green, or orange butterflies with rapid sustained flight and strong migratory behaviour in several species.

Typical habitats: Open grasslands, agricultural margins, scrub, riverbeds, and Nilgiri/Palani high-altitude meadows (Colias).

Field photography tip: For Eurema grass yellows, zoom into the underside forewing cell to count the small dark cell spots. Capture the closed-wing underside when possible, spot counts in Eurema and wingbar markings in Appias and Ixias are key underside clues.

தமிழ் அறிமுகம் & அடையாள சுருக்கம்:

இதன் தமிழ்ப் பெயர் முப்புள்ளி புல் மஞ்சள். Three-spot Grass Yellow (Eurema blanda) என்பது வெள்ளையன்கள் (புல் மஞ்சள் மற்றும் கொன்னை வெள்ளையன்கள்) குடும்பத்தைச் சேர்ந்த தமிழ்நாடு பட்டாம்பூச்சியாகும். iNaturalist தமிழ்நாடு திட்டத்தில் இதுவரை 456 பதிவுகள் பதிவாகியுள்ளன.

விரிவான இறக்கை அடையாளக் குறிப்புகள் ஆங்கிலத்தில்:

Widespread grass yellow distinguished by three forewing cell spots below

Identification marks

Upperwing: Vibrant canary-yellow in males (paler in females) framed by an uneven black forewing margin and narrow black scalloped hindwing rim.

Underwing: Yellow marked lightly with reddish-brown marginal speckles and three clear, separate dark spots inside the forewing cell (smallest near the base).

Lookalikes: Count the spots inside the underside forewing cell: Three-spot shows 3 distinct marks, Common Grass Yellow has 2, and One-spot has 1.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Gardens, wooded plains, moist deciduous foothills, forest glades, and avenue trees.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Statewide from Chennai, Salem, and Coimbatore plains up to 1,400 m in Eastern and Western Ghats hills.

Seasonality: Present year-round across Tamil Nadu with highest abundance post-monsoon.

Flight & behaviour

Flutters continuously near canopy saplings and dense roadside Cassia/Albizia avenues, gathering in loose mud-puddling groups.

Field note: Females often lay tidy egg clutches side-by-side on young Albizia and Gulmohar leaflets, resulting in synchronous caterpillar broods.
