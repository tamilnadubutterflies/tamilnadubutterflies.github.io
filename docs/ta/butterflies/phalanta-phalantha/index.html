Field guide notes for Nymphalidae · Heliconiinae (Fritillaries, Lacewings, Leopards, Coster & Yeoman)

Family overview (Nymphalidae, Brush-footed butterflies): Tamil Nadu’s largest butterfly family (97 checklist species), encompassing tigers, crows, pansies, eggflies, sailers, barons, rajahs, browns, and Tamil Nadu’s state butterfly, the Tamil Yeoman.

Subfamily traits (Heliconiinae): Bright orange-tawny butterflies with dark spots, serrated margins, or pearlescent underside bands, including Tamil Nadu’s state butterfly, Tamil Yeoman.

Typical habitats: Sunny clearings, grasslands, montane sholas, and forest paths.

Field photography tip: Compare hindwing underside bands and marginal scalloping to separate similar orange species. Record both basking upperside and resting underside views, especially for bushbrowns, treebrowns, sailers, and oakleaf butterflies.

தமிழ் அறிமுகம் & அடையாள சுருக்கம்:

இதன் தமிழ்ப் பெயர் சிறுத்தைச் சிறகன். Common Leopard (Phalanta phalantha) என்பது வரியன்கள் (தமிழ் மறவன் மற்றும் சிறுத்தைச் சிறகிகள்) குடும்பத்தைச் சேர்ந்த தமிழ்நாடு பட்டாம்பூச்சியாகும். iNaturalist தமிழ்நாடு திட்டத்தில் இதுவரை 583 பதிவுகள் பதிவாகியுள்ளன.

விரிவான இறக்கை அடையாளக் குறிப்புகள் ஆங்கிலத்தில்:

Bright orange leopard-spotted butterfly common in gardens and foothills

Identification marks

Upperwing: Bright golden-orange thickly scattered with neat rows of rounded black spots and wavy marginal lines.

Underwing: Glossy tawny-yellow washed with lilac sheen and delicate dark rings.

Lookalikes: More elongated forewings and crisp violet-tinted underside sheen separate it from Small Leopard (Phalanta alcippe) and Rustic.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Gardens, dry deciduous foothills, scrub, forest roads, and parks.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Widespread statewide from urban parks to hills.

Seasonality: Abundant throughout the year.

Flight & behaviour

Brisk, darting flight; suns briefly on shrubs with flatly spread wings and sips flower nectar constantly.

Field note: Thrives around both urban willow/Flacourtia hedges and forest paths, often joining mud-puddling assemblages.
