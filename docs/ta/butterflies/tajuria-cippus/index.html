Field guide notes for Lycaenidae · Theclinae (Silverlines, Oakblues, Royals, Flashes & Yamfly)

Family overview (Lycaenidae, Blues and hairstreaks): Small, jewel-like butterflies (96 checklist species) including grass blues, pierrots, lineblues, ceruleans, silverlines, oakblues, royals, flashes, and sunbeams.

Subfamily traits (Theclinae): Often tailed hairstreaks with metallic silver bars (Cigaritis), deep violet-blue upperwings (Arhopala), or swift red-and-slate flashes (Rapala).

Typical habitats: Scrub jungles, forest canopies, mistletoe-bearing trees, and rocky hilltops.

Field photography tip: Capture the hindwing tornal lobes, tail count, and arrangement of underside bands. Sharp macro shots of the hindwing underside bands and spot alignments are usually far more diagnostic than upperside blue.

தமிழ் அறிமுகம் & அடையாள சுருக்கம்:

Peacock Royal (Tajuria cippus) என்பது நீலன்கள் (வெள்ளிவரையன்கள் மற்றும் அரசநீலன்கள்) குடும்பத்தைச் சேர்ந்த தமிழ்நாடு பட்டாம்பூச்சியாகும். iNaturalist தமிழ்நாடு திட்டத்தில் இதுவரை 51 பதிவுகள் பதிவாகியுள்ளன.

விரிவான இறக்கை அடையாளக் குறிப்புகள் ஆங்கிலத்தில்:

Electric cerulean-blue royal with twin vivid orange-crowned tornal spots

Identification marks

Upperwing: Males flash brilliant glowing royal-blue framed by broad black apical and costal borders on the forewing; females are paler greyish-blue with dark veins, a prominent curved discal band of dark lunules on the hindwing, and wider blackish margins.

Underwing: Uniform soft dove-grey crossed by an interrupted postdiscal row of fine black dashes (often very faint on the forewing) and marked on the hindwing by two large jet-black tornal spots brightly crowned with vivid orange and two long white-tipped black tails.

Lookalikes: Males are brighter, richer blue than Plains Blue Royal (Tajuria jehana) and lack the forewing sex brand of Branded Royal (Tajuria melastigma); underside differs from Banded Royal (Rachana jalindra) by uniform dove-grey ground rather than half golden-brown and half white.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Wooded plains, riverine forests, deciduous hills, orchards, and canopy trees hosting heavy mistletoe growth.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Occurs widely across Tamil Nadu from Chennai, Coimbatore, and Trichy wooded campuses to Kallar, Mudumalai, Anamalais, KMTR, Pachamalai, and Shervaroys.

Seasonality: Seen year-round in wooded tracts, peaking between September and February.

Flight & behaviour

Swift, high-perching butterfly that circles sunlit tree crowns and hilltops in morning hours, occasionally descending to nectar on Lantana or wet sand.

Field note: Subspecies Tajuria cippus cippus shows striking pupal camouflage resembling a bird dropping or knotted mistletoe twig.
