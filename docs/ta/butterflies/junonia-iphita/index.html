Field guide notes for Nymphalidae · Nymphalinae (Pansies, Eggflies, Admirals & Oakleaf)

Family overview (Nymphalidae, Brush-footed butterflies): Tamil Nadu’s largest butterfly family (97 checklist species), encompassing tigers, crows, pansies, eggflies, sailers, barons, rajahs, browns, and Tamil Nadu’s state butterfly, the Tamil Yeoman.

Subfamily traits (Nymphalinae): Familiar, colourful butterflies featuring conspicuous eyespots, seasonal morphs, and dramatic dead-leaf underside camouflage.

Typical habitats: Gardens, wetlands, agricultural bunds, scrublands, and hill forests.

Field photography tip: In Junonia pansies and Hypolimnas eggflies, both dorsal colour patches and ventral patterns aid rapid ID. Record both basking upperside and resting underside views, especially for bushbrowns, treebrowns, sailers, and oakleaf butterflies.

தமிழ் அறிமுகம் & அடையாள சுருக்கம்:

இதன் தமிழ்ப் பெயர் செவலை வசீகரன். Chocolate Pansy (Junonia iphita) என்பது வரியன்கள் (வசீகரன்கள் மற்றும் மாய வரியன்கள்) குடும்பத்தைச் சேர்ந்த தமிழ்நாடு பட்டாம்பூச்சியாகும். iNaturalist தமிழ்நாடு திட்டத்தில் இதுவரை 1,426 பதிவுகள் பதிவாகியுள்ளன.

விரிவான இறக்கை அடையாளக் குறிப்புகள் ஆங்கிலத்தில்:

Chocolate-brown pansy common along shaded trails and wet forests

Identification marks

Upperwing: Rich dark chocolate-brown crossed by darker wavy bands and subtle pale marginal lines.

Underwing: Resembles a veined dead leaf with a faint pale central line and small indistinct eyespots.

Lookalikes: Uniform deep chocolate-brown tones and lack of lemon spots or conspicuous large upperwing eyespots distinguish it from other pansies.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Wooded paths, shaded parks, hill forests, wetlands, and bamboo thickets.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Common in both plains greenways and Western/Eastern Ghats forests.

Seasonality: Abundant year-round, especially in monsoon and winter.

Flight & behaviour

Flies low along forest paths and sits flat on sunlit leaves in dappled shade.

Field note: Shady forest roads and damp riverbanks often host multiple territorial males chasing each other in tight spirals.
