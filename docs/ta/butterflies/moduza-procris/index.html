Field guide notes for Nymphalidae · Limenitidinae (Sailers, Sergeants, Lascars, Barons & Clipper)

Family overview (Nymphalidae, Brush-footed butterflies): Tamil Nadu’s largest butterfly family (97 checklist species), encompassing tigers, crows, pansies, eggflies, sailers, barons, rajahs, browns, and Tamil Nadu’s state butterfly, the Tamil Yeoman.

Subfamily traits (Limenitidinae): Gliding butterflies with characteristic black-and-white banded wings (Neptis, Athyma) or emerald-olive camouflaged upperwings (Euthalia, Dophla).

Typical habitats: Forest trails, stream banks, orchards, and wooded gardens.

Field photography tip: Check whether the forewing cell streak is broken into spots or continuous, and compare underside ground colour. Record both basking upperside and resting underside views, especially for bushbrowns, treebrowns, sailers, and oakleaf butterflies.

தமிழ் அறிமுகம் & அடையாள சுருக்கம்:

Commander (Moduza procris) என்பது வரியன்கள் (கனிச்சிறகிகள் மற்றும் வெண்கறுப்பு சிறகிகள்) குடும்பத்தைச் சேர்ந்த தமிழ்நாடு பட்டாம்பூச்சியாகும். iNaturalist தமிழ்நாடு திட்டத்தில் இதுவரை 100 பதிவுகள் பதிவாகியுள்ளன.

விரிவான இறக்கை அடையாளக் குறிப்புகள் ஆங்கிலத்தில்:

Rusty-orange woodland butterfly with a bold white cross-band

Identification marks

Upperwing: Rich brick-red and dark brown wings bisected across both wings by a striking clean white macular band.

Underwing: Pale greenish-grey base shading into warm rusty orange beyond the crisp white central band.

Lookalikes: Bright brick-red upperwing ground combined with the bold white diagonal band makes Commander unmistakable.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Forest stream banks, wooded foothills, damp trails, and riparian gardens.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Western & Eastern Ghats foothills, Mudumalai, Pachamalai, Kovai Kutralam, and riverine green belts.

Seasonality: Common year-round in forested districts and foothills.

Flight & behaviour

Glides smoothly over bushes, perches with wings spread flat on sunlit leaves, and paddles at damp sand.

Field note: Caterpillars tie leaf frass chains onto midribs of Mitragyna saplings to deter crawling predators.
