Field guide notes for Nymphalidae · Charaxinae (Rajahs & Nawabs)

Family overview (Nymphalidae, Brush-footed butterflies): Tamil Nadu’s largest butterfly family (97 checklist species), encompassing tigers, crows, pansies, eggflies, sailers, barons, rajahs, browns, and Tamil Nadu’s state butterfly, the Tamil Yeoman.

Subfamily traits (Charaxinae): Stout-bodied, exceptionally fast-flying canopy butterflies often attracted to tree sap, rotting fruit, and damp sand.

Typical habitats: Deciduous and evergreen hill forests, foothills, and wooded estates.

Field photography tip: Underside pale bands and tail length separate Common, Anomalous, and Blue Nawabs. Record both basking upperside and resting underside views, especially for bushbrowns, treebrowns, sailers, and oakleaf butterflies.

தமிழ் அறிமுகம் & அடையாள சுருக்கம்:

இதன் தமிழ்ப் பெயர் கறுப்புச் சிறகன். Black Rajah (Charaxes solon) என்பது வரியன்கள் (இரட்டைவால் சிறகிகள் மற்றும் இராஜாக்கள்) குடும்பத்தைச் சேர்ந்த தமிழ்நாடு பட்டாம்பூச்சியாகும்.

விரிவான இறக்கை அடையாளக் குறிப்புகள் ஆங்கிலத்தில்:

Agile black-and-lime rajah bred widely on roadside tamarind trees

Identification marks

Upperwing: Velvety brownish-black wings crossed by a crisp narrow pale greenish-white median band breaking into discrete spots toward the forewing apex, accompanied by a neat submarginal row of pale dots; hindwing carries two pointed tails that are noticeably longer in females.

Underwing: Silvery grey-brown finely etched with dark reddish-brown hairlines, repeating the pale discal band alongside orange-ochre touches near the hindwing tornus.

Lookalikes: Darker ground color and much narrower, macular greenish-white median band easily distinguish it from Indian Tawny Rajah (Charaxes psaphon) and Indian Nawab (Charaxes bharata).

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Tamarind-lined plains avenues, urban parks, dry scrublands, village groves, and deciduous foothills.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Statewide across plains and lower hills including Chennai, Chengalpattu, Coimbatore, Erode, Madurai, Salem, Tiruchirappalli, and Ghats foothills.

Seasonality: Active year-round across Tamil Nadu plains and foothills, peaking during August-January.

Flight & behaviour

Darts rapidly around tall tree crowns and returns repeatedly to favoured exposed twigs; feeds eagerly on fermenting tree sap, overripe fruit, and damp mud rather than flowers.

Field note: One of the few large Charaxinae thriving in Tamil Nadu towns and farmlands thanks to widespread planted Tamarindus indica avenues.
