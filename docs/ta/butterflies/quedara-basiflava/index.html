Field guide notes for Hesperiidae · Hesperiinae (Grass Skippers, Swifts, Aces & Darts)

Family overview (Hesperiidae, Skippers): Compact, fast-darting butterflies (83 checklist species) such as awls, flats, angles, aces, bobs, demons, swifts, and grass darts.

Subfamily traits (Hesperiinae): Fast-flying tawny, brown, or black-and-yellow skippers that typically hold forewings and hindwings at different angles when basking.

Typical habitats: Grasslands, paddy margins, palms, bamboo thickets, and forest undergrowth.

Field photography tip: Zoom into the underside hindwing spot pattern and forewing cell spots to distinguish swifts and darts. Photograph both the upperwing hyaline spots and the underside hindwing spot arcs, essential for distinguishing swifts and aces.

தமிழ் அறிமுகம் & அடையாள சுருக்கம்:

Golden Flitter (Quedara basiflava) என்பது தாவிகள் (புல்தாவிகள் மற்றும் தென்னைத்தாவிகள்) குடும்பத்தைச் சேர்ந்த தமிழ்நாடு பட்டாம்பூச்சியாகும். iNaturalist தமிழ்நாடு திட்டத்தில் இதுவரை 10 பதிவுகள் பதிவாகியுள்ளன.

விரிவான இறக்கை அடையாளக் குறிப்புகள் ஆங்கிலத்தில்:

Endemic evergreen flitter hiding a bright golden-yellow hindwing base beneath

Identification marks

Upperwing: Glossy blackish-brown forewing crossed by a broad, conspicuous creamy-white translucent oblique band formed by conjoined spots across the cell end and spaces 2-3 (females adding a small spot in space 1b and minute subapical specks); hindwing completely plain dark.

Underwing: Forewing dark brown repeating the broad white median band; hindwing immediately diagnostic with a bright chrome-yellow basal patch covering the inner third of the wing against glossy dark brown outer scaling.

Lookalikes: The vivid golden-yellow basal patch on the dark hindwing underside separates Golden Flitter instantly from Common Banded Demon (Notocrypta paralysos), Tree Flitter (Hyarotis adrastus), and Brush Flitter (Hyarotis coorga).

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Wet evergreen hill forests, dense rattan gullies, shaded stream valleys, and humid reed-cane undergrowth.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Endemic to the Western Ghats; known in Tamil Nadu from Anamalais, Siruvani, Nilgiri slopes, and Kalakkad-Mundanthurai.

Seasonality: Recorded primarily from August to February across humid evergreen valleys.

Flight & behaviour

Fast, shadow-hugging flight low along shaded cane thickets; settles with wings closed over the back displaying the golden hindwing base, or suns briefly in weak forest light and feeds at Strobilanthes and Leea flowers.

Field note: A Western Ghats endemic whose cryptic closed-wing profile flashes sudden golden-yellow when the skipper shifts posture in dim evergreen ravines.
