Field guide notes for Nymphalidae · Limenitidinae (Sailers, Sergeants, Lascars, Barons & Clipper)

Family overview (Nymphalidae, Brush-footed butterflies): Tamil Nadu’s largest butterfly family (97 checklist species), encompassing tigers, crows, pansies, eggflies, sailers, barons, rajahs, browns, and Tamil Nadu’s state butterfly, the Tamil Yeoman.

Subfamily traits (Limenitidinae): Gliding butterflies with characteristic black-and-white banded wings (Neptis, Athyma) or emerald-olive camouflaged upperwings (Euthalia, Dophla).

Typical habitats: Forest trails, stream banks, orchards, and wooded gardens.

Field photography tip: Check whether the forewing cell streak is broken into spots or continuous, and compare underside ground colour. Record both basking upperside and resting underside views, especially for bushbrowns, treebrowns, sailers, and oakleaf butterflies.

தமிழ் அறிமுகம் & அடையாள சுருக்கம்:

Colour Sergeant (Athyma inara) என்பது வரியன்கள் (கனிச்சிறகிகள் மற்றும் வெண்கறுப்பு சிறகிகள்) குடும்பத்தைச் சேர்ந்த தமிழ்நாடு பட்டாம்பூச்சியாகும். iNaturalist தமிழ்நாடு திட்டத்தில் இதுவரை 11 பதிவுகள் பதிவாகியுள்ளன.

விரிவான இறக்கை அடையாளக் குறிப்புகள் ஆங்கிலத்தில்:

Dramatically dimorphic sergeant pairing black-and-white males with orange females

Identification marks

Upperwing: Strikingly dimorphic: males are velvety black with a crisp white central band across both wings, bright orange forewing apical patches, and broken ochre-white cell spots; females are blackish-brown crossed by broad vivid golden-orange bands.

Underwing: Males are rich rufous-brown repeating the white central band and orange apical flush; females are warm ochre-yellow with paler creamy-orange bands framed by dark brown discal blotches.

Lookalikes: Males differ from all other Tamil Nadu sergeants by pairing a white central band with bright orange forewing tips; orange-banded females could be confused with lascars (Pantoporia) or Yellow Jack Sailer (Lasippa viraja), but are larger with stout Athyma bodies and divided cell markings.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Evergreen, semi-evergreen, and moist deciduous hill forests along shaded streams and forest edges.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Localized in Western Ghats forests of Tamil Nadu, including Nilgiris, Mudumalai, Siruvani, Anamalai, and Kalakkad-Mundanthurai.

Seasonality: Most regular between August and January, especially along hill streams after rains.

Flight & behaviour

Strong, swift flier that perches high on sunny canopy leaves with open wings and visits wet sandbanks and flower clusters along streams.

Field note: Often treated historically as Athyma nefte inara; its extreme sexual dichromatism makes male and female appear like two unrelated butterfly species in the field.
