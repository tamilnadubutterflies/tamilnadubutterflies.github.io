Field guide notes for Lycaenidae · Theclinae (Silverlines, Oakblues, Royals, Flashes & Yamfly)

Family overview (Lycaenidae, Blues and hairstreaks): Small, jewel-like butterflies (96 checklist species) including grass blues, pierrots, lineblues, ceruleans, silverlines, oakblues, royals, flashes, and sunbeams.

Subfamily traits (Theclinae): Often tailed hairstreaks with metallic silver bars (Cigaritis), deep violet-blue upperwings (Arhopala), or swift red-and-slate flashes (Rapala).

Typical habitats: Scrub jungles, forest canopies, mistletoe-bearing trees, and rocky hilltops.

Field photography tip: Capture the hindwing tornal lobes, tail count, and arrangement of underside bands. Sharp macro shots of the hindwing underside bands and spot alignments are usually far more diagnostic than upperside blue.

தமிழ் அறிமுகம் & அடையாள சுருக்கம்:

Many-tailed Oakblue (Thaduka multicaudata) என்பது நீலன்கள் (வெள்ளிவரையன்கள் மற்றும் அரசநீலன்கள்) குடும்பத்தைச் சேர்ந்த தமிழ்நாடு பட்டாம்பூச்சியாகும். iNaturalist தமிழ்நாடு திட்டத்தில் இதுவரை 26 பதிவுகள் பதிவாகியுள்ளன.

விரிவான இறக்கை அடையாளக் குறிப்புகள் ஆங்கிலத்தில்:

Unmistakable Sahyadri oakblue sporting three bold tails and scalloped wings

Identification marks

Upperwing: Both wings are black with a bright silvery-smalt blue basal and discal field, slightly broader and paler blue in females, framed by deeply scalloped hindwing margins and three black tails.

Underwing: Dark vinous-brown to purplish-grey crossed by square dark discal spots and angled submarginal lines on the forewing, irregular broken wavy dark bands on the hindwing, and three tails at veins 1b, 2, and 3 with the middle tail longest.

Lookalikes: Distinct from all true Arhopala species by its scalloped wing outline, silver-blue dorsal sheen inside wide black margins, and three prominent tails lacking the white costal spot of Aberrant Bushblue (Arhopala abseus).

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Wet evergreen and semi-evergreen hill forests, wooded river gorges, and shaded forest tracks.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Southern and central Western Ghats of Tamil Nadu including Nilgiris, Mudumalai, Anamalais, Siruvani, and Kalakkad-Mundanthurai.

Seasonality: Scarce and localized, seen mostly between October and April.

Flight & behaviour

Fast, erratic flyer that perches high on shaded subcanopy branches and drops down briefly to damp sand or sunlit forest paths during morning hours.

Field note: Only species in the genus Thaduka; southern Indian populations belong to the Western Ghats subspecies Thaduka multicaudata kanara.
