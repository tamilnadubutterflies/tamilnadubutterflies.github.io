Field guide notes for Nymphalidae · Heliconiinae (Fritillaries, Lacewings, Leopards, Coster & Yeoman)

Family overview (Nymphalidae, Brush-footed butterflies): Tamil Nadu’s largest butterfly family (97 checklist species), encompassing tigers, crows, pansies, eggflies, sailers, barons, rajahs, browns, and Tamil Nadu’s state butterfly, the Tamil Yeoman.

Subfamily traits (Heliconiinae): Bright orange-tawny butterflies with dark spots, serrated margins, or pearlescent underside bands, including Tamil Nadu’s state butterfly, Tamil Yeoman.

Typical habitats: Sunny clearings, grasslands, montane sholas, and forest paths.

Field photography tip: Compare hindwing underside bands and marginal scalloping to separate similar orange species. Record both basking upperside and resting underside views, especially for bushbrowns, treebrowns, sailers, and oakleaf butterflies.

தமிழ் அறிமுகம் & அடையாள சுருக்கம்:

Cruiser (Vindula erota) என்பது வரியன்கள் (தமிழ் மறவன் மற்றும் சிறுத்தைச் சிறகிகள்) குடும்பத்தைச் சேர்ந்த தமிழ்நாடு பட்டாம்பூச்சியாகும். iNaturalist தமிழ்நாடு திட்டத்தில் இதுவரை 32 பதிவுகள் பதிவாகியுள்ளன.

விரிவான இறக்கை அடையாளக் குறிப்புகள் ஆங்கிலத்தில்:

Large tailed orange-and-olive brush-footed butterfly of hill streams

Identification marks

Upperwing: Males are bright golden-orange; females are dramatically larger, dark olive-brown crossed by a wide white median band.

Underwing: Ochre-orange in males or brownish-white in females with subtle violet wash and twin hindwing eyespots.

Lookalikes: Short pointed hindwing tails and larger size cleanly separate Cruiser males from Tamil Yeoman.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Moist deciduous and evergreen hill forests, especially along sunlit forest streams.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Mudumalai, Siruvani, Kallar, Anamalai, Palani Hills, and southern Western Ghats.

Seasonality: Year-round in Western Ghats hills, peaking post-monsoon.

Flight & behaviour

Males puddle eagerly along damp forest tracks and stream pebbles; females patrol canopy clearings.

Field note: Sexual dimorphism is so pronounced that early collectors described bright orange males and olive-green females separately.
