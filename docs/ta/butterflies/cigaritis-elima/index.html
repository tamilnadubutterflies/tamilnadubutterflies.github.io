Field guide notes for Lycaenidae · Theclinae (Silverlines, Oakblues, Royals, Flashes & Yamfly)

Family overview (Lycaenidae, Blues and hairstreaks): Small, jewel-like butterflies (96 checklist species) including grass blues, pierrots, lineblues, ceruleans, silverlines, oakblues, royals, flashes, and sunbeams.

Subfamily traits (Theclinae): Often tailed hairstreaks with metallic silver bars (Cigaritis), deep violet-blue upperwings (Arhopala), or swift red-and-slate flashes (Rapala).

Typical habitats: Scrub jungles, forest canopies, mistletoe-bearing trees, and rocky hilltops.

Field photography tip: Capture the hindwing tornal lobes, tail count, and arrangement of underside bands. Sharp macro shots of the hindwing underside bands and spot alignments are usually far more diagnostic than upperside blue.

தமிழ் அறிமுகம் & அடையாள சுருக்கம்:

Scarce Shot Silverline (Cigaritis elima) என்பது நீலன்கள் (வெள்ளிவரையன்கள் மற்றும் அரசநீலன்கள்) குடும்பத்தைச் சேர்ந்த தமிழ்நாடு பட்டாம்பூச்சியாகும். iNaturalist தமிழ்நாடு திட்டத்தில் இதுவரை 7 பதிவுகள் பதிவாகியுள்ளன.

விரிவான இறக்கை அடையாளக் குறிப்புகள் ஆங்கிலத்தில்:

Acute-winged dry-scrub silverline with pale red-edged ventral bands

Identification marks

Upperwing: Forewings are distinctly pointed at the apex; males are brown glossed with steely smalt-blue over the lower forewing and central hindwing with a small subapical reddish patch enclosing two dark spots, while females are browner without blue and display a large triangular orange-red forewing patch.

Underwing: Pale sandy testaceous to straw-buff with very pale, delicate reddish-edged transverse bands traversed by thin silver lines; the forewing lacks a separate basal spot below the cell, and the hindwing anal lobe projects prominently ahead of two fine tails.

Lookalikes: Differs from the closely similar Common Shot Silverline (Cigaritis ictis) by its sharper forewing tip, bright smalt-blue sheen on the male upperside, and paler sandy underside lacking the extra subbasal forewing spot.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Dry thorn scrub, rain-shadow foothill savannahs, rocky dry hill slopes, and open deciduous woodland.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Rain-shadow plains and eastern foothill tracts of Tamil Nadu including Coimbatore, Sathyamangalam, Nilgiri eastern slopes, Dharmapuri, and Madurai.

Seasonality: Observed mainly during post-monsoon and dry winter months from October to April.

Flight & behaviour

Swifter and warier than Cigaritis vulcanus; settles briefly on bare twigs, dry soil patches, and small flowering Acacia herbs in hot midday sun.

Field note: Far scarcer in Tamil Nadu than Cigaritis ictis; early stages remain poorly documented in the southern hills, though larvae depend strictly on Crematogaster ants.
