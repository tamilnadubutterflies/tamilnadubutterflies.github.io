Field guide notes for Nymphalidae · Satyrinae (Browns, Bushbrowns, Treebrowns & Rings)

Family overview (Nymphalidae, Brush-footed butterflies): Tamil Nadu’s largest butterfly family (97 checklist species), encompassing tigers, crows, pansies, eggflies, sailers, barons, rajahs, browns, and Tamil Nadu’s state butterfly, the Tamil Yeoman.

Subfamily traits (Satyrinae): Shade-loving brown butterflies marked with concentric underside eyespots (ocelli) that frequently exhibit wet- and dry-season forms.

Typical habitats: Grasslands, forest understorey, bamboo groves, palm groves, and shaded gardens.

Field photography tip: Underside ocelli sizes, brand marks, and discal line shape (straight vs curved) are crucial for species ID. Record both basking upperside and resting underside views, especially for bushbrowns, treebrowns, sailers, and oakleaf butterflies.

தமிழ் அறிமுகம் & அடையாள சுருக்கம்:

இதன் தமிழ்ப் பெயர் அந்திச் சிறகன். Common Evening Brown (Melanitis leda) என்பது வரியன்கள் (பழுப்பு மற்றும் நால்வளையன் சிறகிகள்) குடும்பத்தைச் சேர்ந்த தமிழ்நாடு பட்டாம்பூச்சியாகும். iNaturalist தமிழ்நாடு திட்டத்தில் இதுவரை 879 பதிவுகள் பதிவாகியுள்ளன.

விரிவான இறக்கை அடையாளக் குறிப்புகள் ஆங்கிலத்தில்:

Crepuscular brown butterfly masters of seasonal dry-leaf camouflage

Identification marks

Upperwing: Dark earthy brown bearing a prominent black eyespot with two crisp white pupils and bright orange halos near the forewing tip.

Underwing: Shows dramatic seasonal polyphenism: wet-season butterflies carry crisp concentric eyespots, while dry-season morphs turn into intricately mottled dead leaves.

Lookalikes: Common Evening Brown has larger orange halos around the white forewing spots than Dark Evening Brown (Melanitis phedima).

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Shaded gardens, bamboo clumps, coconut groves, paddy bunds, and forest understorey.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Very common across plains, farms, urban gardens, and forests throughout Tamil Nadu.

Seasonality: Year-round statewide; dry-season form dominates December–May.

Flight & behaviour

Rests camouflaged among fallen leaf litter by day and becomes briskly active at dusk and dawn, feeding on ripe fruit and sap.

Field note: Famous for extreme dry-season leaf polymorphism, where no two dry-season individuals look identically patterned underneath.
