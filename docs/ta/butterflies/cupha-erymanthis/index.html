Field guide notes for Nymphalidae · Heliconiinae (Fritillaries, Lacewings, Leopards, Coster & Yeoman)

Family overview (Nymphalidae, Brush-footed butterflies): Tamil Nadu’s largest butterfly family (97 checklist species), encompassing tigers, crows, pansies, eggflies, sailers, barons, rajahs, browns, and Tamil Nadu’s state butterfly, the Tamil Yeoman.

Subfamily traits (Heliconiinae): Bright orange-tawny butterflies with dark spots, serrated margins, or pearlescent underside bands, including Tamil Nadu’s state butterfly, Tamil Yeoman.

Typical habitats: Sunny clearings, grasslands, montane sholas, and forest paths.

Field photography tip: Compare hindwing underside bands and marginal scalloping to separate similar orange species. Record both basking upperside and resting underside views, especially for bushbrowns, treebrowns, sailers, and oakleaf butterflies.

தமிழ் அறிமுகம் & அடையாள சுருக்கம்:

இதன் தமிழ்ப் பெயர் காட்டுக்களா சிறகன். Rustic (Cupha erymanthis) என்பது வரியன்கள் (தமிழ் மறவன் மற்றும் சிறுத்தைச் சிறகிகள்) குடும்பத்தைச் சேர்ந்த தமிழ்நாடு பட்டாம்பூச்சியாகும். iNaturalist தமிழ்நாடு திட்டத்தில் இதுவரை 167 பதிவுகள் பதிவாகியுள்ளன.

விரிவான இறக்கை அடையாளக் குறிப்புகள் ஆங்கிலத்தில்:

Bright yellow-banded woodland nymphalid common along forested hill streams

Identification marks

Upperwing: Warm ochre-brown crossed by a broad curved pale golden-yellow forewing median band enclosing black spots, set off by a broad black forewing apex and fine wavy marginal lines; sexes look similar.

Underwing: Pale tawny-buff with a cream-yellow forewing median band, narrow dark transverse discal lines, and a continuous row of small dark-centered postdiscal spots.

Lookalikes: Immediately told apart from Tamil Yeoman (Cirrochroa thais) and Common Leopard (Phalanta phalantha) by its broad pale yellow forewing band enclosed by a bold black wingtip.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Moist deciduous and evergreen forests, wooded hill streams, coffee estates, and Eastern Ghats shola-fringe valleys.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Widespread across Western Ghats (Mudumalai, Siruvani, Kallar, Anamalai, KMTR) and Eastern Ghats hill complexes including Pachamalai, Kolli, and Shervarayan hills.

Seasonality: Recorded year-round in hill ranges, surging strongly from August to December.

Flight & behaviour

Active, jerky flyer patrolling sunlit forest edges and stream trails; feeds eagerly at Lantana, Chromolaena, and wild Ixora, and males gather at damp sand.

Field note: Represented in peninsular India by Cupha erymanthis maja; its bright yellow forewing band flashes distinctly during rapid zig-zag flights along forest paths.
