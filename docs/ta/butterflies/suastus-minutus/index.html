Field guide notes for Hesperiidae · Hesperiinae (Grass Skippers, Swifts, Aces & Darts)

Family overview (Hesperiidae, Skippers): Compact, fast-darting butterflies (83 checklist species) such as awls, flats, angles, aces, bobs, demons, swifts, and grass darts.

Subfamily traits (Hesperiinae): Fast-flying tawny, brown, or black-and-yellow skippers that typically hold forewings and hindwings at different angles when basking.

Typical habitats: Grasslands, paddy margins, palms, bamboo thickets, and forest undergrowth.

Field photography tip: Zoom into the underside hindwing spot pattern and forewing cell spots to distinguish swifts and darts. Photograph both the upperwing hyaline spots and the underside hindwing spot arcs, essential for distinguishing swifts and aces.

தமிழ் அறிமுகம் & அடையாள சுருக்கம்:

Small Palm Bob (Suastus minutus) என்பது தாவிகள் (புல்தாவிகள் மற்றும் தென்னைத்தாவிகள்) குடும்பத்தைச் சேர்ந்த தமிழ்நாடு பட்டாம்பூச்சியாகும்.

விரிவான இறக்கை அடையாளக் குறிப்புகள் ஆங்கிலத்தில்:

Rattan-dwelling palm bob with sparse black dots on a blue-grey hindwing below

Identification marks

Upperwing: Dark blackish-brown; males of the southern subspecies are nearly plain above or retain only obscure pale specks on the forewing, while females bear small white spots in the cell and spaces 2-3; hindwing plain dark brown.

Underwing: Forewing brown with faint pale marks at the cell end and space 1b; hindwing dusted smoothly in pale bluish-grey and marked with just three or four small black dots (a cell-end dot, an inner-space spot, and a tornal spot).

Lookalikes: Separated from Indian Palm Bob (Suastus gremius) by smaller size, plain or weakly spotted male upperwings, and far fewer black spots on a distinctly bluer-grey hindwing underside; separated from Vindhyan Bob (Arnetta vindhiana) and Maculate Lancer (Salanoemia sala) by sharp black dots lacking a ring-circle pattern.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Primary evergreen forest understorey, cane brakes, shaded palm and rattan gullies, and wet foothill streams.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Southern and central Western Ghats of Tamil Nadu including Siruvani, Anamalais, Kalakkad-Mundanthurai, and Kanyakumari forests.

Seasonality: Recorded chiefly from July to January across wet Western Ghats slopes, with scattered dry-season sightings.

Flight & behaviour

Quick, jerky flight low among forest rattan shoots; settles on sunlit fern blades or stream rocks in a half-open basking stance and feeds on bird droppings and small forest blossoms.

Field note: Represented in southern India by the Western Ghats subspecies Suastus minutus bipunctus, closely associated with wild Calamus rattans in undisturbed hill forests.
