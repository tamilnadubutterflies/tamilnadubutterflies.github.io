Field guide notes for Nymphalidae · Biblidinae (Castors & Joker)

Family overview (Nymphalidae, Brush-footed butterflies): Tamil Nadu’s largest butterfly family (97 checklist species), encompassing tigers, crows, pansies, eggflies, sailers, barons, rajahs, browns, and Tamil Nadu’s state butterfly, the Tamil Yeoman.

Subfamily traits (Biblidinae): Warm brown or orange-black butterflies with wavy transverse bands and slightly scalloped wing margins.

Typical habitats: Open scrub, gardens, castor stands, and dry deciduous foothills.

Field photography tip: Check wing margin contour (angled vs rounded) and hindwing sub-basal wavy lines. Record both basking upperside and resting underside views, especially for bushbrowns, treebrowns, sailers, and oakleaf butterflies.

தமிழ் அறிமுகம் & அடையாள சுருக்கம்:

Joker (Byblia ilithyia) என்பது வரியன்கள் (ஆமணக்கு வரியன்கள்) குடும்பத்தைச் சேர்ந்த தமிழ்நாடு பட்டாம்பூச்சியாகும். iNaturalist தமிழ்நாடு திட்டத்தில் இதுவரை 342 பதிவுகள் பதிவாகியுள்ளன.

விரிவான இறக்கை அடையாளக் குறிப்புகள் ஆங்கிலத்தில்:

Orange scrubland butterfly displaying bold black-edged white bands underneath

Identification marks

Upperwing: Bright rich orange overlaid with bold black costal bars, irregular black cell streaks, wavy discal bands, and a black-spotted orange margin; females are slightly paler with heavier black submarginal bands.

Underwing: Warm ochre-orange crossed prominently on the hindwing by three creamy-white transverse bands boldly edged by rows of black spots and lines, bordered by checkered black-and-white fringes.

Lookalikes: Separated from Common and Angled Castors (Ariadne spp.) by its smooth rounded wing margins and bold black-edged creamy-white bands across the hindwing underside; lacks the elongated abdomen and white-spotted black hindwing margin of Tawny Coster (Acraea terpsicore).

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Dry thorny scrub, arid grasslands, coastal plains, fallow agricultural edges, and open savanna foothills.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Common in dry plains and scrub belts across Coimbatore, Tiruchirappalli, Madurai, Ramanathapuram, Tirunelveli, and Eastern Ghats foothills.

Seasonality: Most abundant from September to March following northeast and southwest monsoon showers.

Flight & behaviour

Weak, fluttering flight close to dry grasses and stinging Tragia vines; settles frequently on bare earth, low herbs, and Tridax blooms with wings half-open.

Field note: One of the few butterflies in Tamil Nadu thriving on stinging Tragia creepers; the dry-season brood shows expanded creamy bands underneath for resting camouflage on dry grass stems.
