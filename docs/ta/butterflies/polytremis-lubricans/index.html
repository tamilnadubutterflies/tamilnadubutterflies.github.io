Field guide notes for Hesperiidae · Hesperiinae (Grass Skippers, Swifts, Aces & Darts)

Family overview (Hesperiidae, Skippers): Compact, fast-darting butterflies (83 checklist species) such as awls, flats, angles, aces, bobs, demons, swifts, and grass darts.

Subfamily traits (Hesperiinae): Fast-flying tawny, brown, or black-and-yellow skippers that typically hold forewings and hindwings at different angles when basking.

Typical habitats: Grasslands, paddy margins, palms, bamboo thickets, and forest undergrowth.

Field photography tip: Zoom into the underside hindwing spot pattern and forewing cell spots to distinguish swifts and darts. Photograph both the upperwing hyaline spots and the underside hindwing spot arcs, essential for distinguishing swifts and aces.

தமிழ் அறிமுகம் & அடையாள சுருக்கம்:

Contiguous Swift (Polytremis lubricans) என்பது தாவிகள் (புல்தாவிகள் மற்றும் தென்னைத்தாவிகள்) குடும்பத்தைச் சேர்ந்த தமிழ்நாடு பட்டாம்பூச்சியாகும். iNaturalist தமிழ்நாடு திட்டத்தில் இதுவரை 18 பதிவுகள் பதிவாகியுள்ளன.

விரிவான இறக்கை அடையாளக் குறிப்புகள் ஆங்கிலத்தில்:

Ochre-tinted swift recognized by fused pale yellow forewing cell spots

Identification marks

Upperwing: Dark golden-olive brown clothed basally in warm ochreous hairs; forewing carries pale yellow translucent spots featuring two elongated cell spots closely fused together, staggered discal spots in spaces 1b-4, and three subapical spots; hindwing shows 2-3 small pale yellow discal dots.

Underwing: Warm rusty ochreous-brown washed evenly across the hindwing, carrying a neat curved row of 3-4 tiny pale yellow dark-ringed discal dots in spaces 2-5 with no white cell spot; forewing repeats the conjoined cell marks.

Lookalikes: Fused pale yellow forewing cell spots and warm rusty-ochre hindwing underside lacking a cell spot separate Contiguous Swift cleanly from Pelopidas branded swifts and Caltoris swifts.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Forest clearings, bamboo borders, grassy hill tracks, overgrown estate edges, and stream-side meadows.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Western Ghats hills and foothills (Nilgiris, Mudumalai, Siruvani, Anamalais, Palani Hills, Kanyakumari) as well as Kolli Hills and Shevaroys.

Seasonality: Common from June to January in hills and foothills, scarcer during dry summer months.

Flight & behaviour

Fast, agile flier patrolling sunlit openings near woodland borders; basks in two-angle jet-plane posture on ferns and grass heads and feeds at Lantana, Ageratum, and damp mineral mud.

Field note: Unlike Pelopidas and Borbo whose cell spots stay neatly separated, the fused hourglass-shaped forewing cell spot gives this species its vernacular name.
