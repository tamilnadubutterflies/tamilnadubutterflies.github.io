Field guide notes for Lycaenidae · Polyommatinae (Grass Blues, Pierrots, Lineblues & Ceruleans)

Family overview (Lycaenidae, Blues and hairstreaks): Small, jewel-like butterflies (96 checklist species) including grass blues, pierrots, lineblues, ceruleans, silverlines, oakblues, royals, flashes, and sunbeams.

Subfamily traits (Polyommatinae): Delicate small blues with distinctive underside spot chains, zebra stripes, or white-and-black pied markings.

Typical habitats: Lawns, grasslands, damp sand, woodland edges, and hill paths across all elevations.

Field photography tip: Macro underside views showing hindwing sub-basal spots and tornal orange eyespots are essential. Sharp macro shots of the hindwing underside bands and spot alignments are usually far more diagnostic than upperside blue.

தமிழ் அறிமுகம் & அடையாள சுருக்கம்:

Grass Jewel (Freyeria trochylus) என்பது நீலன்கள் (புல் நீலன்கள் மற்றும் விகடன்கள்) குடும்பத்தைச் சேர்ந்த தமிழ்நாடு பட்டாம்பூச்சியாகும்.

விரிவான இறக்கை அடையாளக் குறிப்புகள் ஆங்கிலத்தில்:

Localized miniature jewel carrying bright orange-crowned hindwing spots

Identification marks

Upperwing: Tailless; both sexes are dark smoky brown lacking blue scaling, with the hindwing submarginal black spots distinctly capped inwardly by warm orange-ochreous crescents (brightest in females and fresh individuals).

Underwing: Pale brownish-grey marked with white-ringed dark brown basal and discal spots, and a hindwing marginal series of metallic green-speckled black spots crowned conspicuously on the inner side by vivid orange lunules.

Lookalikes: Closely mirrors Small Grass Jewel (Freyeria putli) in size and ground pattern, but distinguished by prominent bright orange inner crowns bordering the metallic-flecked black hindwing spots on both surfaces.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Semi-arid stony scrublands, dry grassy fallows, and rainshadow foothills.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Scarce and localized in drier western and southern parts of Tamil Nadu including Coimbatore, Erode, Tiruppur, Madurai, and dry Western Ghats rainshadow foothills.

Seasonality: Patchy and localized, recorded mainly in dry and post-monsoon seasons from October to April.

Flight & behaviour

Hovers slowly inches above sun-baked ground and tiny prostrate herbs, basking briefly with half-parted wings during calm mornings.

Field note: Long lumped under Freyeria putli in regional checklists; larvae secrete sweet exudates attracting tending ants on low dry-zone herbs.
