Field guide notes for Papilionidae · Papilioninae (True Swallowtails & Birdwings)

Family overview (Papilionidae, Swallowtails): Large to very large, strong-flying butterflies including birdwings, roses, mimes, Mormons, jays, swordtails, and banded peacocks. Many species carry prominent hindwing tails and feed actively while fluttering their wings at flowers.

Subfamily traits (Papilioninae): Includes Graphium jays and swordtails, Pachliopta roses, Papilio swallowtails, and the Southern Birdwing (Troides minos).

Typical habitats: Forest edges, hill streams, gardens, scrub, and evergreen shola margins across Tamil Nadu.

Field photography tip: Note hindwing tail shape, body colour (red vs black), and submarginal spot patterns. Both upperwing (dorsal) and underwing (ventral) photographs help separate mimics, mimes, and closely related dark Papilio species.

தமிழ் அறிமுகம் & அடையாள சுருக்கம்:

இதன் தமிழ்ப் பெயர் கத்திவால் அழகி. Spot Swordtail (Graphium nomius) என்பது அழகிகள் (அழகிகள் மற்றும் பறவையிறகிகள்) குடும்பத்தைச் சேர்ந்த தமிழ்நாடு பட்டாம்பூச்சியாகும். iNaturalist தமிழ்நாடு திட்டத்தில் இதுவரை 154 பதிவுகள் பதிவாகியுள்ளன.

விரிவான இறக்கை அடையாளக் குறிப்புகள் ஆங்கிலத்தில்:

Deciduous-woodland swallowtail striped in black and powdery white

Identification marks

Upperwing: Crisp white to very pale bluish-white crossed by five broad jet-black transverse bands on the forewing, a dark outer border bearing a row of white spots, and a long slender black tail.

Underwing: Warm bronzy-brown markings replacing parts of the black bands, accented by a neat row of small red spots nestled between black bars on the hindwing.

Lookalikes: Lacks the Five-bar Swordtail's greenish-yellow hindwing underside and instead shows distinct bronzy-brown bands enclosing red hindwing spots.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Dry and moist deciduous forests, scrub-clad foothills, seasonal stream beds, and wooded campus edges.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Recorded across Eastern Ghats (Kalvarayan, Shervarayan, Pachamalai), Kallar foothills, Mudumalai, and foothills bordering Coimbatore and Madurai.

Seasonality: Strong seasonal peak from February through June following dry-season heat and early showers.

Flight & behaviour

Emerges synchronously after first rains, fluttering energetically over moist sandbanks and nectar-rich Lantana or Albizia blossoms.

Field note: Caterpillars feed primarily on deciduous Annonaceae trees like Miliusa tomentosa, causing brief spring abundance bursts across foothill scrubs.
