Field guide notes for Nymphalidae · Charaxinae (Rajahs & Nawabs)

Family overview (Nymphalidae, Brush-footed butterflies): Tamil Nadu’s largest butterfly family (97 checklist species), encompassing tigers, crows, pansies, eggflies, sailers, barons, rajahs, browns, and Tamil Nadu’s state butterfly, the Tamil Yeoman.

Subfamily traits (Charaxinae): Stout-bodied, exceptionally fast-flying canopy butterflies often attracted to tree sap, rotting fruit, and damp sand.

Typical habitats: Deciduous and evergreen hill forests, foothills, and wooded estates.

Field photography tip: Underside pale bands and tail length separate Common, Anomalous, and Blue Nawabs. Record both basking upperside and resting underside views, especially for bushbrowns, treebrowns, sailers, and oakleaf butterflies.

Twin-tailed creamy-banded nawab diagnosed by two pale spots near the forewing tip

Identification marks

Upperwing: Velvety black-brown wings bisected by a moderately broad pale greenish-cream median band and sporting two sharp hindwing tails; forewing carries two distinct pale subapical spots near the costa (a larger inner spot and smaller outer dot). Females are larger with a slightly wider pale band.

Underwing: Satiny cream-white framed by warm olive-brown outer borders, fine dark hairlines, rust-red discal spots along the band edge, and twin pale spots mirroring the forewing apex.

Lookalikes: Closely resembles Indian Nawab (Charaxes bharata), but separated reliably by having two pale costal/subapical spots on the forewing rather than a single prominent spot.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Dry and moist deciduous foothills, scrub jungles, stony riverbeds, and open wooded clearings.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Widespread across Western and Eastern Ghats foothills and dry hills including Kallar, Coimbatore, Sathyamangalam, Mudumalai, Salem, Yercaud, Pachamalai, and Madurai.

Seasonality: Active year-round in suitable dry-to-moist tracts, with peaks in June-August and October-January.

Flight & behaviour

Powerful, bullet-fast flier that perches high on thorny canopies and drops rapidly to damp sand, animal scat, and tree sap.

Field note: Formerly placed in genus Polyura; prefers slightly drier foothill and scrub landscapes alongside the widespread Indian Nawab.
