Field guide notes for Lycaenidae · Polyommatinae (Grass Blues, Pierrots, Lineblues & Ceruleans)

Family overview (Lycaenidae, Blues and hairstreaks): Small, jewel-like butterflies (96 checklist species) including grass blues, pierrots, lineblues, ceruleans, silverlines, oakblues, royals, flashes, and sunbeams.

Subfamily traits (Polyommatinae): Delicate small blues with distinctive underside spot chains, zebra stripes, or white-and-black pied markings.

Typical habitats: Lawns, grasslands, damp sand, woodland edges, and hill paths across all elevations.

Field photography tip: Macro underside views showing hindwing sub-basal spots and tornal orange eyespots are essential. Sharp macro shots of the hindwing underside bands and spot alignments are usually far more diagnostic than upperside blue.

Lawnside grass blue lacking forewing cell spots and shifting spot 6 outward

Identification marks

Upperwing: Tailless; males are dull violet-blue bordered broadly in dark brown along the outer third of both wings, while females are plain dark brown with sparse dull blue scales restricted near the base.

Underwing: Pale buff-grey with faint blurry submarginal lunules, completely lacking dark spots inside the forewing cell and space 11, and showing the hindwing discal spot in space 6 shifted clearly outward out of line with spots 5 and 7.

Lookalikes: Separated from Dark Grass Blue (Zizeeria karsandra) by lacking a spot inside the forewing cell, from Pale Grass Blue (Pseudozizeeria maha) by smaller size and outward-shifted hindwing spot 6, and from Tiny Grass Blue (Zizula hylax) by lacking two forewing costal spots.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Short lawns, roadside verges, paddy bunds, coastal grasslands, and disturbed scrub.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Common across every district of Tamil Nadu from coastal Chennai lawns to Nilgiri and Palani foothill pastures.

Seasonality: Abundant year-round statewide.

Flight & behaviour

Weak, hovering flight inches above turf; settles frequently on prostrate clovers and Tridax blooms with wings half-open.

Field note: Indian subspecies Zizina otis decreta breeds steadily on creeping Fabaceae weeds in urban parks and agricultural bunds.
