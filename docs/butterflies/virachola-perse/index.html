Field guide notes for Lycaenidae · Theclinae (Silverlines, Oakblues, Royals, Flashes & Yamfly)

Family overview (Lycaenidae, Blues and hairstreaks): Small, jewel-like butterflies (96 checklist species) including grass blues, pierrots, lineblues, ceruleans, silverlines, oakblues, royals, flashes, and sunbeams.

Subfamily traits (Theclinae): Often tailed hairstreaks with metallic silver bars (Cigaritis), deep violet-blue upperwings (Arhopala), or swift red-and-slate flashes (Rapala).

Typical habitats: Scrub jungles, forest canopies, mistletoe-bearing trees, and rocky hilltops.

Field photography tip: Capture the hindwing tornal lobes, tail count, and arrangement of underside bands. Sharp macro shots of the hindwing underside bands and spot alignments are usually far more diagnostic than upperside blue.

Bold-spotted forest fruit-borer flashing bright sky-blue on both sexes

Identification marks

Upperwing: Males are shining bright sky-blue over the inner wings inside broad black costal and outer margins (sometimes tinged faintly orange beyond the cell); females are duller blue with a whitish patch beyond the forewing cell and dark submarginal hindwing spots.

Underwing: Vinous grey-brown overlaid with a lilac sheen in fresh males, marked with dark pale-edged cell-end twin bars, broad chain-like discal spots, a diagnostic round black subbasal spot near the hindwing costa, orange-ringed tornal ocelli with metallic blue scales, and a slender white-tipped tail.

Lookalikes: Larger and brighter sky-blue above than Guava Blue (Virachola isocrates), cleanly separated beneath by the bold black spot near the upper base of the hindwing and broader linked discal spots.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Deciduous and semi-evergreen hill forests, foothill scrub woodlands, and wooded ridges where wild Catunaregam grows.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Occurs across Western Ghats foothills (Kallar, Mudumalai, Sathyamangalam, Anamalais, KMTR) and Eastern Ghats ranges including Sirumalai, Pachamalai, and Shervaroys.

Seasonality: Recorded across most months, peaking between October and March.

Flight & behaviour

Powerful, fast flyer that hill-tops on sunlit ridges during late morning and perches high on deciduous tree canopies.

Field note: Like Virachola isocrates, larvae hollow out tough Catunaregam (Randia) fruits internally and secure the stem to the branch with silk before pupating inside the dried shell.

Caterpillar host plants

Catunaregam spinosa (Randia dumetorum)Punica granatum
