Field guide notes for Nymphalidae · Biblidinae (Castors & Joker)

Family overview (Nymphalidae, Brush-footed butterflies): Tamil Nadu’s largest butterfly family (97 checklist species), encompassing tigers, crows, pansies, eggflies, sailers, barons, rajahs, browns, and Tamil Nadu’s state butterfly, the Tamil Yeoman.

Subfamily traits (Biblidinae): Warm brown or orange-black butterflies with wavy transverse bands and slightly scalloped wing margins.

Typical habitats: Open scrub, gardens, castor stands, and dry deciduous foothills.

Field photography tip: Check wing margin contour (angled vs rounded) and hindwing sub-basal wavy lines. Record both basking upperside and resting underside views, especially for bushbrowns, treebrowns, sailers, and oakleaf butterflies.

Tawny-russet castor with sharply angled margins and single wavy lines

Identification marks

Upperwing: Warm tawny-russet crossed by fine, widely spaced single dark brown wavy lines, a tiny white subapical forewing spot, and distinctly truncated, sharply angled outer margins; males carry a glossy dark scent-patch underneath the forewing overlap.

Underwing: Deep chestnut-brown to mottled purplish-rufous (darker in dry-season morphs) crossed by darker brown bands and fine pale outer lines.

Lookalikes: Separated reliably from Common Castor (Ariadne merione) by the acutely angled, concave forewing outer margin and single, finer dark discal lines across the upperwing (versus rounded margins and paired wavy lines in A. merione).

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Scrub jungles, dry deciduous foothills, village hedges, neglected gardens, and forest clearings where stinging nettle-vines grow.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Widespread across Tamil Nadu plains, coastal scrub, Coimbatore, Chennai greenways, Eastern Ghats, and Western Ghats foothills.

Seasonality: Present throughout the year statewide, peaking during post-monsoon months (October to January).

Flight & behaviour

Flies slowly with jerky, gliding wingbeats close to low shrubs, basking with wings held flat and frequently circling patches of stinging Tragia vines.

Field note: Represented in peninsular India by Ariadne ariadne indica; the deeply angular wing silhouette makes it look like a crinkled dry leaf when settled on twigs.
