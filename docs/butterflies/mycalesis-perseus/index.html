Field guide notes for Nymphalidae · Satyrinae (Browns, Bushbrowns, Treebrowns & Rings)

Family overview (Nymphalidae, Brush-footed butterflies): Tamil Nadu’s largest butterfly family (97 checklist species), encompassing tigers, crows, pansies, eggflies, sailers, barons, rajahs, browns, and Tamil Nadu’s state butterfly, the Tamil Yeoman.

Subfamily traits (Satyrinae): Shade-loving brown butterflies marked with concentric underside eyespots (ocelli) that frequently exhibit wet- and dry-season forms.

Typical habitats: Grasslands, forest understorey, bamboo groves, palm groves, and shaded gardens.

Field photography tip: Underside ocelli sizes, brand marks, and discal line shape (straight vs curved) are crucial for species ID. Record both basking upperside and resting underside views, especially for bushbrowns, treebrowns, sailers, and oakleaf butterflies.

Widespread grass-feeding bushbrown with an inward-shifted hindwing eyespot beneath

Identification marks

Upperwing: Dull dark grey-brown with faint pale marginal lines and a single prominent white-pupilled lower forewing ocellus in space 2 (larger in females); males carry a small dark forewing brand and a pale yellow hindwing hair-tuft.

Underwing: Wet-season morph shows a straight pale violet-white discal line and ringed ocelli (2-4 on forewing, 7 on hindwing) where the hindwing ocellus in space 2 sits distinctly shifted inward out of line with its neighbors; dry-season morph turns mottled bark-brown with ocelli shrunk to white specks.

Lookalikes: Separated in the wet season from Dark-brand Bushbrown (Mycalesis mineus) by the inward-displaced hindwing ocellus in space 2 (leaving only the three lowest ocelli aligned, versus four aligned in M. mineus).

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Grassy meadows, paddy bunds, scrub edges, urban parks, and Ghats foothills.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Statewide across plains, wetlands, farms, and Western and Eastern Ghats foothills.

Seasonality: Abundant year-round across Tamil Nadu; wet-season morphs fly July-December and dry-season forms dominate January-May.

Flight & behaviour

Bobs low through tall grass, basking diagonally in morning sun and resting sideways among dry grass stalks during midday heat.

Field note: Dry-season adults are nearly impossible to separate by wing pattern alone from dry-season M. mineus without examining male scent brands.
