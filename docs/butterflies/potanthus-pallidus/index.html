Field guide notes for Hesperiidae · Hesperiinae (Grass Skippers, Swifts, Aces & Darts)

Family overview (Hesperiidae, Skippers): Compact, fast-darting butterflies (83 checklist species) such as awls, flats, angles, aces, bobs, demons, swifts, and grass darts.

Subfamily traits (Hesperiinae): Fast-flying tawny, brown, or black-and-yellow skippers that typically hold forewings and hindwings at different angles when basking.

Typical habitats: Grasslands, paddy margins, palms, bamboo thickets, and forest undergrowth.

Field photography tip: Zoom into the underside hindwing spot pattern and forewing cell spots to distinguish swifts and darts. Photograph both the upperwing hyaline spots and the underside hindwing spot arcs, essential for distinguishing swifts and aces.

Scarce hill dart with pale yellowish bands split sharply by dark veins

Identification marks

Upperwing: Dark blackish-brown patterned with distinctly paler, dull yellowish-orange bands sharply segmented into blocks by dark-dusted veins; forewing spots in spaces 4 and 5 sit widely detached from spots 2-3 below and subapical spots 6-8 above; hindwing discal band narrow and vein-divided.

Underwing: Dull pale greenish-ochreous across the hindwing, with the narrow pale yellow discal band conspicuously cut by darkened veins and bordered by fine blackish spots.

Lookalikes: Separated from Indian Dart (Potanthus pseudomaesa), Palni Dart (Potanthus palnia), and Chinese Dart (Potanthus confucius) by paler yellowish-cream markings, strongly dark-dusted veins bisecting the bands, and widely detached forewing spots in spaces 4 and 5.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Higher-elevation grassy slopes, montane forest clearings, bamboo-fringed streams, and wooded hill paths.

Tamil Nadu distribution: High-elevation Western Ghats of Tamil Nadu including Nilgiris, Palani Hills, and Anamalais.

Seasonality: Scarce and seasonal, recorded chiefly during post-monsoon and winter months from September to February.

Flight & behaviour

Brisk, low flier favoring sunny gaps inside hill forests; suns on exposed blades in two-angle jet-plane stance and visits wild hillside blooms and damp gravel.

Field note: Much less frequent in South India than Potanthus pseudomaesa, best spotted by its pale straw-tinted bands and widely detached forewing spots 4 and 5.
