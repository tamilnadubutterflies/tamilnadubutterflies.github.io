Field guide notes for Nymphalidae · Charaxinae (Rajahs & Nawabs)

Family overview (Nymphalidae, Brush-footed butterflies): Tamil Nadu’s largest butterfly family (97 checklist species), encompassing tigers, crows, pansies, eggflies, sailers, barons, rajahs, browns, and Tamil Nadu’s state butterfly, the Tamil Yeoman.

Subfamily traits (Charaxinae): Stout-bodied, exceptionally fast-flying canopy butterflies often attracted to tree sap, rotting fruit, and damp sand.

Typical habitats: Deciduous and evergreen hill forests, foothills, and wooded estates.

Field photography tip: Underside pale bands and tail length separate Common, Anomalous, and Blue Nawabs. Record both basking upperside and resting underside views, especially for bushbrowns, treebrowns, sailers, and oakleaf butterflies.

Fast-flying black-and-lime nawab adorned with twin hindwing tails

Identification marks

Upperwing: Velvety black wings bisected by a wide pale creamy-green median band and sporting two sharp hindwing tails.

Underwing: Pale satiny cream bordered by warm olive-brown margins and dark spots.

Lookalikes: Previously known as Polyura athamas; separated from Anomalous Nawab (Charaxes agrarius) by subtly narrower median band and habitat overlap.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Forest streams, damp gravel roads, foothills, and wooded clearings.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Western and Eastern Ghats including Kallar, Siruvani, Mudumalai, Pachamalai, and Salem hills.

Seasonality: Common year-round in hills and wooded districts.

Flight & behaviour

Exceptionally fast flight; males puddle intently on wet sand, animal dung, and tree sap.

Field note: Highlighted in TNBS field photography from moist Tamil Nadu hill tracts.
