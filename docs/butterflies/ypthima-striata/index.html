Field guide notes for Nymphalidae · Satyrinae (Browns, Bushbrowns, Treebrowns & Rings)

Family overview (Nymphalidae, Brush-footed butterflies): Tamil Nadu’s largest butterfly family (97 checklist species), encompassing tigers, crows, pansies, eggflies, sailers, barons, rajahs, browns, and Tamil Nadu’s state butterfly, the Tamil Yeoman.

Subfamily traits (Satyrinae): Shade-loving brown butterflies marked with concentric underside eyespots (ocelli) that frequently exhibit wet- and dry-season forms.

Typical habitats: Grasslands, forest understorey, bamboo groves, palm groves, and shaded gardens.

Field photography tip: Underside ocelli sizes, brand marks, and discal line shape (straight vs curved) are crucial for species ID. Record both basking upperside and resting underside views, especially for bushbrowns, treebrowns, sailers, and oakleaf butterflies.

Endemic montane ringlet marked beneath by sharp dark striations and banded hindwing eyespots

Identification marks

Upperwing: Dark brown on both wings; forewing displays a distinct bright yellow-ringed bi-pupilled subapical ocellus and hindwing carries 2-3 small yellow-ringed ocelli. Males have an indistinct scent patch on the forewing disc.

Underwing: Stark white boldly etched with coarse dark brown striae and three prominent brown transverse fasciae (subbasal, discal, subterminal); hindwing displays a double apical ocellus on a short dark bar and three linear postdiscal-tornal ocelli (the innermost bi-pupilled) seated neatly atop a shared brown band.

Lookalikes: Distinguished from Small Jewel Four-ring (Ypthima singala) by coarser dark striations and distinct brown bands running directly across the hindwing eyespots, and from Nilgiri Four-ring (Ypthima chenu) by smaller size and rounded linear tornal ocelli.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Mid-to-high elevation Western Ghats grasslands, grassy hill slopes, and open forest borders.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Western Ghats hill ranges of Tamil Nadu including Nilgiris, Anamalais, Palani Hills, and Agasthyamalai-Kalakkad-Mundanthurai.

Seasonality: Localized and seasonal, recorded primarily from August to December and March-May.

Flight & behaviour

Hops briskly low over open hillside grass in bright sun, pausing briefly on dry stalks and rocks with closed striated undersides.

Field note: Endemic to the southern Western Ghats and originally described from the Nilgiris; also called the Striated Five-ring in classical Indian literature.

Caterpillar host plants

Montane and hill-slope Poaceae grasses
