Field guide notes for Lycaenidae · Polyommatinae (Grass Blues, Pierrots, Lineblues & Ceruleans)

Family overview (Lycaenidae, Blues and hairstreaks): Small, jewel-like butterflies (96 checklist species) including grass blues, pierrots, lineblues, ceruleans, silverlines, oakblues, royals, flashes, and sunbeams.

Subfamily traits (Polyommatinae): Delicate small blues with distinctive underside spot chains, zebra stripes, or white-and-black pied markings.

Typical habitats: Lawns, grasslands, damp sand, woodland edges, and hill paths across all elevations.

Field photography tip: Macro underside views showing hindwing sub-basal spots and tornal orange eyespots are essential. Sharp macro shots of the hindwing underside bands and spot alignments are usually far more diagnostic than upperside blue.

Tiger-striped garden blue breeding on Plumbago shrubs statewide

Identification marks

Upperwing: Males are rich violet-blue with a hairline black margin and subtle dark spots showing near the hindwing tornus; females are dark brown with bright blue basal scales and checkered white-and-brown central patches.

Underwing: Creamy white tightly rippled from base to outer margin with crisp brown zebra-like bands, twin jet-black hindwing tornal eyespots flecked with metallic green and edged in pale orange, and a short thread-like tail.

Lookalikes: Separated from Pea Blue (Lampides boeticus) by continuous bold brown-and-white zebra stripes covering the entire underside rather than a broad pale white submarginal hindwing band.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Home gardens, urban hedges, scrublands, riparian weeds, and hill station roadsides.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Found throughout every district of Tamil Nadu from coastal Chennai parks to Nilgiri and Palani hill gardens.

Seasonality: Abundant year-round across Tamil Nadu.

Flight & behaviour

Quick, fluttering flight right around flowering leadwort bushes; mud-puddles frequently and rotates hindwings gently while perched to draw attention to tornal false-head eyespots.

Field note: Caterpillars consume Plumbago flower buds and young pods and are regularly tended by Camponotus and Crematogaster ants.
