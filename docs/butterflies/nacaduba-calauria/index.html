Field guide notes for Lycaenidae · Polyommatinae (Grass Blues, Pierrots, Lineblues & Ceruleans)

Family overview (Lycaenidae, Blues and hairstreaks): Small, jewel-like butterflies (96 checklist species) including grass blues, pierrots, lineblues, ceruleans, silverlines, oakblues, royals, flashes, and sunbeams.

Subfamily traits (Polyommatinae): Delicate small blues with distinctive underside spot chains, zebra stripes, or white-and-black pied markings.

Typical habitats: Lawns, grasslands, damp sand, woodland edges, and hill paths across all elevations.

Field photography tip: Macro underside views showing hindwing sub-basal spots and tornal orange eyespots are essential. Sharp macro shots of the hindwing underside bands and spot alignments are usually far more diagnostic than upperside blue.

High-contrast dark six-lineblue of southern evergreen rainforests

Identification marks

Upperwing: Males are deep dark violet-purple with narrow dark borders and dense opaque scales; females are dark brown with bright blue restricted to the forewing base and disc and crisp black submarginal hindwing lunules.

Underwing: Deep chocolate-brown crossed by crisp stark white six-lineblue striae (including forewing cell sub-basal lines) with strong dark internal band lines, a straight forewing postdiscal band, sharply pointed submarginal spots in spaces 4 and 5, an orange-capped black spot in space 2, and a delicate tail.

Lookalikes: Darker beneath with sharper white bands and sagittate submarginal spots in spaces 4 and 5 than Rounded 6-lineblue (Nacaduba berenice), and much darker and higher contrast than Nacaduba kurava.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Undisturbed wet evergreen rainforests, shaded streamsides, and mid-elevation hill valleys.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Uncommon specialist of southern and central Western Ghats in Tamil Nadu, recorded from Kanyakumari, Kalakkad-Mundanthurai, Anamalai, Siruvani, and Nilgiris.

Seasonality: Recorded intermittently between May and January in heavy-rainfall tracts.

Flight & behaviour

Swift understory flier; males visit wet gravel bars near forest streams while females remain higher among canopy vines.

Field note: Indian and Sri Lankan populations correspond to subspecies Nacaduba calauria evansi; its larval ecology in the Western Ghats remains an active field gap.

Caterpillar host plants

Forest Sapindaceae and Fabaceae climbers (confirmed Indian larval host plant awaiting documentation)
