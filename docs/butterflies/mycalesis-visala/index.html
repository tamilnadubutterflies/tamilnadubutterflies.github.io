Field guide notes for Nymphalidae · Satyrinae (Browns, Bushbrowns, Treebrowns & Rings)

Family overview (Nymphalidae, Brush-footed butterflies): Tamil Nadu’s largest butterfly family (97 checklist species), encompassing tigers, crows, pansies, eggflies, sailers, barons, rajahs, browns, and Tamil Nadu’s state butterfly, the Tamil Yeoman.

Subfamily traits (Satyrinae): Shade-loving brown butterflies marked with concentric underside eyespots (ocelli) that frequently exhibit wet- and dry-season forms.

Typical habitats: Grasslands, forest understorey, bamboo groves, palm groves, and shaded gardens.

Field photography tip: Underside ocelli sizes, brand marks, and discal line shape (straight vs curved) are crucial for species ID. Record both basking upperside and resting underside views, especially for bushbrowns, treebrowns, sailers, and oakleaf butterflies.

Identification marks

Upperwing: Slightly produced, angular forewing tip; uniform dark brown with one large yellow-ringed ocellus in space 2 (rarely a minute apical dot); males possess a pale hair-tuft overlapping a dark brand near the hindwing costa.

Underwing: Wet-season morph is pale brown crossed by a narrow whitish discal line and ringed submarginal ocelli (2 on forewing, 7 on hindwing), with males displaying a very long, bright ochreous-yellow brand reaching or crossing the discal line; dry-season morph turns sharply angular and striated like a dry leaf.

Lookalikes: Separated from Dark-brand Bushbrown (Mycalesis mineus) by its sharply angled forewing apex and very long bright ochre male forewing brand, and from Tamil Bushbrown (Mycalesis subdita) by its more acute forewing tip and narrower ochre brand.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Moist and dry deciduous hill forests, bamboo-shaded foothills, and grassy stream edges.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Western and Eastern Ghats hills and foothills including Mudumalai, Siruvani, Kallar, Anamalais, Shevaroys, Kolli Hills, and Kalakkad-Mundanthurai.

Seasonality: Seen throughout the year in hills and wooded foothills, with angular dry-season morphs common December-May.

Flight & behaviour

Skulks in shaded bamboo and grass undergrowth, flying short bouncing hops and feeding on ripe fallen fruit and damp earth.

Field note: Dry-season adults show an exaggerated falcate forewing profile and intricate leaf striations that render them nearly invisible on dead foliage.
