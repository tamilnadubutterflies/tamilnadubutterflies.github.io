Field guide notes for Nymphalidae · Danainae (Tigers, Crows & Tree Nymphs)

Family overview (Nymphalidae, Brush-footed butterflies): Tamil Nadu’s largest butterfly family (97 checklist species), encompassing tigers, crows, pansies, eggflies, sailers, barons, rajahs, browns, and Tamil Nadu’s state butterfly, the Tamil Yeoman.

Subfamily traits (Danainae): Tough, unpalatable butterflies whose caterpillars feed on milkweeds and dogbanes; central to Tamil Nadu’s seasonal Eastern–Western Ghats migrations.

Typical habitats: Hills, plains, urban parks, and forest corridors throughout Tamil Nadu.

Field photography tip: Count subapical spots and pale streaks in the forewing cell to separate Blue Tiger vs Dark Blue Tiger and the Crow species. Record both basking upperside and resting underside views, especially for bushbrowns, treebrowns, sailers, and oakleaf butterflies.

Identification marks

Upperwing: Peninsular subspecies E. k. kollari is rich dark olive-brown paling toward the outer margins, bordered by crisp white marginal dots and larger elongated white submarginal spots on the hindwing without metallic blue gloss; males carry a single short, broad velvety scent brand on the forewing.

Underwing: Uniform paler olive-brown displaying the twin marginal and submarginal white spot rows plus 2-3 tiny bluish-white discal dots near the forewing cell.

Lookalikes: Separated from Common Crow (Euploea core) by its shorter, wider forewing male scent brand and elongated tornal hindwing streaks, and from Double-branded Crow (Euploea sylvester) by lacking deep blue sheen and having only one forewing brand.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Plains scrublands, urban avenues, riverine groves, agricultural hedges, and dry deciduous foothills.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Plains and foothills statewide including Chennai, Chengalpattu, Coimbatore, Erode, Madurai, Salem, Tiruchirappalli, and Eastern Ghats margins.

Seasonality: Recorded throughout the year across Tamil Nadu, peaking during September-December monsoon and post-monsoon months.

Flight & behaviour

Sails unhurriedly above shrubs and garden trees, gathering on drying Crotalaria and Heliotropium stems to harvest alkaloids and occasionally joining Danainae migrations.

Field note: Historically treated as a separate species, the peninsular Indian Brown King Crow (subspecies kollari) lacks the iridescent blue sheen typical of northeastern E. k. klugii.
