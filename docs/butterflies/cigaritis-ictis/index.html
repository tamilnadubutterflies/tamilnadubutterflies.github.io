Field guide notes for Lycaenidae · Theclinae (Silverlines, Oakblues, Royals, Flashes & Yamfly)

Family overview (Lycaenidae, Blues and hairstreaks): Small, jewel-like butterflies (96 checklist species) including grass blues, pierrots, lineblues, ceruleans, silverlines, oakblues, royals, flashes, and sunbeams.

Subfamily traits (Theclinae): Often tailed hairstreaks with metallic silver bars (Cigaritis), deep violet-blue upperwings (Arhopala), or swift red-and-slate flashes (Rapala).

Typical habitats: Scrub jungles, forest canopies, mistletoe-bearing trees, and rocky hilltops.

Field photography tip: Capture the hindwing tornal lobes, tail count, and arrangement of underside bands. Sharp macro shots of the hindwing underside bands and spot alignments are usually far more diagnostic than upperside blue.

Sun-loving dry-zone silverline flashing an angled orange forewing patch

Identification marks

Upperwing: Dark brown glossed with purple-blue at wing bases in males, featuring a prominent roughly triangular bright orange discal patch on the upper forewing enclosing two short dark bars; females show a broader, brighter orange patch and lack the purplish gloss.

Underwing: Pale ochreous-yellow to greyish-buff crossed by pale tawny-brown bands lined with narrow metallic silver and fine dark edges; on the forewing a subbasal band reaches below the median vein and the hindwing carries two white-tipped black tails at a dull orange anal lobe.

Lookalikes: Readily distinguished from Common Silverline (Cigaritis vulcanus) by the broad triangular orange forewing patch instead of discrete transverse bars, and separated from Scarce Shot Silverline (Cigaritis elima) by having a forewing subbasal spot below the cell and duller orange-brown ventral bands.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Dry deciduous scrub, thorny plains, rocky hillocks, open grasslands, and dry eastern foothills.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Widespread across dry interior Tamil Nadu and foothills, including Coimbatore, Sathyamangalam, Madurai, Virudhunagar, Tirunelveli, Pachamalai, and Kallar.

Seasonality: Flies throughout the year in dry plains and foothills, peaking during September to March.

Flight & behaviour

Extremely fast, erratic flight low over rocky ground and thorny bushes; males aggressively defend sunlit gravel perches and damp patches.

Field note: Exhibits marked seasonal variation between darker wet-season and paler dry-season broods; caterpillars associate closely with Crematogaster ants inside stem shelters.
