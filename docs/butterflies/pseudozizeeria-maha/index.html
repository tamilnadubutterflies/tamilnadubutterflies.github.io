Field guide notes for Lycaenidae · Polyommatinae (Grass Blues, Pierrots, Lineblues & Ceruleans)

Family overview (Lycaenidae, Blues and hairstreaks): Small, jewel-like butterflies (96 checklist species) including grass blues, pierrots, lineblues, ceruleans, silverlines, oakblues, royals, flashes, and sunbeams.

Subfamily traits (Polyommatinae): Delicate small blues with distinctive underside spot chains, zebra stripes, or white-and-black pied markings.

Typical habitats: Lawns, grasslands, damp sand, woodland edges, and hill paths across all elevations.

Field photography tip: Macro underside views showing hindwing sub-basal spots and tornal orange eyespots are essential. Sharp macro shots of the hindwing underside bands and spot alignments are usually far more diagnostic than upperside blue.

Largest Tamil Nadu grass blue tied closely to creeping woodsorrel

Identification marks

Upperwing: Tailless; males are pale silvery sky-blue (deeper purplish-blue in wet-season forms) with wide dusky brown margins, while females are dark brown with variable pale blue basal suffusion that expands widely in dry-season broods.

Underwing: Pale chalky grey dotted with sharp black spots: lacks a spot inside the forewing cell, shows the uppermost forewing postdiscal spot (space 6) shifted abruptly inward, and bears a small black basal dot in hindwing space 7.

Lookalikes: Visibly larger with a chalky grey underside and inward-shifted top forewing discal spot compared to Lesser Grass Blue (Zizina otis) and Dark Grass Blue (Zizeeria karsandra).

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Urban lawns, botanical gardens, moist fallow meadows, tea and coffee estate borders, and hill grasslands.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Statewide across Tamil Nadu plains, cities, and high hills including Chennai, Coimbatore, Madurai, Salem, Nilgiris, Kodaikanal, Valparai, and Yercaud.

Seasonality: Abundant year-round across Tamil Nadu, particularly conspicuous in hill stations and post-monsoon plains.

Flight & behaviour

Flutters weakly just above shaded and sunny ground cover, basking flat on Oxalis mats and sipping nectar from tiny composite weeds.

Field note: Subspecies Pseudozizeeria maha ossa shows pronounced seasonal dimorphism, with cool dry-season females gaining bright pale blue wings resembling males.
