Field guide notes for Nymphalidae · Nymphalinae (Pansies, Eggflies, Admirals & Oakleaf)

Family overview (Nymphalidae, Brush-footed butterflies): Tamil Nadu’s largest butterfly family (97 checklist species), encompassing tigers, crows, pansies, eggflies, sailers, barons, rajahs, browns, and Tamil Nadu’s state butterfly, the Tamil Yeoman.

Subfamily traits (Nymphalinae): Familiar, colourful butterflies featuring conspicuous eyespots, seasonal morphs, and dramatic dead-leaf underside camouflage.

Typical habitats: Gardens, wetlands, agricultural bunds, scrublands, and hill forests.

Field photography tip: In Junonia pansies and Hypolimnas eggflies, both dorsal colour patches and ventral patterns aid rapid ID. Record both basking upperside and resting underside views, especially for bushbrowns, treebrowns, sailers, and oakleaf butterflies.

Large eggfly of wooded gardens and forests with iridescent egg spots

Identification marks

Upperwing: Males are velvety black with glowing violet-rimmed white oval spots on all wings; females vary from dark Common Crow mimics to orange-patched morphs.

Underwing: Dark brown crossed by a crisp pale scalloped submarginal chain.

Lookalikes: Larger than Danaid Eggfly and bears white oval patches on all four wings in males (including small subapical forewing spots).

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Wooded parks, forest edges, gardens, and foothills.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Common across plains and hill forests throughout Tamil Nadu.

Seasonality: Year-round, peaking during monsoon and winter months.

Flight & behaviour

Males perch conspicuously high on sunlit shrubs and swoop after intruders.

Field note: Females exhibit remarkable regional polymorphism across India, unlike uniform white-patched iridescent males.
