Field guide notes for Nymphalidae · Limenitidinae (Sailers, Sergeants, Lascars, Barons & Clipper)

Family overview (Nymphalidae, Brush-footed butterflies): Tamil Nadu’s largest butterfly family (97 checklist species), encompassing tigers, crows, pansies, eggflies, sailers, barons, rajahs, browns, and Tamil Nadu’s state butterfly, the Tamil Yeoman.

Subfamily traits (Limenitidinae): Gliding butterflies with characteristic black-and-white banded wings (Neptis, Athyma) or emerald-olive camouflaged upperwings (Euthalia, Dophla).

Typical habitats: Forest trails, stream banks, orchards, and wooded gardens.

Field photography tip: Check whether the forewing cell streak is broken into spots or continuous, and compare underside ground colour. Record both basking upperside and resting underside views, especially for bushbrowns, treebrowns, sailers, and oakleaf butterflies.

Large mossy-green forest duke bearing crimson cell and basal wing spots

Identification marks

Upperwing: Massive dark metallic olive-green butterfly etched with sinuous black cell loops enclosing a single vivid crimson spot inside the forewing cell; females are larger and paler green with a broad greyish-white forewing costal patch.

Underwing: Muted sap-green densely dusted in leaden-grey, carrying the crimson forewing cell spot and three vivid black-ringed crimson spots near the base of the hindwing.

Lookalikes: Far larger than Common Baron (Euthalia aconthea) and Grey Count (Tanaecia lepidea), and uniquely identified by the single red forewing cell spot above and three black-ringed crimson basal spots on the hindwing underside.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Shaded wet evergreen and moist semi-evergreen hill forests, wooded ravines, and rubber or cardamom estate edges.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Restricted to wet Western Ghats tracts in Tamil Nadu including Siruvani, Kovai Kutralam, Anamalai, Valparai, Kalakkad-Mundanthurai, and Kanyakumari.

Seasonality: Seen year-round in dense wet hill forests, peaking between September and February.

Flight & behaviour

Powerful, fast flier keeping close to shaded understory tracks and low tree trunks; strongly attracted to rotting forest fruits, fallen jackfruit, and toddy-palm sap.

Field note: South Indian race Dophla evelina laudabilis; despite its large wingspan, its mossy green upperwings blend rapidly into dappled jungle shade once settled.
