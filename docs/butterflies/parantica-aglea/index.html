Field guide notes for Nymphalidae · Danainae (Tigers, Crows & Tree Nymphs)

Family overview (Nymphalidae, Brush-footed butterflies): Tamil Nadu’s largest butterfly family (97 checklist species), encompassing tigers, crows, pansies, eggflies, sailers, barons, rajahs, browns, and Tamil Nadu’s state butterfly, the Tamil Yeoman.

Subfamily traits (Danainae): Tough, unpalatable butterflies whose caterpillars feed on milkweeds and dogbanes; central to Tamil Nadu’s seasonal Eastern–Western Ghats migrations.

Typical habitats: Hills, plains, urban parks, and forest corridors throughout Tamil Nadu.

Field photography tip: Count subapical spots and pale streaks in the forewing cell to separate Blue Tiger vs Dark Blue Tiger and the Crow species. Record both basking upperside and resting underside views, especially for bushbrowns, treebrowns, sailers, and oakleaf butterflies.

Delicate translucent tiger with a broad black-lined glassy patch in the forewing cell

Identification marks

Upperwing: Sooty black-brown boldly patterned with semi-transparent bluish-white glassy streaks and submarginal spots; forewing discal cell contains a wide club-shaped glassy patch traversed by two fine dark lines (subspecies P. a. aglea). Sexes look alike, though males bear a small dark grey scent patch near the hindwing tornus.

Underwing: Paler dark brown with the same crisp translucent bluish-white streaks, narrow black veins, and clean white spotting on head and thorax.

Lookalikes: Separated from Blue Tiger (Tirumala limniace) and Dark Blue Tiger (Tirumala septentrionis) by pale translucent glassy-white markings instead of vivid turquoise-blue spots and by the wide black-veined glassy forewing cell.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Moist evergreen and deciduous hill forests, shaded valleys, wooded estates, and seasonal migration corridors.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Widespread in Western and Eastern Ghats including Nilgiris, Mudumalai, Siruvani, Kallar, Anamalais, Palani Hills, Shevaroys, Pachamalai, and Kalakkad-Mundanthurai.

Seasonality: Present year-round in forested hills, peaking from September to December during post-monsoon dispersal.

Flight & behaviour

Glides slowly and effortlessly in dappled shade and along forest paths, pausing frequently on Crotalaria, Chromolaena, and Heliotropium for nectar and pyrrolizidine alkaloids.

Field note: Joins mixed Danainae assemblages in Tamil Nadu's seasonal Ghats flights and sequesters cardiac glycosides and alkaloids for chemical defense.
