Field guide notes for Nymphalidae · Satyrinae (Browns, Bushbrowns, Treebrowns & Rings)

Family overview (Nymphalidae, Brush-footed butterflies): Tamil Nadu’s largest butterfly family (97 checklist species), encompassing tigers, crows, pansies, eggflies, sailers, barons, rajahs, browns, and Tamil Nadu’s state butterfly, the Tamil Yeoman.

Subfamily traits (Satyrinae): Shade-loving brown butterflies marked with concentric underside eyespots (ocelli) that frequently exhibit wet- and dry-season forms.

Typical habitats: Grasslands, forest understorey, bamboo groves, palm groves, and shaded gardens.

Field photography tip: Underside ocelli sizes, brand marks, and discal line shape (straight vs curved) are crucial for species ID. Record both basking upperside and resting underside views, especially for bushbrowns, treebrowns, sailers, and oakleaf butterflies.

Evergreen-forest bushbrown instantly tagged by an oblique white forewing tip bar

Identification marks

Upperwing: Deep blackish-brown on both wings crossed cleanly by an oblique snow-white preapical bar near the forewing tip (wider in females); males possess a pale yellow hair-tuft over a hindwing scent brand.

Underwing: Dark smoky brown across the basal half contrasting sharply with a pale ochre-buff outer marginal border; carries submarginal ringed ocelli (2-3 on forewing, 7 on hindwing) that diminish in dry-season adults while keeping the crisp white forewing bar.

Lookalikes: Unmistakable among Tamil Nadu Mycalesis species by the pure white oblique preapical bar on the dark forewing upperwing.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Shaded mid-to-high elevation evergreen and semi-evergreen hill forests and damp forest trails.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Western Ghats hill ranges of Tamil Nadu including Nilgiris, Gudalur, Siruvani, Anamalais, Palani Hills, Meghamalai, and Kalakkad-Mundanthurai.

Seasonality: Active throughout the year in wet Western Ghats forests, peaking from August to January.

Flight & behaviour

Hugs dark undergrowth and damp mossy banks, flying short distances low to the ground and feeding on rotting forest fruit and tree sap.

Field note: A reliable indicator of shaded, intact evergreen forest floor cover in Tamil Nadu hills.
