Field guide notes for Nymphalidae · Satyrinae (Browns, Bushbrowns, Treebrowns & Rings)

Family overview (Nymphalidae, Brush-footed butterflies): Tamil Nadu’s largest butterfly family (97 checklist species), encompassing tigers, crows, pansies, eggflies, sailers, barons, rajahs, browns, and Tamil Nadu’s state butterfly, the Tamil Yeoman.

Subfamily traits (Satyrinae): Shade-loving brown butterflies marked with concentric underside eyespots (ocelli) that frequently exhibit wet- and dry-season forms.

Typical habitats: Grasslands, forest understorey, bamboo groves, palm groves, and shaded gardens.

Field photography tip: Underside ocelli sizes, brand marks, and discal line shape (straight vs curved) are crucial for species ID. Record both basking upperside and resting underside views, especially for bushbrowns, treebrowns, sailers, and oakleaf butterflies.

Montane Western Ghats bushbrown with fiery orange-ringed eyespots on dark umber wings

Identification marks

Upperwing: Dark umber-brown; forewing carries a prominent white-pupilled lower black ocellus ringed with bright reddish-orange and a tiny subapical spot, while the hindwing bears a tidy row of 2-4 small orange-ringed submarginal ocelli.

Underwing: Dark umber-brown sprinkled with fine transverse striae, with the basal half sharply demarcated by a dark brown discal line and broadly paler outer margins; bears 2 forewing and 7 curved small orange-ringed hindwing ocelli.

Lookalikes: Separated from Red-disc Bushbrown (Telinga oculus) by having only a narrow orange ring around the forewing ocellus rather than a huge bright orange-red patch, and from Palni Bushbrown (Telinga davisoni) by brighter orange eye rings and 2 (not 4) forewing ocelli below.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: High-elevation shola-grassland margins, montane evergreen understorey, and shaded hill streams.

Tamil Nadu distribution: High-elevation Western Ghats of Tamil Nadu including Nilgiris (Coonoor, Ooty, Kotagiri), Mudumalai upper slopes, Siruvani, Anamalais, and Palani Hills.

Seasonality: Active across mountain habitats most of the year, peaking in March-May and September-December.

Flight & behaviour

Flies low along sunlit shola edges and bracken slopes, basking with half-open wings in chilly mountain sunshine before diving into dense grass.

Field note: Strictly endemic to the southern Western Ghats and formerly placed under Mycalesis or Heteropsis before recent molecular studies restored Telinga.
