Field guide notes for Lycaenidae · Polyommatinae (Grass Blues, Pierrots, Lineblues & Ceruleans)

Family overview (Lycaenidae, Blues and hairstreaks): Small, jewel-like butterflies (96 checklist species) including grass blues, pierrots, lineblues, ceruleans, silverlines, oakblues, royals, flashes, and sunbeams.

Subfamily traits (Polyommatinae): Delicate small blues with distinctive underside spot chains, zebra stripes, or white-and-black pied markings.

Typical habitats: Lawns, grasslands, damp sand, woodland edges, and hill paths across all elevations.

Field photography tip: Macro underside views showing hindwing sub-basal spots and tornal orange eyespots are essential. Sharp macro shots of the hindwing underside bands and spot alignments are usually far more diagnostic than upperside blue.

Identification marks

Upperwing: Males are dark lilac-blue with a relatively wide dusky brown forewing outer border and dark hindwing marginal spots capped with orange; females are brown with faint pale blue basal dusting and clearer orange-ringed hindwing spots.

Underwing: Pale whitish-grey marked cleanly with white-edged dark brown postdiscal spots and bars, four jet-black sub-basal spots across the hindwing, two black subtornal eyespots crowned in bright orange and speckled with metallic scales, and a short white-tipped tail.

Lookalikes: Smaller with cleaner whitish-grey underside and wider male dark border than Plains Cupid (Chilades pandava); distinguished from Babul Blues (Azanus) by possessing a fine tail and bright orange tornal crowns.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Arid thorn scrub, Acacia grasslands, dry foothill pastures, and open sandy scrub.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Found across drier plains and foothills of Tamil Nadu including Coimbatore, Erode, Madurai, Tirunelveli, Ramanathapuram, Sathyamangalam, and Chengalpattu scrub.

Seasonality: Active throughout dry and post-monsoon months, peaking from October to March.

Flight & behaviour

Flits rapidly close to low thorny scrub and dry grass clumps; settles on tiny dry-zone herbs and damp soil margins.

Field note: Often confused with tiny Chilades pandava in dry scrub, yet strictly tied to Mimosoideae thorn shrubs rather than cycads and tended by ants.
