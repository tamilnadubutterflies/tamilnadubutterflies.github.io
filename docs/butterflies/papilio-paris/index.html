Field guide notes for Papilionidae · Papilioninae (True Swallowtails & Birdwings)

Family overview (Papilionidae, Swallowtails): Large to very large, strong-flying butterflies including birdwings, roses, mimes, Mormons, jays, swordtails, and banded peacocks. Many species carry prominent hindwing tails and feed actively while fluttering their wings at flowers.

Subfamily traits (Papilioninae): Includes Graphium jays and swordtails, Pachliopta roses, Papilio swallowtails, and the Southern Birdwing (Troides minos).

Typical habitats: Forest edges, hill streams, gardens, scrub, and evergreen shola margins across Tamil Nadu.

Field photography tip: Note hindwing tail shape, body colour (red vs black), and submarginal spot patterns. Both upperwing (dorsal) and underwing (ventral) photographs help separate mimics, mimes, and closely related dark Papilio species.

Black-and-green swallowtail carrying a glowing sapphire hindwing patch

Identification marks

Upperwing: Black thickly dusted with metallic green scales, illuminated by a large iridescent peacock-blue patch on the upper hindwing and a narrow green forewing line.

Underwing: Dull charcoal-black with a white-edged row of reddish-orange submarginal lunules along the hindwing.

Lookalikes: Easily separated from Papilio crino by the brilliant square-ish blue hindwing patch instead of a continuous narrow band.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Evergreen hill forests, moist forest clearings, streams, and shaded trails.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Nilgiris, Mudumalai, Siruvani, Valparai, Palani Hills, and southern Western Ghats.

Seasonality: Recorded mostly during monsoon and winter across Western Ghats hills.

Flight & behaviour

Strong, patrolling flight; fond of sunlit stream-bed pebbles and wet sand.

Field note: Recorded during the June 2024 Mudumalai Tiger Reserve Monsoon Butterfly Survey.
