Field guide notes for Lycaenidae · Theclinae (Silverlines, Oakblues, Royals, Flashes & Yamfly)

Family overview (Lycaenidae, Blues and hairstreaks): Small, jewel-like butterflies (96 checklist species) including grass blues, pierrots, lineblues, ceruleans, silverlines, oakblues, royals, flashes, and sunbeams.

Subfamily traits (Theclinae): Often tailed hairstreaks with metallic silver bars (Cigaritis), deep violet-blue upperwings (Arhopala), or swift red-and-slate flashes (Rapala).

Typical habitats: Scrub jungles, forest canopies, mistletoe-bearing trees, and rocky hilltops.

Field photography tip: Capture the hindwing tornal lobes, tail count, and arrangement of underside bands. Sharp macro shots of the hindwing underside bands and spot alignments are usually far more diagnostic than upperside blue.

Identification marks

Upperwing: Males are deep glossy violet-blue with noticeably broad black costal and outer borders on both forewing and hindwing; females are dark greyish-black with muted pale bluish scaling confined to the basal wing centers and a more rounded forewing apex.

Underwing: Warm brown overlaid with a soft rosy or pale lilac sheen, crossed by a long narrow slightly curved forewing discal band, a broken hindwing discal band, white-dusted scaling around the anal angle, and a slender white-tipped tail at vein 2.

Lookalikes: Smaller than Large Oakblue (Arhopala amantes) and Centaur Oakblue (Arhopala centaurus), lacking silvery-green cell outlines; separated from Tamil Oakblue (Arhopala bazaloides) by its rosy-lilac underside tone and lack of frosty pale apical forewing dusting.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Dense wet evergreen and moist semi-evergreen forest canopies and shaded hill streams in the southern Western Ghats.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Endemic to the Western Ghats, occurring locally in Tamil Nadu wet forests across the Nilgiris, Anamalais, Meghamalai, and Kalakkad-Mundanthurai.

Seasonality: Observed mainly during post-monsoon and cool winter months from October to March.

Flight & behaviour

Shade-loving forest dweller that keeps close to evergreen undergrowth and mid-storey branches, settling motionless with closed wings on leaf undersides.

Field note: Also called Sahyadri Rosy Oakblue; its restricted Western Ghats distribution and dependence on intact evergreen canopy trees make it a key bioindicator of forest continuity.
