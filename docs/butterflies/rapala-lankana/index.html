Field guide notes for Lycaenidae · Theclinae (Silverlines, Oakblues, Royals, Flashes & Yamfly)

Family overview (Lycaenidae, Blues and hairstreaks): Small, jewel-like butterflies (96 checklist species) including grass blues, pierrots, lineblues, ceruleans, silverlines, oakblues, royals, flashes, and sunbeams.

Subfamily traits (Theclinae): Often tailed hairstreaks with metallic silver bars (Cigaritis), deep violet-blue upperwings (Arhopala), or swift red-and-slate flashes (Rapala).

Typical habitats: Scrub jungles, forest canopies, mistletoe-bearing trees, and rocky hilltops.

Field photography tip: Capture the hindwing tornal lobes, tail count, and arrangement of underside bands. Sharp macro shots of the hindwing underside bands and spot alignments are usually far more diagnostic than upperside blue.

Deep-purple hill flash carrying a warm rusty-brown underside

Identification marks

Upperwing: Males appear nearly velvety black unless angled in sunlight, when the lower forewing and entire hindwing glow deep rich purple with a rusty spot on the anal lobe; females are pale violet-brown with a thin black margin and rusty anal lobe.

Underwing: Warm pale ferruginous (rusty-brown) darkening toward the wing margins, crossed cleanly by a straight deep ferruginous discal band edged with white speckles only near the curved hindwing anal portion, plus a black tornal spot and white-tipped black tail.

Lookalikes: Instantly told apart from Slate Flash (Rapala manea) and Indigo Flash (Rapala varuna) by its warm rusty-ferruginous underside lacking broad white-rimmed dark grey bands, and separated from Cornelian (Deudorix epijarbas) female by its rusty rather than grey-brown underside.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Wet evergreen, semi-evergreen, and moist deciduous hill forests along wooded stream valleys.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Southern and central Western Ghats of Tamil Nadu including Nilgiris, Siruvani, Anamalais, Meghamalai, and Kalakkad-Mundanthurai.

Seasonality: Recorded sporadically between October and April in moist hill tracts.

Flight & behaviour

Uncommon, fast-flying forest-edge butterfly that perches on sunlit mid-storey twigs, visits damp forest paths, and feeds briefly at hill-forest blossoms.

Field note: Restricted to the Western Ghats and Sri Lanka; its deep purple male sheen and rusty-ferruginous underside make it one of southern India's most distinctive Flashes.
