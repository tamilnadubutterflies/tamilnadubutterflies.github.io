Field guide notes for Nymphalidae · Cyrestinae (Map Butterflies)

Family overview (Nymphalidae, Brush-footed butterflies): Tamil Nadu’s largest butterfly family (97 checklist species), encompassing tigers, crows, pansies, eggflies, sailers, barons, rajahs, browns, and Tamil Nadu’s state butterfly, the Tamil Yeoman.

Subfamily traits (Cyrestinae): Delicate white wings patterned with fine dark contour-like lines and rusty-orange hindwing margins, represented by Common Map.

Typical habitats: Hill streams, moist boulders, and wet evergreen forest paths.

Field photography tip: Capture open-wing dorsal views showing the fine dark map-like venation lines. Record both basking upperside and resting underside views, especially for bushbrowns, treebrowns, sailers, and oakleaf butterflies.

Fragile translucent white butterfly etched with dark cartographic map lines

Identification marks

Upperwing: Semi-translucent creamy-white wings (pale yellow-tinted in dry-season forms) laced with delicate dark brown map-like veins and wavy transverse lines, dusky brown forewing tips, short pointed hindwing tails, and a bright orange-ochre tornal patch dotted with black.

Underwing: Mirrors the crisp cartographic line network of the upperwing through thin translucent scales, showing paler outer marginal lines and the orange tornal patch.

Lookalikes: Unmistakable in Tamil Nadu for its translucent paper-like wings covered in fine dark contour-like lines and a small hindwing tail.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Moist deciduous and evergreen hill forests, shaded mountain streams, waterfalls, and riparian fig groves.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Widespread across Western Ghats slopes (Nilgiris, Mudumalai, Siruvani, Anamalai, Palani Hills, KMTR) and higher Eastern Ghats ranges such as Kolli and Shervarayan hills.

Seasonality: Year-round in wet hill forests, with conspicuous puddling congregations during dry sunny spells from November to April.

Flight & behaviour

Glides gracefully with flatly held wings across forest streams, often resting upside-down on the underside of leaves and gathering in tight clusters on wet stream sand.

Field note: Southern Indian subspecies Cyrestis thyodamas indica; its fragile semi-transparent wings resemble tattered leaves or spiderwebs when motionless beneath foliage.
