Field guide notes for Nymphalidae · Satyrinae (Browns, Bushbrowns, Treebrowns & Rings)

Family overview (Nymphalidae, Brush-footed butterflies): Tamil Nadu’s largest butterfly family (97 checklist species), encompassing tigers, crows, pansies, eggflies, sailers, barons, rajahs, browns, and Tamil Nadu’s state butterfly, the Tamil Yeoman.

Subfamily traits (Satyrinae): Shade-loving brown butterflies marked with concentric underside eyespots (ocelli) that frequently exhibit wet- and dry-season forms.

Typical habitats: Grasslands, forest understorey, bamboo groves, palm groves, and shaded gardens.

Field photography tip: Underside ocelli sizes, brand marks, and discal line shape (straight vs curved) are crucial for species ID. Record both basking upperside and resting underside views, especially for bushbrowns, treebrowns, sailers, and oakleaf butterflies.

Tailed Western Ghats endemic palmfly mimicking milkweed tigers in flight

Identification marks

Upperwing: Dark brownish-black forewings patterned with a bold cream-white subapical band and pale bluish-grey marginal spots, paired with bright chestnut-orange hindwings bearing white-centered black borders and a sharp narrow tail at vein 4. Females are larger with a broader white forewing band.

Underwing: Cryptically marbled in intricate dark brown, cream-white, and rufous striations that camouflage the resting butterfly against dry palm fronds and bark.

Lookalikes: Separated cleanly from Common Palmfly (Elymnias hypermnestra) by the prominent tail projection on the hindwing and the pale subapical band present in both sexes.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Moist evergreen and semi-evergreen foothills, shaded forest gardens, and areca-coconut estates.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Western Ghats foothills and hills of Tamil Nadu including Gudalur, Nilgiris, Siruvani, Kallar, Anamalais, Palani foothills, and Kanyakumari.

Seasonality: Recorded throughout the year across wet Western Ghats slopes, peaking between July and January.

Flight & behaviour

Flies weakly and lazily in dappled shade around palm crowns, mimicking unpalatable Striped and Plain Tigers until settling suddenly with closed wings.

Field note: Endemic to the Western Ghats and southern peninsular India, serving as a classic Batesian mimic of toxic Danainae butterflies.
