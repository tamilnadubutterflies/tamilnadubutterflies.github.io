Field guide notes for Nymphalidae · Limenitidinae (Sailers, Sergeants, Lascars, Barons & Clipper)

Family overview (Nymphalidae, Brush-footed butterflies): Tamil Nadu’s largest butterfly family (97 checklist species), encompassing tigers, crows, pansies, eggflies, sailers, barons, rajahs, browns, and Tamil Nadu’s state butterfly, the Tamil Yeoman.

Subfamily traits (Limenitidinae): Gliding butterflies with characteristic black-and-white banded wings (Neptis, Athyma) or emerald-olive camouflaged upperwings (Euthalia, Dophla).

Typical habitats: Forest trails, stream banks, orchards, and wooded gardens.

Field photography tip: Check whether the forewing cell streak is broken into spots or continuous, and compare underside ground colour. Record both basking upperside and resting underside views, especially for bushbrowns, treebrowns, sailers, and oakleaf butterflies.

Magnificent large emerald-and-pearl glider of Tamil Nadu hill forests

Identification marks

Upperwing: Velvety olive-emerald wings crossed by translucent pearly-white forewing spots and bold dark tiger-like stripes.

Underwing: Paler bluish-green echoing the translucent forewing window spots.

Lookalikes: Huge wingspan, translucent pearly forewing squares, and sweeping gliding flight make Clipper unique.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Moist evergreen and semi-evergreen forests, wooded waterfalls, and hill streams.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Western Ghats forests of Tamil Nadu including Kovai Kutralam, Siruvani, Mudumalai, Valparai, and Tirunelveli.

Seasonality: Year-round in wet hill forests.

Flight & behaviour

Powerful, soaring glider that perches high with wings spread flat and visits Mussaenda and Lantana.

Field note: Subspecies Parthenos sylvia virens patrols riparian corridors and roosts beneath high riverside branches at dusk.
