Field guide notes for Nymphalidae · Libytheinae (Snout Butterflies / Beaks)

Family overview (Nymphalidae, Brush-footed butterflies): Tamil Nadu’s largest butterfly family (97 checklist species), encompassing tigers, crows, pansies, eggflies, sailers, barons, rajahs, browns, and Tamil Nadu’s state butterfly, the Tamil Yeoman.

Subfamily traits (Libytheinae): Unmistakable elongated labial palpi projecting forward like a beak; dark brown wings with orange-tawny patches.

Typical habitats: Hill streams, wet roadsides, forest clearings, and mud-puddling patches.

Field photography tip: Inspect forewing orange band breaks and hindwing underside mottling. Record both basking upperside and resting underside views, especially for bushbrowns, treebrowns, sailers, and oakleaf butterflies.

Cryptic snout butterfly of drier scrub and foothills with a divided orange forewing streak

Identification marks

Upperwing: Deep brown with notched outer margins and projecting snout-like palpi; forewing displays a fragmented orange cell streak split into a narrow basal bar and a detached discal lobe alongside whitish-orange subapical spots, and hindwing bears a wavy lower orange band (subspecies L. l. lepitoides).

Underwing: Forewing underside clearly retains the broken orange cell pattern; hindwing shifts from warm brown-grey to finely freckled ash-grey bark mottling, becoming more cryptic in dry months.

Lookalikes: Distinguished from Club Beak (Libythea myrrha) by the cleanly separated basal streak and rectangular discal spot inside the forewing cell, and a more strongly scalloped outer forewing margin.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Dry deciduous forests, scrub jungles, foothill riverbeds, and thorny woodland edges.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Foothills and dry tracts across Tamil Nadu including Coimbatore, Kallar, Sathyamangalam, Salem, Pachamalai, Gingee, and Tirunelveli.

Seasonality: Active throughout the year in drier zones, with noticeable surges during October-January and pre-monsoon dry spells.

Flight & behaviour

Nervous, rapid flier that settles abruptly on dry twigs, rocks, or wet sand to puddle, holding its wings tightly folded to expose only its bark-patterned underside.

Field note: Tolerates noticeably drier and lower-elevation thorn-scrub habitats than the more moisture-loving Club Beak.
