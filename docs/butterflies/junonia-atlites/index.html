Field guide notes for Nymphalidae · Nymphalinae (Pansies, Eggflies, Admirals & Oakleaf)

Family overview (Nymphalidae, Brush-footed butterflies): Tamil Nadu’s largest butterfly family (97 checklist species), encompassing tigers, crows, pansies, eggflies, sailers, barons, rajahs, browns, and Tamil Nadu’s state butterfly, the Tamil Yeoman.

Subfamily traits (Nymphalinae): Familiar, colourful butterflies featuring conspicuous eyespots, seasonal morphs, and dramatic dead-leaf underside camouflage.

Typical habitats: Gardens, wetlands, agricultural bunds, scrublands, and hill forests.

Field photography tip: In Junonia pansies and Hypolimnas eggflies, both dorsal colour patches and ventral patterns aid rapid ID. Record both basking upperside and resting underside views, especially for bushbrowns, treebrowns, sailers, and oakleaf butterflies.

Pale silvery-grey marsh pansy ringed with delicate orange-centered eyespots

Identification marks

Upperwing: Pale lavender-grey to silvery-buff crossed by delicate dark wavy lines across the cells and discs, featuring a curved row of orange-and-black centered eyespots across the outer half of both wings; females are slightly darker and larger.

Underwing: Very pale silvery-white to soft buff-grey with faint wavy lines and reduced eyespots in dry-season forms, sharpening to crisp dark-edged eyespots in wet-season individuals.

Lookalikes: Pale silvery-grey ground colour with delicate wavy lines and a row of small orange-rimmed eyespots easily distinguishes it from Lemon (Junonia lemonias) and Chocolate (Junonia iphita) Pansies.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Paddy bunds, marsh edges, wet grassy ditches, canal banks, and humid coastal gardens.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Common across irrigated lowlands, Cauvery delta, coastal marshes (Chennai, Chengalpattu, Vedanthangal, Point Calimere), and foothill wetlands statewide.

Seasonality: Year-round near water bodies, becoming especially abundant from August to February after rains.

Flight & behaviour

Flutters gently low over damp marsh herbs and rice-field margins, basking openly on grass blades and nectaring on Phyla nodiflora and Tridax.

Field note: Strongly associated with humid wetlands and irrigated paddy landscapes, often sharing Hygrophila host beds with Peacock Pansy (Junonia almana).
