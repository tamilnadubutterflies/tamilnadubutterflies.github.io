Field guide notes for Pieridae · Coliadinae (Yellows, Emigrants & Clouded Yellows)

Family overview (Pieridae, Whites and yellows): Medium-to-small butterflies dominated by white, yellow, orange, and black pigments, ranging from garden grass yellows and emigrants to hill-dwelling clouded yellows and forest albatrosses.

Subfamily traits (Coliadinae): Yellow, pale green, or orange butterflies with rapid sustained flight and strong migratory behaviour in several species.

Typical habitats: Open grasslands, agricultural margins, scrub, riverbeds, and Nilgiri/Palani high-altitude meadows (Colias).

Field photography tip: For Eurema grass yellows, zoom into the underside forewing cell to count the small dark cell spots. Capture the closed-wing underside when possible, spot counts in Eurema and wingbar markings in Appias and Ixias are key underside clues.

Open-grassland grass yellow with smoothly curved forewing black border

Identification marks

Upperwing: Warm deep yellow with a broad black forewing tip and outer margin whose inner edge sweeps in an unbroken smooth arc (lacking the deep square notch of E. hecabe).

Underwing: Buff to pale ochre-yellow with tiny scattered grey-black cell dots and indistinct dusky marginal speckles in dry-season broods.

Lookalikes: Immediately distinguished from Common and Three-spot Grass Yellows by the evenly curved black dorsal forewing border lacking an inward tooth in mid-wing.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Dry grasslands, open hill meadows, scrub margins, roadsides, and fallow agricultural plots.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Common across Tamil Nadu plains and open hill slopes from Coimbatore and Tirunelveli up to Nilgiri and Palani grasslands.

Seasonality: Active throughout the year with clear wet-season (darker) and dry-season (paler reddish-fringed) morphs.

Flight & behaviour

Skims quickly just centimeters above short grasses, frequently visiting yellow Asteraceae blooms such as Tridax procumbens.

Field note: Often tolerates drier, sun-baked rocky slopes better than other grass yellows thanks to prostrate Chamaecrista host herbs.
