Field guide notes for Hesperiidae · Hesperiinae (Grass Skippers, Swifts, Aces & Darts)

Family overview (Hesperiidae, Skippers): Compact, fast-darting butterflies (83 checklist species) such as awls, flats, angles, aces, bobs, demons, swifts, and grass darts.

Subfamily traits (Hesperiinae): Fast-flying tawny, brown, or black-and-yellow skippers that typically hold forewings and hindwings at different angles when basking.

Typical habitats: Grasslands, paddy margins, palms, bamboo thickets, and forest undergrowth.

Field photography tip: Zoom into the underside hindwing spot pattern and forewing cell spots to distinguish swifts and darts. Photograph both the upperwing hyaline spots and the underside hindwing spot arcs, essential for distinguishing swifts and aces.

Identification marks

Upperwing: Dull olive-brown forewing bearing narrow pale yellowish-white translucent spots comprising one small upper cell spot (lower cell spot usually absent), three small discal spots in spaces 2-4, two or three subapical dots, and no spot in space 1b; hindwing unmarked.

Underwing: Pale greyish-ochreous brown across the hindwing, showing a faint curved arc of four or five tiny white discal dots across spaces 2-6 and no white cell spot.

Lookalikes: Separated from Rice Swift (Borbo cinnara) by duller greyish-ochreous hindwing underside with up to five faint evenly spaced dots (including space 4) instead of three bold spots in spaces 2, 3, and 6, plus a single upper cell spot; separated from Pelopidas swifts by lacking a hindwing cell spot.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Dry grasslands, upland meadows, paddy field edges, scrub foothills, and sunny woodland clearings.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Widespread across Tamil Nadu plains and hills including Coimbatore, Chennai, Salem, Nilgiris, Palani Hills, and Tirunelveli scrub.

Seasonality: Active throughout the year across grassy plains and foothills, peaking from August to February.

Flight & behaviour

Darts swiftly between low grass stalks and perches in full sun in a two-angle jet-plane stance; visits Tridax procumbens, Lantana, and Chromolaena flowers and puddles on damp soil.

Field note: Treated in some references as Pseudoborbo bevani; often feeds alongside Rice Swifts on roadside flowers across dry interior districts.
