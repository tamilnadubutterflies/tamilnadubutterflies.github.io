Field guide notes for Nymphalidae · Satyrinae (Browns, Bushbrowns, Treebrowns & Rings)

Family overview (Nymphalidae, Brush-footed butterflies): Tamil Nadu’s largest butterfly family (97 checklist species), encompassing tigers, crows, pansies, eggflies, sailers, barons, rajahs, browns, and Tamil Nadu’s state butterfly, the Tamil Yeoman.

Subfamily traits (Satyrinae): Shade-loving brown butterflies marked with concentric underside eyespots (ocelli) that frequently exhibit wet- and dry-season forms.

Typical habitats: Grasslands, forest understorey, bamboo groves, palm groves, and shaded gardens.

Field photography tip: Underside ocelli sizes, brand marks, and discal line shape (straight vs curved) are crucial for species ID. Record both basking upperside and resting underside views, especially for bushbrowns, treebrowns, sailers, and oakleaf butterflies.

Delicate foothill ringlet carrying finely striated white hindwings and neat yellow-ringed spots

Identification marks

Upperwing: Dark greyish-brown; males are plain brown above with a faint or small forewing ocellus and 1-3 tiny hindwing dots, while females show a paler whitish-dusted hindwing submarginal zone framing small dark eyespots.

Underwing: White cleanly overlaid with fine, even rufous-brown striations lacking heavy dark transverse bands; forewing carries a large oval bi-pupilled yellow-ringed ocellus, while hindwing bears 4-6 small yellow-ringed ocelli (typically a double or paired apical unit and 3-4 neat postdiscal spots in a shallow arc).

Lookalikes: Separated from Nilgiri Jewel Four-ring (Ypthima striata) by finer, more uniform underside striations lacking bold dark brown transverse bands anchoring the hindwing spots, and from White Four-ring by lacking a solid stark white hindwing patch above in males.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Dry-to-moist foothill grasslands, open hill savannas, and scrubby forest clearings.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Foothills and mid-elevation slopes across southern Tamil Nadu including Mudumalai, Amaravathi-Chinnar slopes, Palani foothills, Sirumalai, Kalakkad-Mundanthurai, and Kolli Hills.

Seasonality: Uncommon and localized; most records fall between July and January.

Flight & behaviour

Weak, low-skimming grass flier that weaves neatly among rocky hillside grass tufts and feeds on small composite blooms.

Field note: Shared between Sri Lanka and peninsular southern India; historically confused with Y. striata and Y. avanta complexes until genital and striation characters clarified regional records.

Caterpillar host plants

Foothill Poaceae grassesThemeda and Cymbopogon grasses
