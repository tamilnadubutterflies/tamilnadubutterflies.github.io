Field guide notes for Lycaenidae · Theclinae (Silverlines, Oakblues, Royals, Flashes & Yamfly)

Family overview (Lycaenidae, Blues and hairstreaks): Small, jewel-like butterflies (96 checklist species) including grass blues, pierrots, lineblues, ceruleans, silverlines, oakblues, royals, flashes, and sunbeams.

Subfamily traits (Theclinae): Often tailed hairstreaks with metallic silver bars (Cigaritis), deep violet-blue upperwings (Arhopala), or swift red-and-slate flashes (Rapala).

Typical habitats: Scrub jungles, forest canopies, mistletoe-bearing trees, and rocky hilltops.

Field photography tip: Capture the hindwing tornal lobes, tail count, and arrangement of underside bands. Sharp macro shots of the hindwing underside bands and spot alignments are usually far more diagnostic than upperside blue.

Lowland mistletoe royal breeding quietly across gardens and scrub plains

Identification marks

Upperwing: Males are duller slaty-blue than Peacock Royal with broad black forewing costal and apical borders; females are paler greyish-blue with a small whitish patch beyond the forewing cell, broader hindwing margins, and a darker discal lunular band.

Underwing: Creamy greyish-white crossed by a fine continuous wavy brown discal line, two faint submarginal lunular rows, two prominent black tornal spots capped in dull orange-red, and two white-tipped black tails at veins 1b and 2.

Lookalikes: Distinguished from Peacock Royal (Tajuria cippus) by its duller slaty-blue male upperside, continuous fine brown underside discal line rather than disjoint black dashes, and duller orange-red tornal caps; lacks the male hair tuft of Tufted White Royal (Pratapa deva).

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Open plains, urban gardens, dry scrub, roadside avenue trees, and dry deciduous foothills supporting Dendrophthoe mistletoes.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Widespread across Tamil Nadu plains and foothills including Chennai, Coimbatore, Tiruchirappalli, Madurai, Sathyamangalam, and eastern Western Ghats fringes.

Seasonality: Active throughout the year across plains and foothills, common from October to March.

Flight & behaviour

Less strictly tied to high forest canopy than other royals; flies quickly around mistletoe clumps on roadside neem, mango, or tamarind trees and perches at medium height.

Field note: Early stages on Loranthaceae mistletoes in Coimbatore were documented in detail by TNBS member Ramanasaran Hari in Pattampoochi Magazine.
