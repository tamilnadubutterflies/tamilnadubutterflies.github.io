Field guide notes for Nymphalidae · Satyrinae (Browns, Bushbrowns, Treebrowns & Rings)

Family overview (Nymphalidae, Brush-footed butterflies): Tamil Nadu’s largest butterfly family (97 checklist species), encompassing tigers, crows, pansies, eggflies, sailers, barons, rajahs, browns, and Tamil Nadu’s state butterfly, the Tamil Yeoman.

Subfamily traits (Satyrinae): Shade-loving brown butterflies marked with concentric underside eyespots (ocelli) that frequently exhibit wet- and dry-season forms.

Typical habitats: Grasslands, forest understorey, bamboo groves, palm groves, and shaded gardens.

Field photography tip: Underside ocelli sizes, brand marks, and discal line shape (straight vs curved) are crucial for species ID. Record both basking upperside and resting underside views, especially for bushbrowns, treebrowns, sailers, and oakleaf butterflies.

Common bushbrown carrying a short dark male brand and four straight-lined hindwing ocelli

Identification marks

Upperwing: Dark earthy brown with pale marginal hairlines and one prominent yellow-ringed lower ocellus in space 2 on the forewing (occasionally a faint apical dot); males have a short, small dark brown scent brand on the forewing underside and a pale hindwing hair-tuft.

Underwing: Wet-season form features a crisp whitish postdiscal band bordered inwardly by a dark line toothed near vein 1b, plus ringed ocelli (2 on forewing, 7 on hindwing) with the lower four hindwing ocelli arranged in a straight row; dry-season morph turns buff-brown with pinpoint dots.

Lookalikes: Separated from Common Bushbrown (Mycalesis perseus) by having the four posterior hindwing ocelli in a straight line and from Long-brand Bushbrown (Mycalesis visala) by the male's short dark brown forewing brand rather than a long yellow-ochre brand.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Shaded gardens, damp grass banks, scrub borders, plantations, and moist-to-dry forest edges.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Widespread statewide from Chennai and coastal plains to Coimbatore, Madurai, Salem, and Western and Eastern Ghats foothills.

Seasonality: Common throughout the year across Tamil Nadu, peaking in August-December.

Flight & behaviour

Flies weakly through shaded grass tufts and feeds on rotting fruit, wet soil, and sap rather than nectar flowers.

Field note: South Indian populations correspond to subspecies M. m. polydecta and show sharp seasonal contrast between ocellated wet broods and cryptic dry broods.
