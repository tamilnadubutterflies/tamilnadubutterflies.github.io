Field guide notes for Nymphalidae · Libytheinae (Snout Butterflies / Beaks)

Family overview (Nymphalidae, Brush-footed butterflies): Tamil Nadu’s largest butterfly family (97 checklist species), encompassing tigers, crows, pansies, eggflies, sailers, barons, rajahs, browns, and Tamil Nadu’s state butterfly, the Tamil Yeoman.

Subfamily traits (Libytheinae): Unmistakable elongated labial palpi projecting forward like a beak; dark brown wings with orange-tawny patches.

Typical habitats: Hill streams, wet roadsides, forest clearings, and mud-puddling patches.

Field photography tip: Inspect forewing orange band breaks and hindwing underside mottling. Record both basking upperside and resting underside views, especially for bushbrowns, treebrowns, sailers, and oakleaf butterflies.

Fast-darting snout butterfly marked by an unbroken club-shaped orange forewing streak

Identification marks

Upperwing: Dark chocolate-brown with long forward-pointing beak-like palpi and squared wing tips; forewing bears an uninterrupted club-shaped bright orange streak from base through cell plus two pale orange-white subapical spots, while hindwing carries an oblique discal orange band (subspecies L. m. rama in South India).

Underwing: Forewing repeats the continuous orange cell streak and pale apical markings; hindwing is cryptically marbled in mottled greyish-brown and lilac striations that conceal perched adults like dry bark.

Lookalikes: Separated from Southern Beak (Libythea laius) by the continuous, unbroken orange streak running through the forewing cell rather than a streak split into basal and discal lobes.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Hill streams, moist deciduous and semi-evergreen forest paths, and wooded valleys.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Western and Eastern Ghats hill tracts including Nilgiris, Mudumalai, Siruvani, Kallar, Anamalais, Palani Hills, Shevaroys, and Kolli Hills.

Seasonality: Seen nearly year-round in the hills, peaking after monsoon rains between September and January and again in March-May.

Flight & behaviour

Flies swiftly with erratic, jerky turns along sunny stream banks, puddles avidly on damp sand and mineral-rich mud, and rests with wings snapped shut on bare twigs.

Field note: Often gathers in small puddling groups beside forest streams; the elongated labial palpi mimic a dead leaf petiole when perched.
