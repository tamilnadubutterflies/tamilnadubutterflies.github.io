Field guide notes for Nymphalidae · Satyrinae (Browns, Bushbrowns, Treebrowns & Rings)

Family overview (Nymphalidae, Brush-footed butterflies): Tamil Nadu’s largest butterfly family (97 checklist species), encompassing tigers, crows, pansies, eggflies, sailers, barons, rajahs, browns, and Tamil Nadu’s state butterfly, the Tamil Yeoman.

Subfamily traits (Satyrinae): Shade-loving brown butterflies marked with concentric underside eyespots (ocelli) that frequently exhibit wet- and dry-season forms.

Typical habitats: Grasslands, forest understorey, bamboo groves, palm groves, and shaded gardens.

Field photography tip: Underside ocelli sizes, brand marks, and discal line shape (straight vs curved) are crucial for species ID. Record both basking upperside and resting underside views, especially for bushbrowns, treebrowns, sailers, and oakleaf butterflies.

Hill-forest treebrown with a spotted white female band and an enlarged apical hindwing ocellus

Identification marks

Upperwing: Dark greyish-brown (subspecies L. r. neelgheriensis); females show a disjointed oblique forewing band composed of three separated white spots plus two white apical dots, whereas males lack the discal spots and carry only two small white subapical spots.

Underwing: Dark grey-brown crossed by a straight pale lilac-white inner discal line; hindwing bears an arched series of 6 ringed ocelli in which the uppermost apical eyespot is noticeably larger than the remaining five.

Lookalikes: Separated from Bamboo Treebrown (Lethe europa) by the broken three-spot white band in females and smaller separated forewing ocelli underneath, and from Tamil Treebrown (Lethe drypetis) by having one enlarged apical hindwing ocellus rather than two large eyespots.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Montane evergreen edges, shola borders, shaded forest clearings, and mid-to-high hill slopes.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Higher hill ranges across Tamil Nadu including Nilgiris, Kodaikanal, Siruvani, Anamalais, Shevaroys, Kolli Hills, and Kalakkad-Mundanthurai.

Seasonality: Common in the hills throughout the year, peaking during June-December.

Flight & behaviour

Bobs briskly along sunlit shola edges and shaded paths, settling abruptly on tree trunks, wet boulders, or damp earth.

Field note: Unlike the bamboo-obligate Lethe europa, the South Indian Common Treebrown feeds broadly on woodland grasses, letting it thrive up to high montane elevations.
