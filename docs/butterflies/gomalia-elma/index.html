Field guide notes for Hesperiidae · Pyrginae (Flats, Angles & Grizzled Skippers)

Family overview (Hesperiidae, Skippers): Compact, fast-darting butterflies (83 checklist species) such as awls, flats, angles, aces, bobs, demons, swifts, and grass darts.

Subfamily traits (Pyrginae): Skippers that commonly settle with wings spread flat against leaves or tucked underneath foliage.

Typical habitats: Forest trails, rocky streams, dry open grasslands, and hill clearings.

Field photography tip: Dorsal open-wing photos clearly reveal white hyaline forewing and hindwing spots. Photograph both the upperwing hyaline spots and the underside hindwing spot arcs, essential for distinguishing swifts and aces.

Compact olive-and-white marbled skipper of dry scrub and Abutilon thickets

Identification marks

Upperwing: Olive-brown marbled with blackish-brown bands, a square dark costal patch framed by white hyaline cell and subapical dots on the forewing, and a broad pale creamy median band across the hindwing.

Underwing: Paler greyish-olive repeating the crisp white median hindwing band and forewing cell markings, with white-ringed dark palpi and rounded, non-hooked antennal clubs.

Lookalikes: Separated from Indian Grizzled Skipper (Spialia galba) by its olive-marbled pattern, broad continuous white hindwing band, and lack of neat black-and-white checkered wing borders.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Arid scrub jungles, coastal dune thickets, fallow fields, rocky hillocks, and dry plains.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Widespread across Chennai, Chengalpattu, Madurai, Ramanathapuram, Coimbatore plains, Moyar scrub, and dry Eastern Ghats foothills.

Seasonality: Active throughout the year in dry plains and scrublands, peaking November to March.

Flight & behaviour

Darts rapidly inches above sun-baked ground around Abutilon bushes, perching flat on bare soil, stones, or tiny wildflowers with wings open.

Field note: South Asian populations represent subspecies Gomalia elma albofasciata; a classic dry-zone skipper shared between Africa and peninsular India.
