Field guide notes for Nymphalidae · Limenitidinae (Sailers, Sergeants, Lascars, Barons & Clipper)

Family overview (Nymphalidae, Brush-footed butterflies): Tamil Nadu’s largest butterfly family (97 checklist species), encompassing tigers, crows, pansies, eggflies, sailers, barons, rajahs, browns, and Tamil Nadu’s state butterfly, the Tamil Yeoman.

Subfamily traits (Limenitidinae): Gliding butterflies with characteristic black-and-white banded wings (Neptis, Athyma) or emerald-olive camouflaged upperwings (Euthalia, Dophla).

Typical habitats: Forest trails, stream banks, orchards, and wooded gardens.

Field photography tip: Check whether the forewing cell streak is broken into spots or continuous, and compare underside ground colour. Record both basking upperside and resting underside views, especially for bushbrowns, treebrowns, sailers, and oakleaf butterflies.

Tawny-orange dry-forest nymphalid flashing a brick-red white-banded underside

Identification marks

Upperwing: Bright tawny-orange framed by heavy black borders and crossed by bold black cell bars, black discal blotches, and a curved row of rounded black spots on the hindwing; sexes share similar patterns with slightly larger paler females.

Underwing: Rich brick-red to dark ochraceous-red, starkly patterned on the forewing with black-outlined crimson cell spots and a broad oblique creamy-white discal band edged in black.

Lookalikes: Unmistakable among Tamil Nadu Limenitidinae by its vivid tawny-orange and black-spotted upperside paired with a brick-red, white-banded underside; unlike dull brown Common Baron (Euthalia aconthea).

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Dry deciduous forests, thorny scrub jungles, open woodlands, savannah foothills, and forest roads.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Widespread in drier foothills and Eastern Ghats ranges including Kalvarayan, Shervarayan, Pachamalai, Sathyamangalam, and Mudumalai dry deciduous belts.

Seasonality: Active throughout the year, especially abundant from October to March in dry hill tracts.

Flight & behaviour

Skims briskly just above bare soil and dry leaf litter, settling flat with wings pressed wide to bask on sunlit dirt tracks and fallen fruits.

Field note: Recorded at 700-800 m elevation in Kalvarayan and Shervarayan Eastern Ghats surveys; its bright dorsal wings quickly snap shut to expose red-brown camouflage matching laterite forest soil.
