Field guide notes for Nymphalidae · Satyrinae (Browns, Bushbrowns, Treebrowns & Rings)

Family overview (Nymphalidae, Brush-footed butterflies): Tamil Nadu’s largest butterfly family (97 checklist species), encompassing tigers, crows, pansies, eggflies, sailers, barons, rajahs, browns, and Tamil Nadu’s state butterfly, the Tamil Yeoman.

Subfamily traits (Satyrinae): Shade-loving brown butterflies marked with concentric underside eyespots (ocelli) that frequently exhibit wet- and dry-season forms.

Typical habitats: Grasslands, forest understorey, bamboo groves, palm groves, and shaded gardens.

Field photography tip: Underside ocelli sizes, brand marks, and discal line shape (straight vs curved) are crucial for species ID. Record both basking upperside and resting underside views, especially for bushbrowns, treebrowns, sailers, and oakleaf butterflies.

Jewel-like Western Ghats bushbrown ringed beneath in metallic silvery-blue and gold

Identification marks

Upperwing: Dark umber-brown framed by twin bright ochre-yellow marginal lines; forewing features a large lower black ocellus topped by a broad orange-yellow iris and triangular orange discal patch plus a small apical spot, while hindwing carries two tiny tawny-ringed ocelli.

Underwing: Deep rich ochre-yellow crossed by a gleaming silvery-blue discal line; 2 forewing and 7 hindwing ocelli are encircled in outer metallic silvery-blue rings, with the apical and paired median hindwing spots glowing on bright yellow patches (subdued in dry season).

Lookalikes: Unmistakable in Tamil Nadu by its bright orange-ochre forewing patch above and rich golden underside adorned with metallic silvery-blue ocelli rings.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Shaded evergreen and semi-evergreen hill forests, mossy forest trails, and humid understorey.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Western Ghats forests of Tamil Nadu including Nilgiris, Gudalur, Siruvani, Anamalais, Meghamalai, and Kalakkad-Mundanthurai.

Seasonality: Active throughout the year in moist Western Ghats slopes, peaking between July and December.

Flight & behaviour

Flies close to shaded forest floors and rests with closed wings on damp rocks or ferns, briefly flashing bright yellow undersides during short hops.

Field note: Often regarded as the Western Ghats counterpart of Sri Lanka's Mycalesis patnia and sometimes treated as a full southern Indian endemic species.
