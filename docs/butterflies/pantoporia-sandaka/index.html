Field guide notes for Nymphalidae · Limenitidinae (Sailers, Sergeants, Lascars, Barons & Clipper)

Family overview (Nymphalidae, Brush-footed butterflies): Tamil Nadu’s largest butterfly family (97 checklist species), encompassing tigers, crows, pansies, eggflies, sailers, barons, rajahs, browns, and Tamil Nadu’s state butterfly, the Tamil Yeoman.

Subfamily traits (Limenitidinae): Gliding butterflies with characteristic black-and-white banded wings (Neptis, Athyma) or emerald-olive camouflaged upperwings (Euthalia, Dophla).

Typical habitats: Forest trails, stream banks, orchards, and wooded gardens.

Field photography tip: Check whether the forewing cell streak is broken into spots or continuous, and compare underside ground colour. Record both basking upperside and resting underside views, especially for bushbrowns, treebrowns, sailers, and oakleaf butterflies.

Elusive wet-forest lascar separated by beaded grey submarginal spots

Identification marks

Upperwing: Deep blackish-brown patterned in rich golden-orange; the forewing cell streak carries a distinct upper notch, and the outer orange submarginal line is bolder than the inner grey line, which breaks into bead-like pale dots; males bear a glossy whitish hindwing costal speculum.

Underwing: Warm pale ochre-yellow with crisp dark-outlined bands and fine submarginal lines, darkening subtly with softer edges in dry-season individuals.

Lookalikes: Very similar to Common Lascar (Pantoporia hordonia), but distinguished by the narrower, bead-broken grey forewing submarginal line, notched cell streak, and the male's pearly costal speculum on the dorsal hindwing.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Moist evergreen and semi-evergreen hill forests, shaded forest streams, and bamboo-rich valleys.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Uncommon resident of Tamil Nadu's Western Ghats forests, recorded from Nilgiris, Mudumalai, Siruvani, Anamalai, and Tirunelveli hills.

Seasonality: Seen mainly between September and March, overlapping with Common Lascar after monsoon rains.

Flight & behaviour

Patrols mid-height sunlit openings along forest trails with rapid wingbeats and stiff glides; males occasionally settle on damp sandbars and wet stones.

Field note: Represented in peninsular India by Pantoporia sandaka davidsoni; frequently overlooked in field surveys due to its close similarity to Common Lascar.
