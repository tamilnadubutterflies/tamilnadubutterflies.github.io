Field guide notes for Lycaenidae · Polyommatinae (Grass Blues, Pierrots, Lineblues & Ceruleans)

Family overview (Lycaenidae, Blues and hairstreaks): Small, jewel-like butterflies (96 checklist species) including grass blues, pierrots, lineblues, ceruleans, silverlines, oakblues, royals, flashes, and sunbeams.

Subfamily traits (Polyommatinae): Delicate small blues with distinctive underside spot chains, zebra stripes, or white-and-black pied markings.

Typical habitats: Lawns, grasslands, damp sand, woodland edges, and hill paths across all elevations.

Field photography tip: Macro underside views showing hindwing sub-basal spots and tornal orange eyespots are essential. Sharp macro shots of the hindwing underside bands and spot alignments are usually far more diagnostic than upperside blue.

Ubiquitous tailed lineblue flashing bright ochre beneath in dry months

Identification marks

Upperwing: Males are shining purple-blue with narrow dark brown borders; females are dull dark brown with pale blue scaling confined near the wing bases.

Underwing: Pale buff-brown to warm yellow-ochre (brightest in dry-season broods) crossed by pairs of crisp white lines enclosing darker sub-basal, cell-end, and postdiscal bands extending below the forewing cell, an orange-crowned black subtornal spot in space 2, and a short white-tipped tail.

Lookalikes: Separated instantly from Tailless Lineblue (Prosotas dubiosa) and White-tipped Lineblue (Prosotas noreia) by its distinct hindwing tail, and from Pointed Lineblue (Ionolyce helicon) by its rounded forewing apex.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Scrub jungles, deciduous foothills, forest trails, riverbanks, and wooded rural edges.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Statewide across Tamil Nadu from coastal scrub and interior plains to high Western and Eastern Ghats valleys.

Seasonality: Abundant year-round across Tamil Nadu hills and plains, peaking after monsoons.

Flight & behaviour

Weak erratic flight close to thorny shrubs and stream edges; dozens of males pack tightly onto wet mud patches during hot midday hours.

Field note: Shows marked seasonal polyphenism in Tamil Nadu, shifting from grey-brown in wet monsoon months to warm yellowish-ochre in dry summer broods; larvae are tended by Camponotus and Technomyrmex ants.
