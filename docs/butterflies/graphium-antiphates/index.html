Field guide notes for Papilionidae · Papilioninae (True Swallowtails & Birdwings)

Family overview (Papilionidae, Swallowtails): Large to very large, strong-flying butterflies including birdwings, roses, mimes, Mormons, jays, swordtails, and banded peacocks. Many species carry prominent hindwing tails and feed actively while fluttering their wings at flowers.

Subfamily traits (Papilioninae): Includes Graphium jays and swordtails, Pachliopta roses, Papilio swallowtails, and the Southern Birdwing (Troides minos).

Typical habitats: Forest edges, hill streams, gardens, scrub, and evergreen shola margins across Tamil Nadu.

Field photography tip: Note hindwing tail shape, body colour (red vs black), and submarginal spot patterns. Both upperwing (dorsal) and underwing (ventral) photographs help separate mimics, mimes, and closely related dark Papilio species.

Gliding cream-and-black swallowtail with slender long hindwing tails

Identification marks

Upperwing: Creamy-white with five bold black transverse bars crossing the forewing cell and inner margin, smoky grey-black wing tips, and long slender black tails edged with white.

Underwing: Translucent pale green near the forewing base, bright ochre-yellow across the hindwing outer half banded in black, and black bars showing through from above.

Lookalikes: Separated from the Spot Swordtail (Graphium nomius) by five thick wedge-shaped black costal bars that stop halfway across the forewing rather than crisp spotted bands and chained marginal dots.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Evergreen and moist deciduous hill forests, forest streams, damp gravel roads, and shaded valleys.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Western Ghats ranges of Tamil Nadu including Mudumalai, Nilgiris, Siruvani, Kallar, Anamalai, and Kalakkad-Mundanthurai.

Seasonality: Most conspicuous during pre-monsoon showers (March-June) and post-monsoon sunshine (September-December).

Flight & behaviour

Glides rapidly high among canopy crowns before circling down in numbers to mud-puddle on wet sand and mineral-rich stream edges during warm mornings.

Field note: Occasionally congregates in dozens alongside Lime Butterflies and Common Jays on wet hill-stream sandbanks.
