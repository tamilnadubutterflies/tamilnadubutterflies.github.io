Field guide notes for Lycaenidae · Theclinae (Silverlines, Oakblues, Royals, Flashes & Yamfly)

Family overview (Lycaenidae, Blues and hairstreaks): Small, jewel-like butterflies (96 checklist species) including grass blues, pierrots, lineblues, ceruleans, silverlines, oakblues, royals, flashes, and sunbeams.

Subfamily traits (Theclinae): Often tailed hairstreaks with metallic silver bars (Cigaritis), deep violet-blue upperwings (Arhopala), or swift red-and-slate flashes (Rapala).

Typical habitats: Scrub jungles, forest canopies, mistletoe-bearing trees, and rocky hilltops.

Field photography tip: Capture the hindwing tornal lobes, tail count, and arrangement of underside bands. Sharp macro shots of the hindwing underside bands and spot alignments are usually far more diagnostic than upperside blue.

Wood-brown underside blue whose females lack dorsal blue and carry two tails

Identification marks

Upperwing: Males are deep glossy violet-purple framed by broad dark brown costal and outer borders; females are uniform dull smoky-brown without any blue scaling, showing a paler brown central sheen in bright sun.

Underwing: Bark-like pale ochreous-brown to greyish-brown crossed by fine, irregular dark wavy lines, minute submarginal black dots, and a tiny silver-ringed tornal spot; males bear one short tail at vein 2 while females carry two tails at veins 2 and 3.

Lookalikes: Separated from Silver-streaked Acacia Blue (Zinaspa todara) by lacking crisp silvery-white underside streak edges and by the female's completely brown upperside without blue.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Acacia-rich thorny scrub, deciduous foothills, forest clearings, and woodland edges.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Widespread across Tamil Nadu hills and foothills including Coimbatore, Kallar, Mudumalai, Sathyamangalam, Anamalais, KMTR, Pachamalai, and Shervaroys.

Seasonality: Active almost year-round, peaking from October to March.

Flight & behaviour

Flies rapidly around thorny Acacia climbers, rests closely appressed against dry twigs and foliage where its bark-brown underside camouflages it, and feeds on small white flowers.

Field note: Represented in peninsular India by subspecies Surendra quercetorum biplagiata; larvae are tended by Crematogaster and Anoplolepis ants on young Acacia shoots.
