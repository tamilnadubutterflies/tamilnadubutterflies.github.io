Field guide notes for Lycaenidae · Polyommatinae (Grass Blues, Pierrots, Lineblues & Ceruleans)

Family overview (Lycaenidae, Blues and hairstreaks): Small, jewel-like butterflies (96 checklist species) including grass blues, pierrots, lineblues, ceruleans, silverlines, oakblues, royals, flashes, and sunbeams.

Subfamily traits (Polyommatinae): Delicate small blues with distinctive underside spot chains, zebra stripes, or white-and-black pied markings.

Typical habitats: Lawns, grasslands, damp sand, woodland edges, and hill paths across all elevations.

Field photography tip: Macro underside views showing hindwing sub-basal spots and tornal orange eyespots are essential. Sharp macro shots of the hindwing underside bands and spot alignments are usually far more diagnostic than upperside blue.

Dainty tailed blue bearing twin black spots framed in bright orange

Identification marks

Upperwing: Males are bright violet-blue with narrow dark outer borders and two small dark marginal dots near the hindwing tornus; females are dark brown with pale blue basal dusting and orange-capped dark tornal spots on the hindwing.

Underwing: Silky greyish-white marked with pale grey-brown discal dashes, two conspicuous black sub-basal dots on the hindwing, two jet-black tornal eyespots crowned together by a vivid orange-red patch in spaces 2 and 3, and a tiny hair-like tail at vein 2.

Lookalikes: Separated from Plains Cupid (Chilades pandava) and Small Cupid (Chilades parrhassius) by its silky whiter underside with faint grey discal dashes, two bold black sub-basal hindwing dots, and a single shared bright orange tornal patch.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Open grasslands, forest clearings, wetland margins, fallow fields, and mid-elevation hill meadows.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Widespread across Tamil Nadu grasslands, foothills, and hill ranges including Chennai, Coimbatore, Madurai, Salem, Nilgiris, Palani Hills, and Kalakkad-Mundanthurai.

Seasonality: Recorded year-round, most common from October to March.

Flight & behaviour

Weak low flight over creeping pasture legumes; pauses with wings closed on grass stalks and puddles occasionally on wet mud.

Field note: Represented in peninsular India by subspecies Everes lacturnus syntala; females tuck eggs deep inside tiny flower heads of creeping Fabaceae.
