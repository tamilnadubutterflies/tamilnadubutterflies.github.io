Field guide notes for Hesperiidae · Hesperiinae (Grass Skippers, Swifts, Aces & Darts)

Family overview (Hesperiidae, Skippers): Compact, fast-darting butterflies (83 checklist species) such as awls, flats, angles, aces, bobs, demons, swifts, and grass darts.

Subfamily traits (Hesperiinae): Fast-flying tawny, brown, or black-and-yellow skippers that typically hold forewings and hindwings at different angles when basking.

Typical habitats: Grasslands, paddy margins, palms, bamboo thickets, and forest undergrowth.

Field photography tip: Zoom into the underside hindwing spot pattern and forewing cell spots to distinguish swifts and darts. Photograph both the upperwing hyaline spots and the underside hindwing spot arcs, essential for distinguishing swifts and aces.

Identification marks

Upperwing: Dark brown forewing with a noticeably concave outer margin at vein 2, lacking cell spots and marked with small pale translucent spots in spaces 2-4 and 6-7 plus a small spot in space 1b in both sexes; hindwing plain dark brown.

Underwing: Dark brown heavily washed with bright golden-olive to greenish scaling across the hindwing and forewing apex; hindwing normally unmarked or showing at most one or two minute pale specks.

Lookalikes: Separated from Blank Swift (Caltoris kumara) by smaller size, concave forewing outer margin at vein 2, spot in space 1b, and striking olive-green hindwing underside; separated from Paintbrush Swift (Baoris farri) and Kanara Swift (Caltoris canaraica) by lacking forewing cell spots.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Bamboo clumps in moist forests, coastal and riverine reedbeds, foothill gardens, and humid scrub clearings.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Scattered across coastal and foothill Tamil Nadu as well as Western Ghats valleys including Siruvani, Kallar, Pollachi, Chennai fringes, and Tirunelveli.

Seasonality: Seen mainly from June to February across humid lowland and foothill tracts.

Flight & behaviour

Swift, low darting flight near bamboo and tall reeds; suns in a crisp two-angle jet-plane posture on leaf tips and feeds at small blossoms, damp sand, and bird droppings.

Field note: Represented in peninsular India by Caltoris philippina philippina; the fresh olive-green sheen beneath fades with wear, making the pinched forewing margin helpful on older individuals.
