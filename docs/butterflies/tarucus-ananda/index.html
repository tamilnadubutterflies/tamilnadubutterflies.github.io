Field guide notes for Lycaenidae · Polyommatinae (Grass Blues, Pierrots, Lineblues & Ceruleans)

Family overview (Lycaenidae, Blues and hairstreaks): Small, jewel-like butterflies (96 checklist species) including grass blues, pierrots, lineblues, ceruleans, silverlines, oakblues, royals, flashes, and sunbeams.

Subfamily traits (Polyommatinae): Delicate small blues with distinctive underside spot chains, zebra stripes, or white-and-black pied markings.

Typical habitats: Lawns, grasslands, damp sand, woodland edges, and hill paths across all elevations.

Field photography tip: Macro underside views showing hindwing sub-basal spots and tornal orange eyespots are essential. Sharp macro shots of the hindwing underside bands and spot alignments are usually far more diagnostic than upperside blue.

Woodland pierrot boldly banded in chocolate-black and tended by ants

Identification marks

Upperwing: Males are deep dark glossy purple-black with wide black margins, appearing nearly velvety black in the field; females are dark brown with small, restricted white discal patches and faint dark marginal lunules.

Underwing: Creamy white crossed by remarkably thick, heavy, solid chocolate-brown to black basal, median, and postdiscal bands, marginal dark lunules dusted near the tornus with metallic bluish-green flecks, and a thin white-tipped tail.

Lookalikes: Immediately distinguished from all pale-spotted Tamil Nadu Tarucus species by its dark blackish male upperwing and broad, solid dark brown underside bands rather than fine dots or streaks.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Moist and dry deciduous forests, shaded foothill paths, wooded streams, and hill scrub.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Widespread across Tamil Nadu hill forests and wooded foothills including Siruvani, Kallar, Mudumalai, Anamalai, Kalakkad-Mundanthurai, Shevaroys, Kolli Hills, and Pachamalai.

Seasonality: Active most of the year in forest belts, peaking between August and February.

Flight & behaviour

Keeps closer to wooded hill clearings than plains Tarucus species; males mud-puddle on wet sand and perch on sunlit understory shrubs.

Field note: Obligately myrmecophilous with Crematogaster ants, which even build carton shelters over feeding caterpillars and pupae on Ziziphus twigs; females also oviposit on Loranthus mistletoes hosting the same ants.
