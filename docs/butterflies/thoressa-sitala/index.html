Field guide notes for Hesperiidae · Hesperiinae (Grass Skippers, Swifts, Aces & Darts)

Family overview (Hesperiidae, Skippers): Compact, fast-darting butterflies (83 checklist species) such as awls, flats, angles, aces, bobs, demons, swifts, and grass darts.

Subfamily traits (Hesperiinae): Fast-flying tawny, brown, or black-and-yellow skippers that typically hold forewings and hindwings at different angles when basking.

Typical habitats: Grasslands, paddy margins, palms, bamboo thickets, and forest undergrowth.

Field photography tip: Zoom into the underside hindwing spot pattern and forewing cell spots to distinguish swifts and darts. Photograph both the upperwing hyaline spots and the underside hindwing spot arcs, essential for distinguishing swifts and aces.

Rare high-elevation Nilgiri and Palani ace clothed in rusty ochre undersides

Identification marks

Upperwing: Dark blackish-brown forewing with two small cell spots, two well-separated discal spots, and two subapical white dots; hindwing unmarked dark brown draped in long ochreous hairs, with ochreous hindwing cilia.

Underwing: Forewing apex and costal half washed rusty ferruginous-ochreous; hindwing uniformly rusty ochre-brown marked faintly with two obscure whitish median dots and faint tornal marks; forewing cilia spotted brown.

Lookalikes: Very close to Evershed's Ace (Thoressa evershedi), separated by uniformly ochreous (non-grey) hindwing cilia and a uniformly rusty-ochre under-hindwing without a distinct brown marginal band.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: High-elevation shola-grassland margins, montane evergreen forest edges, and highland bamboo breaks above 1,200 m.

Tamil Nadu distribution: High Western Ghats plateaus of Tamil Nadu including Nilgiris (Coonoor, Ooty slopes), Anamalai, and Palani Hills.

Seasonality: Mainly March to May and September to November.

Flight & behaviour

Darts swiftly along sunlit shola edges and stream clearings, basking in jet-plane posture on bamboo culms and feeding at highland Impatiens and Strobilanthes flowers.

Field note: A localized Western Ghats highland endemic first described from Ooty in the Nilgiris and much prized during mountain butterfly surveys.
