Field guide notes for Nymphalidae · Satyrinae (Browns, Bushbrowns, Treebrowns & Rings)

Family overview (Nymphalidae, Brush-footed butterflies): Tamil Nadu’s largest butterfly family (97 checklist species), encompassing tigers, crows, pansies, eggflies, sailers, barons, rajahs, browns, and Tamil Nadu’s state butterfly, the Tamil Yeoman.

Subfamily traits (Satyrinae): Shade-loving brown butterflies marked with concentric underside eyespots (ocelli) that frequently exhibit wet- and dry-season forms.

Typical habitats: Grasslands, forest understorey, bamboo groves, palm groves, and shaded gardens.

Field photography tip: Underside ocelli sizes, brand marks, and discal line shape (straight vs curved) are crucial for species ID. Record both basking upperside and resting underside views, especially for bushbrowns, treebrowns, sailers, and oakleaf butterflies.

Distinctive palmfly addition mimicking tigers along palm edges

Identification marks

Upperwing: Mainland subspecies (E. h. undularis): males are velvety black-brown with pale blue marginal spots and reddish hindwing borders; females closely mimic Striped Tiger with black-veined orange wings.

Underwing: Mottled bark-brown and cream cryptic striations camouflaging the butterfly against palm bark.

Lookalikes: Separated from Tailed Palmfly (Elymnias caudata) by rounded hindwings lacking a prominent tail projection.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Coconut groves, ornamental palm gardens, coastal parks, and humid hill orchards.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Added recently to the Tamil Nadu Checklist following validated records around Chennai, Puducherry surroundings, and Sirumalai (Dindigul district).

Seasonality: Year-round around palms.

Flight & behaviour

Shade-loving skulker around palm fronds; adults feed on tree sap and fermenting fruit juices.

Field note: One of the newest additions to the Tamil Nadu butterfly list, updating historical assumptions that restricted it north of coastal Andhra.
