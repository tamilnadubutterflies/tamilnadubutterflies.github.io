Field guide notes for Nymphalidae · Charaxinae (Rajahs & Nawabs)

Family overview (Nymphalidae, Brush-footed butterflies): Tamil Nadu’s largest butterfly family (97 checklist species), encompassing tigers, crows, pansies, eggflies, sailers, barons, rajahs, browns, and Tamil Nadu’s state butterfly, the Tamil Yeoman.

Subfamily traits (Charaxinae): Stout-bodied, exceptionally fast-flying canopy butterflies often attracted to tree sap, rotting fruit, and damp sand.

Typical habitats: Deciduous and evergreen hill forests, foothills, and wooded estates.

Field photography tip: Underside pale bands and tail length separate Common, Anomalous, and Blue Nawabs. Record both basking upperside and resting underside views, especially for bushbrowns, treebrowns, sailers, and oakleaf butterflies.

Striking sky-blue-edged white-banded nawab of dense evergreen forest canopies

Identification marks

Upperwing: Deep velvety black subtly glossed with dark indigo at the wing bases and crossed by a wide pure white median band sharply rimmed on both sides with luminous sky-blue, plus two small white apical dots and two stout hindwing tails (subspecies C. s. wardii). Females are larger with a broader white band.

Underwing: Pearly silvery-white crossed by olive-brown bands, dark fine lines, and a vivid submarginal chain of brick-red and black crescent lunules along the hindwing.

Lookalikes: Unmistakable among Tamil Nadu nawabs by the vivid sky-blue borders framing the bright white median band across black wings and the gleaming pearly underside.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Moist evergreen and semi-evergreen forest canopies, shaded mountain streams, and tall riparian corridors.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Western Ghats wet forests including Mudumalai, Gudalur, Nilgiri slopes, Siruvani, Anamalais, Kalakkad-Mundanthurai, and Kanyakumari.

Seasonality: Scarce and seasonal, recorded mostly during March-May and August-December.

Flight & behaviour

Flies powerfully high in the canopy and descends swiftly to sunlit gravel beds, carnivore droppings, rotting fruit, or tree sap along forest streams.

Field note: A prized canopy species of intact South Indian wet forests whose swift, wary flight makes close observation away from mineral puddles rare.
