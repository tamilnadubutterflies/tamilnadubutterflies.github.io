Field guide notes for Hesperiidae · Hesperiinae (Grass Skippers, Swifts, Aces & Darts)

Family overview (Hesperiidae, Skippers): Compact, fast-darting butterflies (83 checklist species) such as awls, flats, angles, aces, bobs, demons, swifts, and grass darts.

Subfamily traits (Hesperiinae): Fast-flying tawny, brown, or black-and-yellow skippers that typically hold forewings and hindwings at different angles when basking.

Typical habitats: Grasslands, paddy margins, palms, bamboo thickets, and forest undergrowth.

Field photography tip: Zoom into the underside hindwing spot pattern and forewing cell spots to distinguish swifts and darts. Photograph both the upperwing hyaline spots and the underside hindwing spot arcs, essential for distinguishing swifts and aces.

Large unbranded swift marked with creamy-yellow spots and a crisp hindwing arc

Identification marks

Upperwing: Large dark brown skipper dusted basally with golden-ochre hairs; forewing bears prominent pale yellowish-white translucent spots (two separate cell spots, a big quadrate spot in space 2, smaller spots in 3-4, three subapical dots, and a spot in space 1b in both sexes), and males lack any sex-brand; hindwing shows 2-4 faint pale dots.

Underwing: Rich ochreous-brown on the hindwing, displaying a neat white spot near the upper cell end and a clean, curved post-discal arc of uniform dark-edged white spots across spaces 2-5 (often extending faintly to space 6).

Lookalikes: Separated from all branded Tamil Nadu Pelopidas swifts (P. mathias, P. agna, P. subochracea) by larger size, yellowish-white spots, presence of a space 1b spot in both sexes, and lack of a male forewing brand; separated from Caltoris swifts by the white spotted hindwing underside with cell spot.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Moist deciduous and evergreen forest edges, bamboo clumps, tall marsh reeds, and shaded hill streams.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Western and Eastern Ghats of Tamil Nadu including Nilgiris, Siruvani, Kallar, Anamalais, Palani Hills, Kalakkad-Mundanthurai, Kolli Hills, and Yercaud.

Seasonality: Recorded throughout the year in humid hill tracts, peaking from August to December.

Flight & behaviour

Swift, heavy-bodied flier patrolling sunny forest clearings and bamboo margins; perches on tall blades in two-angle jet-plane posture and visits Chromolaena, Mussaenda, wet sand, and bird droppings.

Field note: Represented in South India by Pelopidas conjuncta narooa, whose caterpillars roll broad leaf-tubes on coarse bamboos and tall grasses.
