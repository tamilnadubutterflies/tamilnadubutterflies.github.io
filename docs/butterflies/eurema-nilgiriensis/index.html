Field guide notes for Pieridae · Coliadinae (Yellows, Emigrants & Clouded Yellows)

Family overview (Pieridae, Whites and yellows): Medium-to-small butterflies dominated by white, yellow, orange, and black pigments, ranging from garden grass yellows and emigrants to hill-dwelling clouded yellows and forest albatrosses.

Subfamily traits (Coliadinae): Yellow, pale green, or orange butterflies with rapid sustained flight and strong migratory behaviour in several species.

Typical habitats: Open grasslands, agricultural margins, scrub, riverbeds, and Nilgiri/Palani high-altitude meadows (Colias).

Field photography tip: For Eurema grass yellows, zoom into the underside forewing cell to count the small dark cell spots. Capture the closed-wing underside when possible, spot counts in Eurema and wingbar markings in Appias and Ixias are key underside clues.

Western Ghats forest grass yellow endemic to southern Indian hills

Identification marks

Upperwing: Deep golden-yellow in males (paler creamy-yellow in females) bearing a broad dark brown-black forewing border stepped inwards and a crisp black hindwing outer line.

Underwing: Bright yellow in males with two faint cell bars, rusty ring-spot on forewing cell end, and fine dark speckling across hindwing.

Lookalikes: Separated closely from One-spot Grass Yellow (E. andersoni) and Sari (E. sari) by subtly rounded hindwing margin, two small forewing cell markings, and strict hill-forest range.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Wet evergreen shola edges, shaded stream corridors, and mid-to-high elevation Western Ghats forests.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Upper Nilgiris, Mudumalai, Anamalai, and Palani Hills of Tamil Nadu.

Seasonality: Seen mainly from August to February across humid montane valleys.

Flight & behaviour

Weak, hurried flight along shaded forest streams; rarely ventures far into open urban gardens.

Field note: Described from the Nilgiri plateau (Yata, 1990) and retained on the TNBS state checklist as a localized southern hill taxon.
