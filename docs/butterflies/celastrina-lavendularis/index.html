Field guide notes for Lycaenidae · Polyommatinae (Grass Blues, Pierrots, Lineblues & Ceruleans)

Family overview (Lycaenidae, Blues and hairstreaks): Small, jewel-like butterflies (96 checklist species) including grass blues, pierrots, lineblues, ceruleans, silverlines, oakblues, royals, flashes, and sunbeams.

Subfamily traits (Polyommatinae): Delicate small blues with distinctive underside spot chains, zebra stripes, or white-and-black pied markings.

Typical habitats: Lawns, grasslands, damp sand, woodland edges, and hill paths across all elevations.

Field photography tip: Macro underside views showing hindwing sub-basal spots and tornal orange eyespots are essential. Sharp macro shots of the hindwing underside bands and spot alignments are usually far more diagnostic than upperside blue.

Rich lavender-blue montane hedge blue edged with a razor-thin border

Identification marks

Upperwing: Tailless; males are deep dark lavender-blue bordered only by a hairline black margin without white discal patches, while females are paler lavender framed by broad black-brown forewing costal and outer borders and dark hindwing submarginal spots.

Underwing: Greyish-white marked with neat, fine dark postdiscal dashes aligned smoothly on the forewing, a black sub-basal spot in hindwing space 7, and marginal spots enclosed by a sharp zigzag line.

Lookalikes: Separated from Common Hedge Blue (Acytolepis puspa) by its deep uniform lavender-blue male upperwing and thread-thin margin, and from White-disc Hedge Blue (Celatoxia albidisca) by lacking crisp white discal patches.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Mid- to high-elevation wet evergreen, shola, and moist deciduous hill forests above 600 m.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Common in higher Tamil Nadu hills including the Nilgiris, Palani Hills (Kodaikanal), Anamalais, Kalakkad-Mundanthurai, Megamalai, Shevaroys, and Kalvarayan Hills.

Seasonality: Recorded nearly year-round in hill ranges, peaking from October to March.

Flight & behaviour

Strong, rapid flight along montane stream corridors and forest edges; males gather in puddling patches on wet soil and elephant dung.

Field note: Represented in South India and Sri Lanka by subspecies Celastrina lavendularis lavenduris; detailed larval host confirmation in Tamil Nadu hills remains a worthwhile field study.

Caterpillar host plants

Fabaceae forest climbers such as Millettia and Callerya species (South Indian host awaiting formal confirmation)
