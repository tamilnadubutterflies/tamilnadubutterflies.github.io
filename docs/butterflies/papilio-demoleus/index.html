Field guide notes for Papilionidae · Papilioninae (True Swallowtails & Birdwings)

Family overview (Papilionidae, Swallowtails): Large to very large, strong-flying butterflies including birdwings, roses, mimes, Mormons, jays, swordtails, and banded peacocks. Many species carry prominent hindwing tails and feed actively while fluttering their wings at flowers.

Subfamily traits (Papilioninae): Includes Graphium jays and swordtails, Pachliopta roses, Papilio swallowtails, and the Southern Birdwing (Troides minos).

Typical habitats: Forest edges, hill streams, gardens, scrub, and evergreen shola margins across Tamil Nadu.

Field photography tip: Note hindwing tail shape, body colour (red vs black), and submarginal spot patterns. Both upperwing (dorsal) and underwing (ventral) photographs help separate mimics, mimes, and closely related dark Papilio species.

Tailless black-and-yellow swallowtail common in citrus gardens

Identification marks

Upperwing: Black wings densely patterned with pale yellow spots and bands, accented by a blue-edged eyespot near the foremargin and a brick-red spot at the inner hindwing angle.

Underwing: Similar yellow-and-black mosaic overlaid with rich tawny-orange washes in the hindwing cells.

Lookalikes: Tailless unlike most other yellow-and-black Papilio species, and lacks iridescent blue bands.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Gardens, orchards, scrub, urban avenues, and open agricultural landscapes.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Ubiquitous across plains, foothills, and open hills throughout Tamil Nadu.

Seasonality: Year-round, occasionally forming local migratory bursts (documented in Pattampoochi Q3 2018).

Flight & behaviour

Restless, fast-darting flight; settles briefly to mud-puddle on wet sand and bask with spread wings.

Field note: Young caterpillars mimic bird droppings before turning velvety green with eye-like thorax markings.
