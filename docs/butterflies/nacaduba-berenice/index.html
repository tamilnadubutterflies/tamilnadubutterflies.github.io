Field guide notes for Lycaenidae · Polyommatinae (Grass Blues, Pierrots, Lineblues & Ceruleans)

Family overview (Lycaenidae, Blues and hairstreaks): Small, jewel-like butterflies (96 checklist species) including grass blues, pierrots, lineblues, ceruleans, silverlines, oakblues, royals, flashes, and sunbeams.

Subfamily traits (Polyommatinae): Delicate small blues with distinctive underside spot chains, zebra stripes, or white-and-black pied markings.

Typical habitats: Lawns, grasslands, damp sand, woodland edges, and hill paths across all elevations.

Field photography tip: Macro underside views showing hindwing sub-basal spots and tornal orange eyespots are essential. Sharp macro shots of the hindwing underside bands and spot alignments are usually far more diagnostic than upperside blue.

Compact rounded six-lineblue with bold dark striae inside ventral bands

Identification marks

Upperwing: Noticeably rounded forewing apex and convex outer margins; males are dull dark violet-blue with fine black borders and darkened veins, while females are dark brown with restricted leaden-blue discal scaling and pale-ringed hindwing spots.

Underwing: Warm buff-brown showing a full six-lineblue pattern with forewing cell sub-basal lines, strongly etched dark internal striae within the white-bordered bands in both sexes, broad chevron-shaped submarginal spots, a bright orange-crowned black spot in space 2, and a short tail.

Lookalikes: Separated from Opaque 6-lineblue (Nacaduba beroe) and Transparent 6-lineblue (Nacaduba kurava) by its rounder wing profile, strong dark internal band striae in both sexes, and chevron-shaped submarginal lunules.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Dense evergreen and semi-evergreen foothill and mid-elevation rainforests.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Rarely documented in humid Western Ghats pockets of Tamil Nadu including Kanyakumari, Kalakkad-Mundanthurai, Anamalais, Siruvani, and Nilgiri foothills.

Seasonality: Scarce; recorded mainly during humid months from July to February.

Flight & behaviour

Flies close to understory foliage and settles quietly on damp mud or sunny leaves along narrow forest trails.

Field note: Peninsular Indian and Sri Lankan populations represent subspecies Nacaduba berenice ormistoni; field records often require sharp ventral photos to separate from Nacaduba calauria.

Caterpillar host plants

Sapindaceae trees and climbers (local South Indian larval host unconfirmed; feeds on Sapindaceae and Connaraceae shoots across its range)
