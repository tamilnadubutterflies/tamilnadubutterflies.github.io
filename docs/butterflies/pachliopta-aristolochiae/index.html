Field guide notes for Papilionidae · Papilioninae (True Swallowtails & Birdwings)

Family overview (Papilionidae, Swallowtails): Large to very large, strong-flying butterflies including birdwings, roses, mimes, Mormons, jays, swordtails, and banded peacocks. Many species carry prominent hindwing tails and feed actively while fluttering their wings at flowers.

Subfamily traits (Papilioninae): Includes Graphium jays and swordtails, Pachliopta roses, Papilio swallowtails, and the Southern Birdwing (Troides minos).

Typical habitats: Forest edges, hill streams, gardens, scrub, and evergreen shola margins across Tamil Nadu.

Field photography tip: Note hindwing tail shape, body colour (red vs black), and submarginal spot patterns. Both upperwing (dorsal) and underwing (ventral) photographs help separate mimics, mimes, and closely related dark Papilio species.

Red-bodied swallowtail with white-spotted hindwing centre

Identification marks

Upperwing: Black forewings with pale rayed veins and dark-tailed hindwings marked by a central cluster of white spots encircled by pinkish-red marginal lunules.

Underwing: Similar to upperwing; crimson thorax and abdomen clearly signal distastefulness to predators.

Lookalikes: Lacks the bold white forewing stripes of Crimson Rose and displays a white discal patch on the hindwing; lacks the red body of Common Mormon mimics.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Gardens, scrub, grasslands, agricultural edges, and forest clearings.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Widespread across plains, wetlands, and hills up to mid-elevations.

Seasonality: Present year-round across Tamil Nadu.

Flight & behaviour

Weak, unhurried flight close to nectar plants; rests with wings partly spread in morning sun.

Field note: Serves as the classic model mimicked by the 'stichius' form of the female Common Mormon (Papilio polytes).
