Field guide notes for Lycaenidae · Theclinae (Silverlines, Oakblues, Royals, Flashes & Yamfly)

Family overview (Lycaenidae, Blues and hairstreaks): Small, jewel-like butterflies (96 checklist species) including grass blues, pierrots, lineblues, ceruleans, silverlines, oakblues, royals, flashes, and sunbeams.

Subfamily traits (Theclinae): Often tailed hairstreaks with metallic silver bars (Cigaritis), deep violet-blue upperwings (Arhopala), or swift red-and-slate flashes (Rapala).

Typical habitats: Scrub jungles, forest canopies, mistletoe-bearing trees, and rocky hilltops.

Field photography tip: Capture the hindwing tornal lobes, tail count, and arrangement of underside bands. Sharp macro shots of the hindwing underside bands and spot alignments are usually far more diagnostic than upperside blue.

Fiery scarlet male hairstreak whose larvae bore inside forest seed pods

Identification marks

Upperwing: Strongly dimorphic: males are glowing scarlet-red to fiery orange-red framed by broad black forewing costal and outer margins and a black-veined red hindwing, whereas females are uniform dull coppery-brown without red patches.

Underwing: Uniform wood-brown to greyish-ochreous crossed by crisp double white-edged dark discal and submarginal lines, a prominent black tornal spot crowned with orange and edged in metallic green-blue scales, and a fine white-tipped tail at vein 2.

Lookalikes: Male's bright scarlet upperside resembles Indian Red Flash (Rapala iarbus) but Cornelian is larger, lacks the dark trident mark at forewing vein bases, and shows clean double white-edged underside lines; female lacks the orange forewing patch of female Guava Blue.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Moist deciduous, semi-evergreen, and dry evergreen hill forests, orchards, and wooded foothills.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Widespread in Tamil Nadu hill ranges including Nilgiris, Mudumalai, Kallar, Siruvani, Anamalais, Palani Hills, KMTR, Pachamalai, Sirumalai, and Shervaroys.

Seasonality: Active year-round in hill forests, common from September to March.

Flight & behaviour

Extremely fast, direct flyer that patrols high canopy clearings, hill-tops vigorously in midday sun, and visits damp sand and hilltop shrubs.

Field note: Represented in southern India by subspecies Deudorix epijarbas epijarbas; caterpillars tunnel inside tough Sapindus and Connarus fruits and pods.
