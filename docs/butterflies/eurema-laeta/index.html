Field guide notes for Pieridae · Coliadinae (Yellows, Emigrants & Clouded Yellows)

Family overview (Pieridae, Whites and yellows): Medium-to-small butterflies dominated by white, yellow, orange, and black pigments, ranging from garden grass yellows and emigrants to hill-dwelling clouded yellows and forest albatrosses.

Subfamily traits (Coliadinae): Yellow, pale green, or orange butterflies with rapid sustained flight and strong migratory behaviour in several species.

Typical habitats: Open grasslands, agricultural margins, scrub, riverbeds, and Nilgiri/Palani high-altitude meadows (Colias).

Field photography tip: For Eurema grass yellows, zoom into the underside forewing cell to count the small dark cell spots. Capture the closed-wing underside when possible, spot counts in Eurema and wingbar markings in Appias and Ixias are key underside clues.

Pointed-winged grass yellow lacking any spots in the underside forewing cell

Identification marks

Upperwing: Bright lemon-yellow with a conspicuously pointed forewing apex, black outer border ending cleanly near the lower angle, and reduced hindwing border in dry morphs.

Underwing: Dry-season individuals show warm rusty-ochre hindwings crossed by two straight dark streaks; completely lacks distinct spots inside the forewing cell (at most one faint speck).

Lookalikes: Sharp forewing apex and straight oblique dark bars across the dusty underside hindwing distinguish it from Small Grass Yellow (E. brigitta).

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Open dry scrub, hill savannas, forest clearings, and grassy hill footpaths.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Widespread in foothill scrub, Eastern Ghats slopes, Nilgiri eastern slopes, Coimbatore, and Madurai.

Seasonality: Recorded throughout the year, with sharp dry-season morphs dominating from November to April.

Flight & behaviour

Flies close to dry grass tufts during warm midday hours and settles completely closed with cryptic dry-leaf undersides tilted toward the sun.

Field note: Remarkable seasonal polyphenism led early naturalists to treat wet and dry broods as separate species.
