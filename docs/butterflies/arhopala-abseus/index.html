Field guide notes for Lycaenidae · Theclinae (Silverlines, Oakblues, Royals, Flashes & Yamfly)

Family overview (Lycaenidae, Blues and hairstreaks): Small, jewel-like butterflies (96 checklist species) including grass blues, pierrots, lineblues, ceruleans, silverlines, oakblues, royals, flashes, and sunbeams.

Subfamily traits (Theclinae): Often tailed hairstreaks with metallic silver bars (Cigaritis), deep violet-blue upperwings (Arhopala), or swift red-and-slate flashes (Rapala).

Typical habitats: Scrub jungles, forest canopies, mistletoe-bearing trees, and rocky hilltops.

Field photography tip: Capture the hindwing tornal lobes, tail count, and arrangement of underside bands. Sharp macro shots of the hindwing underside bands and spot alignments are usually far more diagnostic than upperside blue.

Triple-tailed forest oakblue bearing a stark white costal spot

Identification marks

Upperwing: Males show brilliant lustrous metallic blue with darkened veins and a broad black forewing border widening toward the apex; females are more rounded-winged with duller violet-blue central areas enclosed within wider blackish-brown margins.

Underwing: Glossy chocolate-brown suffused with pinkish-lilac sheen and heavily banded with pale-edged dark markings, diagnosed instantly by a crisp solid white costal spot in space 7 of the hindwing, metallic blue-green tornal scales, and three white-tipped tails at veins 1b, 2, and 3.

Lookalikes: Easily separated from all other Tamil Nadu Arhopala oakblues by its smaller size, triple-tailed hindwing, and bright white mid-costal spot on the hindwing underside; Many-tailed Oakblue (Thaduka multicaudata) also has three tails but lacks the white costal spot and shows broken square spots.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Shaded evergreen and semi-evergreen hill forests, moist ravines, and dense riparian undergrowth.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Moist evergreen Western Ghats pockets of Tamil Nadu including Nilgiris, Siruvani, Anamalais, Meghamalai, and Kalakkad-Mundanthurai.

Seasonality: Recorded mainly from September to April in humid hill tracts.

Flight & behaviour

Low-to-mid-storey forest skulker that flies swiftly between shaded perches, settles on fern fronds with wings tightly shut, and visits bird droppings or damp forest mud.

Field note: Represented in southern India by subspecies Arhopala abseus indicus; its short forewing vein 9 and triple-tailed hindwing set it apart anatomically from typical oakblues.
