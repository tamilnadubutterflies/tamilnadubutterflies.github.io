Field guide notes for Lycaenidae · Polyommatinae (Grass Blues, Pierrots, Lineblues & Ceruleans)

Family overview (Lycaenidae, Blues and hairstreaks): Small, jewel-like butterflies (96 checklist species) including grass blues, pierrots, lineblues, ceruleans, silverlines, oakblues, royals, flashes, and sunbeams.

Subfamily traits (Polyommatinae): Delicate small blues with distinctive underside spot chains, zebra stripes, or white-and-black pied markings.

Typical habitats: Lawns, grasslands, damp sand, woodland edges, and hill paths across all elevations.

Field photography tip: Macro underside views showing hindwing sub-basal spots and tornal orange eyespots are essential. Sharp macro shots of the hindwing underside bands and spot alignments are usually far more diagnostic than upperside blue.

Lilac-blue open-country regular carrying a midway forewing costal dot

Identification marks

Upperwing: Males are soft pale lilac-blue with a hairline dark margin and a small black spot near the hindwing tornus; females are dark brown with bright blue basal dusting, pale discal shading, and an orange-crowned black hindwing spot.

Underwing: Pale greyish-buff patterned with white-edged darker bands, a diagnostic small black costal dot situated midway between the forewing cell-end bar and the postdiscal band, two black costal spots on the hindwing, a large orange-crowned black eyespot in space 2, and a thin tail.

Lookalikes: Separated from Silver Forget-me-not (Catochrysops panormus) by the forewing costal dot placed midway between cell-end bar and postdiscal band rather than touching the band; differs from Ceruleans by discrete costal dots and buff tone.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Open grasslands, agricultural fields, fallow scrub, home gardens, and forest edges.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Abundant across plains, agricultural belts, urban wetlands, and foothills throughout all districts of Tamil Nadu.

Seasonality: Common year-round statewide.

Flight & behaviour

Brisk low flight over sunny weeds and pulse crops; frequently visits Tridax and legume blossoms and puddles on damp soil.

Field note: Caterpillars feed inside flowers and green pods of legumes and secrete sugary nectar collected by Camponotus and Oecophylla ants.
