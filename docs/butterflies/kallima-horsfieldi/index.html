Field guide notes for Nymphalidae · Nymphalinae (Pansies, Eggflies, Admirals & Oakleaf)

Family overview (Nymphalidae, Brush-footed butterflies): Tamil Nadu’s largest butterfly family (97 checklist species), encompassing tigers, crows, pansies, eggflies, sailers, barons, rajahs, browns, and Tamil Nadu’s state butterfly, the Tamil Yeoman.

Subfamily traits (Nymphalinae): Familiar, colourful butterflies featuring conspicuous eyespots, seasonal morphs, and dramatic dead-leaf underside camouflage.

Typical habitats: Gardens, wetlands, agricultural bunds, scrublands, and hill forests.

Field photography tip: In Junonia pansies and Hypolimnas eggflies, both dorsal colour patches and ventral patterns aid rapid ID. Record both basking upperside and resting underside views, especially for bushbrowns, treebrowns, sailers, and oakleaf butterflies.

Famous Western Ghats leaf mimic flashing iridescent blue in flight

Identification marks

Upperwing: Deep indigo-blue crossed diagonally by a broad pale azure-white forewing band.

Underwing: Astonishingly realistic dry-leaf disguise complete with a central leaf midrib, fungal specks, and leaf-petiole hindwing tail.

Lookalikes: Unmistakable: brilliant blue above, indistinguishable from a dead leaf when wings are snapped shut on tree bark.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Moist evergreen and semi-evergreen hill forests, stream banks, and shaded plantations.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Western Ghats stretches of Tamil Nadu (Mudumalai, Anamalai, Nilgiris, Kalakkad-Mundanthurai).

Seasonality: Most visible June–December.

Flight & behaviour

Darts swiftly between tree trunks and vanishes abruptly upon landing with folded wings to sip sap.

Field note: Protected endemic-complex leaf butterfly revered as one of nature finest examples of dead-leaf camouflage.
