Field guide notes for Lycaenidae · Polyommatinae (Grass Blues, Pierrots, Lineblues & Ceruleans)

Family overview (Lycaenidae, Blues and hairstreaks): Small, jewel-like butterflies (96 checklist species) including grass blues, pierrots, lineblues, ceruleans, silverlines, oakblues, royals, flashes, and sunbeams.

Subfamily traits (Polyommatinae): Delicate small blues with distinctive underside spot chains, zebra stripes, or white-and-black pied markings.

Typical habitats: Lawns, grasslands, damp sand, woodland edges, and hill paths across all elevations.

Field photography tip: Macro underside views showing hindwing sub-basal spots and tornal orange eyespots are essential. Sharp macro shots of the hindwing underside bands and spot alignments are usually far more diagnostic than upperside blue.

Identification marks

Upperwing: Males are bright pale lilac-blue with a narrow black border, slender cell-end streak, and occasional faint dark discal spots; females are dark brown patched boldly with white and pale blue at the bases.

Underwing: Clean white marked with discrete, rounded dark brown to black spots across basal, discal, and postdiscal series rather than fused long streaks, a submarginal row broken into neat isolated dots, metallic green-dusted tornal specks, and a fine tail.

Lookalikes: Differs from Rounded Pierrot (Tarucus nara) by broken submarginal spots instead of a solid line; closely resembles Pointed Pierrot (Tarucus indica) and often requires male genitalic verification where ranges overlap.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Dry scrublands, thorny riverbanks, fallow fields, and dry deciduous foothills.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Patchily distributed across drier plains and foothills of Tamil Nadu including Coimbatore, Erode, Salem, Madurai, Tirunelveli, and Chengalpattu scrub.

Seasonality: Recorded intermittently across dry and post-monsoon months from October to April.

Flight & behaviour

Hovers briskly around low jujube bushes and visits tiny herbaceous blossoms or damp mud edges during sunny mornings.

Field note: Basu et al. (2019) clarified Indian Tarucus taxonomy showing that spotted forms like T. callinara overlap closely with T. indica in peninsular scrub habitats and share ant-tended Ziziphus larvae.
