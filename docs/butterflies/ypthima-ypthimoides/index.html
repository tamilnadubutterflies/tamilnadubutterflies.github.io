Field guide notes for Nymphalidae · Satyrinae (Browns, Bushbrowns, Treebrowns & Rings)

Family overview (Nymphalidae, Brush-footed butterflies): Tamil Nadu’s largest butterfly family (97 checklist species), encompassing tigers, crows, pansies, eggflies, sailers, barons, rajahs, browns, and Tamil Nadu’s state butterfly, the Tamil Yeoman.

Subfamily traits (Satyrinae): Shade-loving brown butterflies marked with concentric underside eyespots (ocelli) that frequently exhibit wet- and dry-season forms.

Typical habitats: Grasslands, forest understorey, bamboo groves, palm groves, and shaded gardens.

Field photography tip: Underside ocelli sizes, brand marks, and discal line shape (straight vs curved) are crucial for species ID. Record both basking upperside and resting underside views, especially for bushbrowns, treebrowns, sailers, and oakleaf butterflies.

Dusky dark-undersided endemic ringlet of high southern Western Ghats grasslands

Identification marks

Upperwing: Uniform dark smoky brown across both wings; forewing displays a large yellow-ringed bi-pupilled subapical ocellus, while hindwing carries 2-3 small dark yellow-ringed postdiscal ocelli.

Underwing: Uniformly dark olive-brown to dusky grey-brown finely speckled with short greyish striae (lacking any stark white background); forewing repeats the bi-pupilled ocellus and hindwing carries 4-5 small neat yellow-ringed ocelli in an even curve, shrinking in dry-season adults.

Lookalikes: Separated cleanly from Nilgiri Four-ring (Ypthima chenu) by its uniformly dark striated brown hindwing underside rather than a bright white hindwing crossed by bold dark bands.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: High-altitude montane grasslands, shola margins, bracken slopes, and marsh edges above 1,400 m.

Tamil Nadu distribution: High-elevation southern Western Ghats of Tamil Nadu including Palani Hills (Kodaikanal grasslands), Anamalais (Grass Hills), Meghamalai, and upper Nilgiris.

Seasonality: Active mostly during March-June and September-December across upper mountain plateaus.

Flight & behaviour

Flies slowly over damp mountain tussocks during sunny breaks in mist, basking with open dark wings on grass stalks and visiting alpine wildflowers.

Field note: Originally described from the Palani Hills by Frederic Moore in 1881 and strictly endemic to the highest montane grasslands of the southern Western Ghats.
