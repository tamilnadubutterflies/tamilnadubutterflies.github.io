Field guide notes for Hesperiidae · Hesperiinae (Grass Skippers, Swifts, Aces & Darts)

Family overview (Hesperiidae, Skippers): Compact, fast-darting butterflies (83 checklist species) such as awls, flats, angles, aces, bobs, demons, swifts, and grass darts.

Subfamily traits (Hesperiinae): Fast-flying tawny, brown, or black-and-yellow skippers that typically hold forewings and hindwings at different angles when basking.

Typical habitats: Grasslands, paddy margins, palms, bamboo thickets, and forest undergrowth.

Field photography tip: Zoom into the underside hindwing spot pattern and forewing cell spots to distinguish swifts and darts. Photograph both the upperwing hyaline spots and the underside hindwing spot arcs, essential for distinguishing swifts and aces.

Jet-black forest skipper crossed by a broad white forewing band without subapical dots

Identification marks

Upperwing: Dark blackish-brown forewing crossed by a broad, slightly curved translucent white discal band across the cell end and spaces 1b-3 plus one small isolated white dot in space 4; subapical dots are normally absent and the hindwing is unmarked.

Underwing: Dark blackish-brown lightly dusted with lilac-grey scales along the forewing apex and outer hindwing margin; the white forewing band repeats cleanly without continuing into pale streaks along the costal margin.

Lookalikes: Separated from Restricted Demon (Notocrypta curvifascia) by lacking crisp white subapical dots in spaces 6-8 and costal extensions above the band below; distinguished from Grass Demon (Udaspes folus) by its plain unmarked dark hindwing.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Moist deciduous and evergreen hill forests, shaded stream banks, bamboo gullies, and overgrown plantation edges.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Western Ghats ranges including Nilgiris, Siruvani, Kallar, Anamalais, Palani Hills, and Kanyakumari, plus moist Eastern Ghats pockets such as Kolli Hills.

Seasonality: Recorded throughout the year in wet hills, peaking between May and December.

Flight & behaviour

Darts swiftly along shaded woodland paths and lands abruptly on ginger leaves or damp sand in a half-open basking posture; probes tubular forest flowers and feeds on bird droppings and wet gravel.

Field note: Females carry slightly wider white forewing bands than males and frequently lay single eggs on unfurling Zingiberaceae shoots in shaded ravines.
