Field guide notes for Papilionidae · Papilioninae (True Swallowtails & Birdwings)

Family overview (Papilionidae, Swallowtails): Large to very large, strong-flying butterflies including birdwings, roses, mimes, Mormons, jays, swordtails, and banded peacocks. Many species carry prominent hindwing tails and feed actively while fluttering their wings at flowers.

Subfamily traits (Papilioninae): Includes Graphium jays and swordtails, Pachliopta roses, Papilio swallowtails, and the Southern Birdwing (Troides minos).

Typical habitats: Forest edges, hill streams, gardens, scrub, and evergreen shola margins across Tamil Nadu.

Field photography tip: Note hindwing tail shape, body colour (red vs black), and submarginal spot patterns. Both upperwing (dorsal) and underwing (ventral) photographs help separate mimics, mimes, and closely related dark Papilio species.

Tailless swallowtail occurring in two distinct milkweed-mimicking forms

Identification marks

Upperwing: Two dramatic forms in both sexes: 'clytia' form is dark velvety brown with marginal arrow-shaped cream spots (mimicking Crows), while 'dissimilis' is black heavily streaked with creamy-white cell stripes (mimicking Tigers).

Underwing: Dark chocolate or streaked cream-and-black accented by a diagnostic row of bright golden-yellow submarginal spots along the hindwing outer edge.

Lookalikes: Separated instantly from true Euploea Crows and Tirumala Tigers by its six stout black swallowtail legs, rounded hindwing yellow marginal spots below, and slow fluttering swallowtail flight.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Evergreen and moist deciduous hill forests, coastal scrub groves, and wooded campuses.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Occurs in Coimbatore foothills (Kallar, Siruvani), Mudumalai, Pachamalai, Sirumalai, and coastal groves such as Chennai Guindy.

Seasonality: Seen throughout the year in forested foothills and plains pockets, peaking August to January.

Flight & behaviour

Glides leisurely at mid-canopy height and lowers to mud-puddle or feed at Ixora flowers with vibrating wings before snapping them upright.

Field note: One of India's finest Batesian mimics: when disturbed from foliage, its slow initial Danainae mimic flight abruptly switches into rapid swallowtail evasion.
