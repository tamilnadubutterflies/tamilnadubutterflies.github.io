Field guide notes for Papilionidae · Papilioninae (True Swallowtails & Birdwings)

Family overview (Papilionidae, Swallowtails): Large to very large, strong-flying butterflies including birdwings, roses, mimes, Mormons, jays, swordtails, and banded peacocks. Many species carry prominent hindwing tails and feed actively while fluttering their wings at flowers.

Subfamily traits (Papilioninae): Includes Graphium jays and swordtails, Pachliopta roses, Papilio swallowtails, and the Southern Birdwing (Troides minos).

Typical habitats: Forest edges, hill streams, gardens, scrub, and evergreen shola margins across Tamil Nadu.

Field photography tip: Note hindwing tail shape, body colour (red vs black), and submarginal spot patterns. Both upperwing (dorsal) and underwing (ventral) photographs help separate mimics, mimes, and closely related dark Papilio species.

Fast-darting emerald-spotted swallowtail of gardens and hills

Identification marks

Upperwing: Black wings spangled with dozens of bright apple-green rounded and bar-shaped spots; hindwing carries a short tail.

Underwing: Warm purplish-brown mirroring the green spots, accented with tiny red marks along the hindwing base.

Lookalikes: Spot rows are broken into discrete emerald dots and short tails rather than the continuous pale turquoise band of Common Jay or Bluebottle.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Urban gardens, avenues with False Ashoka trees, foothills, and evergreen forests.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Abundant statewide in both urban parks and hill forests.

Seasonality: Seen throughout the year across Tamil Nadu.

Flight & behaviour

Restlessly vibrates its wings even while sipping nectar from Ixora and Lantana flowers.

Field note: One of the easiest swallowtails to attract to urban gardens planted with custard apple or False Ashoka.
