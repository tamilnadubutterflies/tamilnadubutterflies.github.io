Field guide notes for Lycaenidae · Theclinae (Silverlines, Oakblues, Royals, Flashes & Yamfly)

Family overview (Lycaenidae, Blues and hairstreaks): Small, jewel-like butterflies (96 checklist species) including grass blues, pierrots, lineblues, ceruleans, silverlines, oakblues, royals, flashes, and sunbeams.

Subfamily traits (Theclinae): Often tailed hairstreaks with metallic silver bars (Cigaritis), deep violet-blue upperwings (Arhopala), or swift red-and-slate flashes (Rapala).

Typical habitats: Scrub jungles, forest canopies, mistletoe-bearing trees, and rocky hilltops.

Field photography tip: Capture the hindwing tornal lobes, tail count, and arrangement of underside bands. Sharp macro shots of the hindwing underside bands and spot alignments are usually far more diagnostic than upperside blue.

Identification marks

Upperwing: Males are rich dark purple-blue bordered by a thread-fine black line along the margins; females are lighter shining violet-blue over the inner half of both wings with broad dark brown costal and outer margins.

Underwing: Warm grey-brown to purplish-brown crossed by large chocolate-brown bands and cell spots neatly edged in pale buff, with diagnostic metallic silvery-green lines outlining the forewing cell spots and an orange-free metallic green-dusted tornal lobe beside a stout white-tipped tail.

Lookalikes: Separated cleanly from Large Oakblue (Arhopala amantes) by the shining silvery-green outlines around the forewing underside cell spots and narrower dark borders on the female; lacks the frosty forewing apex of Tamil Oakblue (Arhopala bazaloides).

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Evergreen, semi-evergreen, and moist deciduous hill forests, shaded stream corridors, and wooded plantations.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Widespread across Tamil Nadu Western Ghats (Nilgiris, Mudumalai, Kallar, Siruvani, Anamalais, Meghamalai, KMTR) and wooded Eastern Ghats hills (Shervaroys, Pachamalai, Kolli Hills).

Seasonality: Recorded nearly year-round in wet hill forests, peaking from August to February.

Flight & behaviour

Strong, rapid canopy flyer that rests in shaded undergrowth during midday heat, occasionally basking with wings half-open on sunlit tree-trunk foliage or puddling on damp sand.

Field note: Represented in southern India by subspecies Arhopala centaurus pirama; caterpillars shelter inside folded leaves guarded fiercely by weaver ants (Oecophylla smaragdina).
