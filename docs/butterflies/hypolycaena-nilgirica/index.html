Field guide notes for Lycaenidae · Theclinae (Silverlines, Oakblues, Royals, Flashes & Yamfly)

Family overview (Lycaenidae, Blues and hairstreaks): Small, jewel-like butterflies (96 checklist species) including grass blues, pierrots, lineblues, ceruleans, silverlines, oakblues, royals, flashes, and sunbeams.

Subfamily traits (Theclinae): Often tailed hairstreaks with metallic silver bars (Cigaritis), deep violet-blue upperwings (Arhopala), or swift red-and-slate flashes (Rapala).

Typical habitats: Scrub jungles, forest canopies, mistletoe-bearing trees, and rocky hilltops.

Field photography tip: Capture the hindwing tornal lobes, tail count, and arrangement of underside bands. Sharp macro shots of the hindwing underside bands and spot alignments are usually far more diagnostic than upperside blue.

Endemic dark-brown hill tit breeding on ground-dwelling Eulophia orchids

Identification marks

Upperwing: Unlike blue royals, both sexes are dark brown above: males are plain dark reddish-brown with a subtle purple-brown sheen and white-rimmed black tornal spots crowned faintly in dull orange, while females are slightly paler brown with prominent white lunules above the hindwing tornal spots.

Underwing: Creamy-white to pale silvery-grey delicately marked with fine dull orange-brown cell-end bars, thin ring-like discal lines, faint submarginal lunules, two small black tornal spots near the lobe, and two white-ciliated black tails.

Lookalikes: Instantly separated from Orchid Tit (Hypolycaena othona) by lacking blue uppersides in males and lacking the prominent black costal spot near the base of the hindwing underside.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Foothill stream edges, grassy rocky slopes, open moist deciduous woods, and seasonal orchid habitats.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Western Ghats foothills of Tamil Nadu including the Nilgiris, Amaidhi Solai near Kombai Dam in the eastern Palani foothills, Anamalais, and KMTR.

Seasonality: Seasonal and localized; most records fall between October and April around orchid flowering.

Flight & behaviour

Weak, fluttering low flyer compared to canopy royals; stays close to ground orchids and stream banks, perching on low herbs and grass stems with gentle hindwing movements.

Field note: Endemic to the Western Ghats and Sri Lanka; its Indian larval host plant, the terrestrial orchid Eulophia epidendraea, was confirmed only recently in southern Western Ghats foothills.

Caterpillar host plants

Eulophia epidendraea (Terrestrial orchid)
