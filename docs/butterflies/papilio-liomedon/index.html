Field guide notes for Papilionidae · Papilioninae (True Swallowtails & Birdwings)

Family overview (Papilionidae, Swallowtails): Large to very large, strong-flying butterflies including birdwings, roses, mimes, Mormons, jays, swordtails, and banded peacocks. Many species carry prominent hindwing tails and feed actively while fluttering their wings at flowers.

Subfamily traits (Papilioninae): Includes Graphium jays and swordtails, Pachliopta roses, Papilio swallowtails, and the Southern Birdwing (Troides minos).

Typical habitats: Forest edges, hill streams, gardens, scrub, and evergreen shola margins across Tamil Nadu.

Field photography tip: Note hindwing tail shape, body colour (red vs black), and submarginal spot patterns. Both upperwing (dorsal) and underwing (ventral) photographs help separate mimics, mimes, and closely related dark Papilio species.

Rare black-and-yellow swallowtail endemic to the Western Ghats

Identification marks

Upperwing: Dark chocolate-black crossed diagonally from forewing apex to inner hindwing by a continuous chain of bright creamy-yellow spots and long hindwing tails.

Underwing: Dark brown with matching pale yellow median band plus brick-red and blue submarginal rings on the hindwing.

Lookalikes: Unique continuous straight yellow spot-band and pronounced spatulate tails distinguish it from all other Tamil Nadu swallowtails.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Primary evergreen and moist deciduous hill forests of the Western Ghats.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Localized in Western Ghats pockets of Tamil Nadu (Anamalai, Nilgiris, Kalakkad-Mundanthurai; highlighted in Pattampoochi Q2 2022).

Seasonality: Mostly July to November around monsoon showers.

Flight & behaviour

Strong canopy flier occasionally seen descending to hill-stream clearings and damp mud.

Field note: A prized Western Ghats endemic documented by TNBS surveys.
