Field guide notes for Nymphalidae · Satyrinae (Browns, Bushbrowns, Treebrowns & Rings)

Family overview (Nymphalidae, Brush-footed butterflies): Tamil Nadu’s largest butterfly family (97 checklist species), encompassing tigers, crows, pansies, eggflies, sailers, barons, rajahs, browns, and Tamil Nadu’s state butterfly, the Tamil Yeoman.

Subfamily traits (Satyrinae): Shade-loving brown butterflies marked with concentric underside eyespots (ocelli) that frequently exhibit wet- and dry-season forms.

Typical habitats: Grasslands, forest understorey, bamboo groves, palm groves, and shaded gardens.

Field photography tip: Underside ocelli sizes, brand marks, and discal line shape (straight vs curved) are crucial for species ID. Record both basking upperside and resting underside views, especially for bushbrowns, treebrowns, sailers, and oakleaf butterflies.

Dusky montane bushbrown of southern hills with shifted tornal eyespots underneath

Identification marks

Upperwing: Uniform dark dusky brown with faint pale submarginal lines; forewing shows a modest lower dark ocellus (and occasional tiny apical dot) framed by a narrow dull ochre-brown ring, lacking bright orange-red patches.

Underwing: Warm earthy brown crossed by a very fine thread-like whitish-edged postdiscal line that fades toward the forewing costa; forewing carries up to 4 small ocelli and hindwing bears 7 ocelli with the two posterior tornal ocelli shifted noticeably outward toward the margin.

Lookalikes: Separated from Red-eye Bushbrown (Telinga adolphei) by duller upperwing eye rings, a finer postdiscal thread fading near the forewing costa, and outward-shifted tornal hindwing ocelli.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Montane evergreen understorey, shola borders, and shaded grassy hill paths.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Southern Western Ghats hill ranges of Tamil Nadu including Palani Hills, Anamalais, Meghamalai, Nilgiris, and Kalakkad-Mundanthurai.

Seasonality: Encountered mainly from March to May and September to December in higher hills.

Flight & behaviour

Low-flying shade specialist that stays close to mossy trails and forest bamboo-grass margins, settling quickly on dry twigs or damp ground.

Field note: Named after naturalist William Davison from specimens collected in the Palani Hills; endemic to the southern Western Ghats.
