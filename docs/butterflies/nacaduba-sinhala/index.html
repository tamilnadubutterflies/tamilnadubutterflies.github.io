Field guide notes for Lycaenidae · Polyommatinae (Grass Blues, Pierrots, Lineblues & Ceruleans)

Family overview (Lycaenidae, Blues and hairstreaks): Small, jewel-like butterflies (96 checklist species) including grass blues, pierrots, lineblues, ceruleans, silverlines, oakblues, royals, flashes, and sunbeams.

Subfamily traits (Polyommatinae): Delicate small blues with distinctive underside spot chains, zebra stripes, or white-and-black pied markings.

Typical habitats: Lawns, grasslands, damp sand, woodland edges, and hill paths across all elevations.

Field photography tip: Macro underside views showing hindwing sub-basal spots and tornal orange eyespots are essential. Sharp macro shots of the hindwing underside bands and spot alignments are usually far more diagnostic than upperside blue.

Pale translucent six-lineblue rediscovered in the Agasthyamalai hills

Identification marks

Upperwing: Rounded wing outline with thin, semi-translucent scaling; males are pale bright violet-blue showing faint hints of ventral bands from above, while females are dark brown with shiny blue restricted to the basal halves and a pale whitish outer forewing patch.

Underwing: Pale ashy-grey to greyish-brown patterned with six-lineblue sub-basal cell lines and broad crisp white bands formed of unusually wide ribbon scales, an orange-ringed black subtornal spot in space 2, and a slender hindwing tail.

Lookalikes: Smaller and paler ashy-grey beneath with wider ribbon-scale white bands than Transparent 6-lineblue (Nacaduba kurava); reliable separation in the Agasthyamalai complex often rests on male genitalia and life-history traits.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Low- to mid-elevation wet evergreen and moist deciduous forests along southern mountain streams.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Restricted in Tamil Nadu to the southern Western Ghats, particularly the Agasthyamalai landscape across Kalakkad-Mundanthurai, Tenkasi foothills, and Kanyakumari.

Seasonality: Recorded mainly during post-monsoon and summer-shower windows from October to May.

Flight & behaviour

Flies swiftly along forest openings and damp tracks; males puddle beside streams in loose company with other lineblues.

Field note: Mainland Indian population was described in 2021 as endemic subspecies Nacaduba sinhala ramaswamii (Ramaswami's Six-lineblue), bridging a historic biogeographic link with Sri Lanka.

Caterpillar host plants

Dimocarpus longan (Longan)
