Field guide notes for Lycaenidae · Curetinae (Sunbeams)

Family overview (Lycaenidae, Blues and hairstreaks): Small, jewel-like butterflies (96 checklist species) including grass blues, pierrots, lineblues, ceruleans, silverlines, oakblues, royals, flashes, and sunbeams.

Subfamily traits (Curetinae): Robust blues with brilliant silvery-white undersides contrasting against bright coppery-orange (males) or white (females) upperwings.

Typical habitats: Moist forests, riverbanks, and shaded forest edges where males bask high on foliage.

Field photography tip: Look at the direction of the dark underside discal line to separate Indian, Angled, and Siva Sunbeams. Sharp macro shots of the hindwing underside bands and spot alignments are usually far more diagnostic than upperside blue.

Wet-forest sunbeam distinguished by aligned hindwing underside dashes

Identification marks

Upperwing: Males glow deep fiery orange-red framed by wide dark brown borders along outer and inner margins and a smaller cell-end tooth than Curetis acuta, with rounded wing outlines; females are dark brown with bold white discal patches.

Underwing: Gleaming silvery-white patterned with fine greyish-brown postdiscal dashes and marginal specks, lacking tails; diagnostic hindwing dash in space 6 stands closely aligned with the dash in space 5.

Lookalikes: Distinguished from Angled Sunbeam (Curetis acuta) by its blunter forewing apex, rounded hindwing margin, and aligned space 5 and space 6 underside striae; separates from Indian Sunbeam (Curetis thetis) by broad dark borders on the male hindwing.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Evergreen and moist semi-evergreen hill forests, wooded valleys, and shaded stream banks.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Humid Western Ghats tracts of Tamil Nadu including Siruvani, Kallar, Nadugani, Valparai, Anamalai Tiger Reserve, Kalakkad-Mundanthurai, and Kanyakumari.

Seasonality: Most frequently recorded between June and December across wet Western Ghats slopes.

Flight & behaviour

Darts rapidly across mid-story foliage and settles upside down beneath leaves or on damp stream boulders during midday puddling.

Field note: Described by W. H. Evans in 1954 and historically overlooked as a form of Curetis thetis; larvae bear whipping tentacular defensive organs rather than ant-tended honey glands.

Caterpillar host plants

Desmodium oojeinense (syn. Ougeinia oojeinensis)
