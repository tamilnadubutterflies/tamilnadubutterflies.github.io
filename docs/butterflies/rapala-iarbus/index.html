Field guide notes for Lycaenidae · Theclinae (Silverlines, Oakblues, Royals, Flashes & Yamfly)

Family overview (Lycaenidae, Blues and hairstreaks): Small, jewel-like butterflies (96 checklist species) including grass blues, pierrots, lineblues, ceruleans, silverlines, oakblues, royals, flashes, and sunbeams.

Subfamily traits (Theclinae): Often tailed hairstreaks with metallic silver bars (Cigaritis), deep violet-blue upperwings (Arhopala), or swift red-and-slate flashes (Rapala).

Typical habitats: Scrub jungles, forest canopies, mistletoe-bearing trees, and rocky hilltops.

Field photography tip: Capture the hindwing tornal lobes, tail count, and arrangement of underside bands. Sharp macro shots of the hindwing underside bands and spot alignments are usually far more diagnostic than upperside blue.

Fiery red-and-black flash common across sunlit hedges and foothill gardens

Identification marks

Upperwing: Males are vivid scarlet-red with broad blackish-brown forewing costal and outer margins and a diagnostic dark trident-shaped mark along the bases of veins 2, 3, and 4; females are uniform dull coppery-brown.

Underwing: Warm pale ochreous-buff to greyish-stone crossed by narrow cell-end bars, a fine white-edged brown postdiscal line angled neatly above the tornus, a bright orange-crowned black tornal spot, and a white-tipped tail at vein 2.

Lookalikes: Smaller and brighter orange-red than Cornelian (Deudorix epijarbas), separated by the dark basal vein trident on the male forewing and single-edged rather than double-edged white discal line beneath; female lacks the orange forewing spot of Guava Blue.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Open scrub, home gardens, urban hedges, forest edges, and deciduous foothills.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Statewide across Tamil Nadu from Chennai, Coimbatore, Trichy, and Madurai to Kallar, Mudumalai, Pachamalai, Sirumalai, and Yercaud.

Seasonality: Common year-round across plains and hills of Tamil Nadu.

Flight & behaviour

Very swift darting flight; perches aggressively on sunlit outer leaves, visits Lantana and Tridax flowers, and twists its hindwings slowly upon landing.

Field note: Subspecies Rapala iarbus sorya is the commonest red flash in Tamil Nadu; black-and-white banded legs and neat false-head tornal eyespots readily distinguish settled adults.
