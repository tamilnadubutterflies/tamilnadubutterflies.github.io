Field guide notes for Hesperiidae · Hesperiinae (Grass Skippers, Swifts, Aces & Darts)

Family overview (Hesperiidae, Skippers): Compact, fast-darting butterflies (83 checklist species) such as awls, flats, angles, aces, bobs, demons, swifts, and grass darts.

Subfamily traits (Hesperiinae): Fast-flying tawny, brown, or black-and-yellow skippers that typically hold forewings and hindwings at different angles when basking.

Typical habitats: Grasslands, paddy margins, palms, bamboo thickets, and forest undergrowth.

Field photography tip: Zoom into the underside hindwing spot pattern and forewing cell spots to distinguish swifts and darts. Photograph both the upperwing hyaline spots and the underside hindwing spot arcs, essential for distinguishing swifts and aces.

Identification marks

Upperwing: Larger, longer-winged dark brown dart with bright golden-orange bands; forewing displays a nearly continuous orange discal band with spots 4-5 overlapping above and below, an orange-filled base in space 3 in males, and clean orange veins; hindwing bears a broad orange band and orange-tipped abdomen.

Underwing: Bright golden-ochre across the hindwing, displaying a broad clean golden discal band edged by dark brown speckles and clean undarkened veins; forewing dark basal shading ends cleanly before the bright discal band.

Lookalikes: Larger and longer-winged than Indian Dart (Potanthus pseudomaesa) and Palni Dart (Potanthus palnia), separated from Chinese Dart (Potanthus confucius) by the male's orange base in forewing space 3 and broader bright ochreous hindwing underside; smaller than Pale Palm-Dart (Telicota colon) and lacking a brand.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Moist deciduous and evergreen forest margins, shaded bamboo corridors, and hill stream valleys.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Western Ghats hills and moist valleys of Tamil Nadu including Nilgiris, Anamalais, Siruvani, and Kalakkad-Mundanthurai.

Seasonality: Uncommon; recorded mainly from July to January across humid forest valleys.

Flight & behaviour

Strong, rapid flier patrolling tall grass and bamboo edges; perches alertly in jet-plane posture on sunlit shrubs and visits Lantana, Chromolaena, and wet sand.

Field note: Represented in peninsular India by Potanthus pava pava; its elongated forewing shape and male orange base in space 3 provide helpful visual cues in the field.
