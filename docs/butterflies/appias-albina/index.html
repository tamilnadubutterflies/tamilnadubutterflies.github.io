Field guide notes for Pieridae · Pierinae (Whites, Orange-tips & Jezebels)

Family overview (Pieridae, Whites and yellows): Medium-to-small butterflies dominated by white, yellow, orange, and black pigments, ranging from garden grass yellows and emigrants to hill-dwelling clouded yellows and forest albatrosses.

Subfamily traits (Pierinae): Includes Delias jezebels, Cepora gulls, Appias albatrosses/puffins, Colotis/Ixias orange-tips, Hebomoia, and Prioneris.

Typical habitats: Spans dry scrub plains (Colotis), urban avenues (Delias), and wet evergreen hills (Appias, Prioneris).

Field photography tip: Capture forewing tip markings and underside hindwing vein patterns. Capture the closed-wing underside when possible, spot counts in Eurema and wingbar markings in Appias and Ixias are key underside clues.

Clean white forest pierid famous for hill-foothill seasonal flights

Identification marks

Upperwing: Pure white in males with faintly dusted black forewing tips; females may be white or yellow with broader dark margins carrying white spots.

Underwing: Unmarked satiny white or pale butter-yellow lacking prominent dark submarginal bands.

Lookalikes: More pointed forewings than Common Gull and lacks the bold dark underside border of Chocolate Albatross.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Semi-evergreen and deciduous hill forests, stream corridors, and dry-to-moist slopes.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Recorded across Mudumalai, Siruvani, Kallar, and abundantly across Pachamalai Hills.

Seasonality: Year-round in hills; abundant across Pachamalai Hills and Western Ghats streams.

Flight & behaviour

Swifts along forest trails and forms dense white puddles on wet sandy stream banks.

Field note: Frequently joins large seasonal Albatross mud-puddling and foothills dispersal flights in western Tamil Nadu.
