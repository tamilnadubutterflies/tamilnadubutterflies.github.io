Field guide notes for Nymphalidae · Satyrinae (Browns, Bushbrowns, Treebrowns & Rings)

Family overview (Nymphalidae, Brush-footed butterflies): Tamil Nadu’s largest butterfly family (97 checklist species), encompassing tigers, crows, pansies, eggflies, sailers, barons, rajahs, browns, and Tamil Nadu’s state butterfly, the Tamil Yeoman.

Subfamily traits (Satyrinae): Shade-loving brown butterflies marked with concentric underside eyespots (ocelli) that frequently exhibit wet- and dry-season forms.

Typical habitats: Grasslands, forest understorey, bamboo groves, palm groves, and shaded gardens.

Field photography tip: Underside ocelli sizes, brand marks, and discal line shape (straight vs curved) are crucial for species ID. Record both basking upperside and resting underside views, especially for bushbrowns, treebrowns, sailers, and oakleaf butterflies.

Tiny Western Ghats grass ringlet separated from Common Five-ring by lacking a male brand

Identification marks

Upperwing: Small, delicate greyish-brown butterfly; forewing carries one crisp yellow-ringed bi-pupilled subapical ocellus and hindwing shows 2-3 small submarginal ocelli. Crucially, males lack the dark velvety forewing scent brand.

Underwing: Pale ochreous-grey finely covered in brown striations and faint discal shading; forewing has one large subapical ocellus and hindwing carries 6 small ocelli arranged cleanly in three pairs (two apical, two median, and a fused tornal pair), reduced to tiny dots in dry-season forms.

Lookalikes: Closely mimics Common Five-ring (Ypthima baldus), but reliably separated by its noticeably smaller wingspan, paler ground tone, and complete absence of a male forewing scent brand.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Shaded grassy forest clearings, bamboo-edge turf, and moist Western Ghats stream banks.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Western Ghats forests and foothills of Tamil Nadu including Nilgiris, Mudumalai, Gudalur, Siruvani, Anamalais, and Kalakkad-Mundanthurai.

Seasonality: Recorded mainly during June-December monsoon and post-monsoon months, with dry forms lingering to March.

Flight & behaviour

Flutters weakly inches above damp forest grass and weed borders, disappearing quickly into low herb tangles when disturbed.

Field note: Endemic to the Western Ghats and long treated as a southern subspecies of Ypthima philomela before being recognized as a distinct species.
