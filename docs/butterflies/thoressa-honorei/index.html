Field guide notes for Hesperiidae · Hesperiinae (Grass Skippers, Swifts, Aces & Darts)

Family overview (Hesperiidae, Skippers): Compact, fast-darting butterflies (83 checklist species) such as awls, flats, angles, aces, bobs, demons, swifts, and grass darts.

Subfamily traits (Hesperiinae): Fast-flying tawny, brown, or black-and-yellow skippers that typically hold forewings and hindwings at different angles when basking.

Typical habitats: Grasslands, paddy margins, palms, bamboo thickets, and forest undergrowth.

Field photography tip: Zoom into the underside hindwing spot pattern and forewing cell spots to distinguish swifts and darts. Photograph both the upperwing hyaline spots and the underside hindwing spot arcs, essential for distinguishing swifts and aces.

Endemic bright orange-patched ace lighting up southern Western Ghats bamboo hills

Identification marks

Upperwing: Dark dusky brown with golden-yellow basal hair streaks, a large rhomboidal cell spot, elongated median and subapical spots on the forewing, and a vivid large orange-yellow discal patch enclosing a dark dot on the hindwing.

Underwing: Forewing blackish with broad ochre-yellow apex and outer margin; hindwing bright warm ochre-yellow throughout, dotted neatly with a black cell-end spot and curved postdiscal dark dots.

Lookalikes: Large bright orange-yellow discal patch on the upper hindwing and bright yellow dotted under-hindwing distinguish it instantly from all other Tamil Nadu Thoressa and Halpe aces.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Evergreen and moist deciduous hill forests with thick bamboo and reed undergrowth.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Nilgiris, Mudumalai, Siruvani, Kovai Kutralam, Anamalai, Palani Hills, Meghamalai, and Kalakkad-Mundanthurai.

Seasonality: Recorded nearly year-round in wet hills, peaking May-June and September-December.

Flight & behaviour

Strong darting flier that perches prominently on sunlit bamboo tips in jet-plane posture, visits Lantana and Strobilanthes blooms, and puddles on wet forest roads.

Field note: First described from the Palani and Nilgiri region in the nineteenth century; the most brightly coloured member of the endemic South Indian Thoressa group.
