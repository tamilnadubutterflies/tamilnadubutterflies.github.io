Field guide notes for Lycaenidae · Polyommatinae (Grass Blues, Pierrots, Lineblues & Ceruleans)

Family overview (Lycaenidae, Blues and hairstreaks): Small, jewel-like butterflies (96 checklist species) including grass blues, pierrots, lineblues, ceruleans, silverlines, oakblues, royals, flashes, and sunbeams.

Subfamily traits (Polyommatinae): Delicate small blues with distinctive underside spot chains, zebra stripes, or white-and-black pied markings.

Typical habitats: Lawns, grasslands, damp sand, woodland edges, and hill paths across all elevations.

Field photography tip: Macro underside views showing hindwing sub-basal spots and tornal orange eyespots are essential. Sharp macro shots of the hindwing underside bands and spot alignments are usually far more diagnostic than upperside blue.

Sharp-winged blue marked by a dark basal spot on the hindwing underside

Identification marks

Upperwing: Males shine brighter, paler lilac-blue with a hairline dark margin, pointed forewing apex, and acutely angled hindwing tornus; females are greyish-brown with pale blue basal suffusion and faint hindwing marginal spots.

Underwing: Buff-grey to pale brown with broken, staggered white-edged discal bands forming a Y-like fork at forewing vein 3, a diagnostic dark white-ringed basal spot in hindwing space 7, an orange-crowned black spot in space 2, and fine ciliary tufts rather than a tail.

Lookalikes: Readily told from Common Ciliate Blue (Anthene emolus) by the acute forewing tip, broken forewing discal band, and prominent dark basal spot touching vein 7 on the hindwing underside.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Dry and moist deciduous forests, foothills, scrub edges, and wooded riverbanks.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Widespread across Tamil Nadu hills and wooded foothills including Kallar, Siruvani, Mudumalai, Anamalai, Kalakkad-Mundanthurai, Shevaroys, Kolli Hills, Pachamalai, and Gingee.

Seasonality: Active throughout much of the year, peaking during dry-to-wet transition months from March to November.

Flight & behaviour

Darts energetically around flowering forest canopies and settles rapidly on wet gravel or bird droppings along sunny forest trails.

Field note: Larvae secrete honeydew from a dorsal nectary organ and are fiercely guarded by Weaver Ants (Oecophylla smaragdina) and Camponotus ants on tender shoots.
