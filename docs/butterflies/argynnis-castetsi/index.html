Field guide notes for Nymphalidae · Heliconiinae (Fritillaries, Lacewings, Leopards, Coster & Yeoman)

Family overview (Nymphalidae, Brush-footed butterflies): Tamil Nadu’s largest butterfly family (97 checklist species), encompassing tigers, crows, pansies, eggflies, sailers, barons, rajahs, browns, and Tamil Nadu’s state butterfly, the Tamil Yeoman.

Subfamily traits (Heliconiinae): Bright orange-tawny butterflies with dark spots, serrated margins, or pearlescent underside bands, including Tamil Nadu’s state butterfly, Tamil Yeoman.

Typical habitats: Sunny clearings, grasslands, montane sholas, and forest paths.

Field photography tip: Compare hindwing underside bands and marginal scalloping to separate similar orange species. Record both basking upperside and resting underside views, especially for bushbrowns, treebrowns, sailers, and oakleaf butterflies.

Endemic Palni Hills shola fritillary with non-mimetic orange females

Identification marks

Upperwing: Rich tawny-orange thickly marked with rounded black spots, sinuous submarginal lines, and dark borders; unlike northern Indian Fritillary populations, females lack a darkened white-banded forewing apex and look nearly identical to males, occasionally showing only a faint pale subapical trace.

Underwing: Pale pinkish-amber across the forewing disc shading into olive-ochre at the tip, paired with a mossy olive-buff hindwing marbled with bold silvery-white dark-rimmed spots and silvery marginal lunules.

Lookalikes: Distinguished from Indian/Nilgiri Fritillary (Argynnis hybrida) by non-mimetic orange-toned females lacking a stark black-and-white forewing tip and its strict distribution south of the Palghat Gap; far larger with silvery hindwing spots underneath than Phalanta leopards.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Montane shola-grassland mosaics, grassy hill slopes, and damp forest clearings above 1,500 m.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Endemic to Western Ghats ranges south of the Palghat Gap, notably Kodaikanal and the Palni Hills, Anamalai Grass Hills, and Meghamalai.

Seasonality: Most frequently seen during dry sunny intervals from February to May and September to December.

Flight & behaviour

Flies rapidly and low over montane grasses, pausing on wild bramble, Strobilanthes, and daisy-like shola flowers in bright sun.

Field note: Described by Oberthur (1891) from the Palni Hills; its loss of female Plain Tiger mimicry south of the Palghat Gap makes it a classic study species in Western Ghats biogeography.
