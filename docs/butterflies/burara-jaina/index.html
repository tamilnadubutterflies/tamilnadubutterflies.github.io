Field guide notes for Hesperiidae · Coeliadinae (Awls, Awlets & Awlkings)

Family overview (Hesperiidae, Skippers): Compact, fast-darting butterflies (83 checklist species) such as awls, flats, angles, aces, bobs, demons, swifts, and grass darts.

Subfamily traits (Coeliadinae): Large, swift skippers active particularly at dawn, dusk, and shaded forest interiors; several possess metallic-green or bright orange markings.

Typical habitats: Dense hill forests, bamboo patches, shaded ravines, and coastal scrub.

Field photography tip: Focus on the underside hindwing white/blue bands and dark tornal spots. Photograph both the upperwing hyaline spots and the underside hindwing spot arcs, essential for distinguishing swifts and aces.

Identification marks

Upperwing: Deep purplish-brown marked by a bright orange costal streak from the forewing base toward mid-wing, bluish-grey basal hairs, and glowing orange-red hindwing cilia.

Underwing: Rich reddish-brown with pale orange-ochre streaks radiating along the hindwing veins, a large pale ochreous cell spot, and bright orange-tinged palpi and thorax front.

Lookalikes: Separated from Pale Green Awlet (Burara gomata) by its orange forewing costal streak above and orange-ochre vein streaks below rather than pale greyish-green interspace stripes; larger and broader-winged than Bibasis sena.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Evergreen and moist deciduous hill forests, shaded stream valleys, and dense woody climber tangles.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Nilgiris, Anamalai Hills, Siruvani, Kallar, Meghamalai, and Kalakkad-Mundanthurai in the Western Ghats.

Seasonality: Active primarily from June to January around monsoon and winter rains.

Flight & behaviour

Strictly crepuscular, launching fast powerful flights at first light and dusk to nectar at tall forest trees, then hiding upright beneath leaves with closed wings by midday.

Field note: South Indian populations belong to subspecies Burara jaina fergusonii; adults sometimes feed on damp sand or bird droppings at dawn.
