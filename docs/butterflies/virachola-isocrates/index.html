Field guide notes for Lycaenidae · Theclinae (Silverlines, Oakblues, Royals, Flashes & Yamfly)

Family overview (Lycaenidae, Blues and hairstreaks): Small, jewel-like butterflies (96 checklist species) including grass blues, pierrots, lineblues, ceruleans, silverlines, oakblues, royals, flashes, and sunbeams.

Subfamily traits (Theclinae): Often tailed hairstreaks with metallic silver bars (Cigaritis), deep violet-blue upperwings (Arhopala), or swift red-and-slate flashes (Rapala).

Typical habitats: Scrub jungles, forest canopies, mistletoe-bearing trees, and rocky hilltops.

Field photography tip: Capture the hindwing tornal lobes, tail count, and arrangement of underside bands. Sharp macro shots of the hindwing underside bands and spot alignments are usually far more diagnostic than upperside blue.

Identification marks

Upperwing: Males are dark smoky brown glossed brilliantly with deep violet-blue in angled light and show at most a faint diffuse orange spot beyond the forewing cell; females are brown with a conspicuous bright ochreous-orange discal patch beyond the cell.

Underwing: Vinous grey-brown crossed by faint dark cell-end bars and an irregular pale-edged darker discal band, a large black tornal spot ringed in orange beside metallic blue scales on the anal lobe, and a fine white-tipped tail at vein 2.

Lookalikes: Smaller than Large Guava Blue (Virachola perse), lacking the large black subbasal spot near the hindwing costa underside and lacking sky-blue patches on the female upperside.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Orchards, home gardens, scrub plains, dry deciduous woodlands, and foothill farms.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Common throughout Tamil Nadu plains, orchards, and hills from Chennai, Salem, Coimbatore, and Madurai to Sirumalai, Pachamalai, and Western Ghats foothills.

Seasonality: Active year-round statewide, peaking from August to March.

Flight & behaviour

Strong, fast flier; adults nectar on garden flowers in morning sun while females hover intently around developing guava and pomegranate fruits to oviposit.

Field note: Caterpillars bore inside ripening fruits and plug the entry hole with their hardened anal shield to keep out predators while tying the fruit stalk with silk so it does not drop prematurely.
