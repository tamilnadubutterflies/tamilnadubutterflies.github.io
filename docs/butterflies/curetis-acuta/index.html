Field guide notes for Lycaenidae · Curetinae (Sunbeams)

Family overview (Lycaenidae, Blues and hairstreaks): Small, jewel-like butterflies (96 checklist species) including grass blues, pierrots, lineblues, ceruleans, silverlines, oakblues, royals, flashes, and sunbeams.

Subfamily traits (Curetinae): Robust blues with brilliant silvery-white undersides contrasting against bright coppery-orange (males) or white (females) upperwings.

Typical habitats: Moist forests, riverbanks, and shaded forest edges where males bask high on foliage.

Field photography tip: Look at the direction of the dark underside discal line to separate Indian, Angled, and Siva Sunbeams. Sharp macro shots of the hindwing underside bands and spot alignments are usually far more diagnostic than upperside blue.

Angled copper-and-silver sunbeam of deciduous canopies and hill streams

Identification marks

Upperwing: Males are bright coppery-orange with wide black margins, an acutely pointed forewing apex, and a prominent black tooth jutting across the forewing cell-end; females are dark brown with large clean white central patches on both wings.

Underwing: Satiny silver-white on both sexes, lightly traced with faint grey-brown postdiscal dashes and marginal dots where the hindwing stria in space 6 is shifted inward out of line with space 5; tailless with a subtly angled hindwing termen.

Lookalikes: Separated from Indian Sunbeam (Curetis thetis) by the sharper forewing apex, broad black hindwing borders, and strong black cell-end tooth; differs from Siva Sunbeam (Curetis siva) by the inward-dislocated hindwing underside dash in space 6.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Moist and dry deciduous hill forests, riparian corridors, bamboo brakes, and shaded forest edges.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Western and Eastern Ghats ranges of Tamil Nadu including Kallar, Siruvani, Mudumalai, Anamalai, Kalakkad-Mundanthurai, Kolli Hills, Shevaroys, and Pachamalai.

Seasonality: Recorded almost year-round in hill forests, peaking from August to January after rains.

Flight & behaviour

Flies swiftly along sunlit forest canopies and stream corridors; males descend eagerly to mud-puddle on wet sand and rest with wings tightly clamped shut like pale leaves, opening briefly to bask.

Field note: Unlike Polyommatinae blues, Curetis caterpillars lack ant-appeasing dorsal nectary organs and instead deter predators using twin tall cylindrical tubercles on segment 11 that evert rapidly when disturbed.
