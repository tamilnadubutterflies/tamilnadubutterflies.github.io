Field guide notes for Nymphalidae · Satyrinae (Browns, Bushbrowns, Treebrowns & Rings)

Family overview (Nymphalidae, Brush-footed butterflies): Tamil Nadu’s largest butterfly family (97 checklist species), encompassing tigers, crows, pansies, eggflies, sailers, barons, rajahs, browns, and Tamil Nadu’s state butterfly, the Tamil Yeoman.

Subfamily traits (Satyrinae): Shade-loving brown butterflies marked with concentric underside eyespots (ocelli) that frequently exhibit wet- and dry-season forms.

Typical habitats: Grasslands, forest understorey, bamboo groves, palm groves, and shaded gardens.

Field photography tip: Underside ocelli sizes, brand marks, and discal line shape (straight vs curved) are crucial for species ID. Record both basking upperside and resting underside views, especially for bushbrowns, treebrowns, sailers, and oakleaf butterflies.

Lowland southern bushbrown carrying a long broad nacreous-edged forewing brand

Identification marks

Upperwing: Dark earthy brown with rounder forewing apex than M. visala and one crisp yellow-ringed lower ocellus in space 2 (occasionally 1-2 faint spots on the hindwing).

Underwing: Wet-season form bears a distinct narrow whitish discal band and submarginal ringed ocelli (2 on forewing, 7 on hindwing) with the posterior four aligned straight; males carry a notably long, broad pale brown scent brand set inside a pearly nacreous patch reaching (but not passing) the discal line, while dry-season morphs show cryptic striae and pinpoint spots.

Lookalikes: Separated from Long-brand Bushbrown (Mycalesis visala) by its rounder forewing apex and broader, nacreous-enclosed male brand stopping at the discal line, and from Mycalesis mineus by the much longer male brand.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Coastal scrublands, dry evergreen patches, lowland grassy groves, and lower foothills.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Plains, coast, and low foothills of Tamil Nadu including Chennai, Vandalur, Chengalpattu, Madurai, Coimbatore, Salem, and Tirunelveli.

Seasonality: Recorded nearly year-round in lowland southern tracts, peaking between September and January.

Flight & behaviour

Low, fluttering grass-dweller active along shaded hedgerows, scrub borders, and fallen leaf litter, feeding on ripe fruit and damp patches.

Field note: Restricted to southern India and Sri Lanka; sometimes treated historically as a southern subspecies of Mycalesis visala before detailed brand and genital comparisons separated it.
