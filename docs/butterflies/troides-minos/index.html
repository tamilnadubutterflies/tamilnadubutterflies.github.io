Field guide notes for Papilionidae · Papilioninae (True Swallowtails & Birdwings)

Family overview (Papilionidae, Swallowtails): Large to very large, strong-flying butterflies including birdwings, roses, mimes, Mormons, jays, swordtails, and banded peacocks. Many species carry prominent hindwing tails and feed actively while fluttering their wings at flowers.

Subfamily traits (Papilioninae): Includes Graphium jays and swordtails, Pachliopta roses, Papilio swallowtails, and the Southern Birdwing (Troides minos).

Typical habitats: Forest edges, hill streams, gardens, scrub, and evergreen shola margins across Tamil Nadu.

Field photography tip: Note hindwing tail shape, body colour (red vs black), and submarginal spot patterns. Both upperwing (dorsal) and underwing (ventral) photographs help separate mimics, mimes, and closely related dark Papilio species.

Largest butterfly of southern India and a Western Ghats endemic

Identification marks

Upperwing: Vast velvety black forewings outlined with pale grey vein streaks paired with brilliant golden-yellow hindwings bordered in black scalloped patches.

Underwing: Mirrors the dramatic black-and-gold upperwing pattern, with golden-yellow abdominal segments and scarlet-red hair tufts on the thorax.

Lookalikes: Unmistakable in Tamil Nadu by sheer wingspan and soaring canopy flight; females carry larger black triangular hindwing spots than males.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Evergreen and moist deciduous hill forests, foothills, coffee and cardamom estates, and forested valleys.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Western Ghats stretches of Tamil Nadu including Nilgiris, Mudumalai, Siruvani, Anamalai, Kodaikanal, and Kanyakumari.

Seasonality: Seen year-round in wet hill forests, peaking between June and December.

Flight & behaviour

Soars effortlessly high in the forest canopy and descends early in the morning to feed on Mussaenda, Ixora, and Lantana blooms.

Field note: Also known as the Sahyadri Birdwing; Tamil Nadu’s largest butterfly species.
