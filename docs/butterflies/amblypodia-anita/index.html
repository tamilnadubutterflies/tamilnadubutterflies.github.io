Field guide notes for Lycaenidae · Theclinae (Silverlines, Oakblues, Royals, Flashes & Yamfly)

Family overview (Lycaenidae, Blues and hairstreaks): Small, jewel-like butterflies (96 checklist species) including grass blues, pierrots, lineblues, ceruleans, silverlines, oakblues, royals, flashes, and sunbeams.

Subfamily traits (Theclinae): Often tailed hairstreaks with metallic silver bars (Cigaritis), deep violet-blue upperwings (Arhopala), or swift red-and-slate flashes (Rapala).

Typical habitats: Scrub jungles, forest canopies, mistletoe-bearing trees, and rocky hilltops.

Field photography tip: Capture the hindwing tornal lobes, tail count, and arrangement of underside bands. Sharp macro shots of the hindwing underside bands and spot alignments are usually far more diagnostic than upperside blue.

Hooked-tip purple hairstreak that closes into a dead brown leaf

Identification marks

Upperwing: Males are deep rich royal purple bordered narrowly in black along the forewing outer margin and broadly along the hindwing costa with a dull red anal lobe; females are duller brown with bright purplish-blue patches confined to the forewing cell and disc.

Underwing: Coppery rufous-brown to purplish-ochreous crossed cleanly from forewing apex to hindwing inner margin by a dark midrib-like discal line, giving resting individuals uncanny resemblance to a fallen dry leaf, with a blunt single tail and short tornal lobe.

Lookalikes: Its strongly hooked forewing apex, single broad blunt tail, and dry-leaf underside with a continuous midrib line separate it easily from all Arhopala oakblues and Yamfly (Loxura atymnus).

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Dry evergreen forests, thorny scrub woodlands, coastal sacred groves, and moist deciduous foothills.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Wide range from coastal Tamil Nadu scrub (Chennai, Guindy, Point Calimere) through interior plains to Western and Eastern Ghats foothills (Kallar, Sathyamangalam, Pachamalai, Sirumalai).

Seasonality: Present across most of the year, especially common from September to March.

Flight & behaviour

Rapid, erratic flyer that settles suddenly on twigs or dry foliage with wings tightly shut, turning sideways to hide its silhouette like a withered leaf; also mud-puddles on damp sand.

Field note: Subspecies Amblypodia anita dina breeds on climbing Olax shrubs; caterpillars secrete honeydew attracting attending ants along thorny stems.

Caterpillar host plants

Olax scandensOlax imbricataOlax wightiana
