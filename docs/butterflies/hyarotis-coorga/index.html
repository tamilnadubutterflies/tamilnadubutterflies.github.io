Field guide notes for Hesperiidae · Hesperiinae (Grass Skippers, Swifts, Aces & Darts)

Family overview (Hesperiidae, Skippers): Compact, fast-darting butterflies (83 checklist species) such as awls, flats, angles, aces, bobs, demons, swifts, and grass darts.

Subfamily traits (Hesperiinae): Fast-flying tawny, brown, or black-and-yellow skippers that typically hold forewings and hindwings at different angles when basking.

Typical habitats: Grasslands, paddy margins, palms, bamboo thickets, and forest undergrowth.

Field photography tip: Zoom into the underside hindwing spot pattern and forewing cell spots to distinguish swifts and darts. Photograph both the upperwing hyaline spots and the underside hindwing spot arcs, essential for distinguishing swifts and aces.

Identification marks

Upperwing: Deep blackish-brown forewing bearing small, discrete semi-translucent white spots (a narrow cell spot, small spots in spaces 2 and 3 separated by dark-scaled veins, and minute subapical dots); hindwing uniformly dark brown with uncheckered fringes.

Underwing: Dark purplish-brown washed subtly paler toward the outer margins, lacking crisp white lunular bands; hindwing carries only faint dark post-discal shadows, while males possess a dark hair brush on the hind tibia.

Lookalikes: Separated from Tree Flitter (Hyarotis adrastus) by smaller vein-separated forewing spots, uncheckered dark fringes, and an unmarked dark brown hindwing underside; separated from Golden Flitter (Quedara basiflava) by lacking a bright yellow basal hindwing patch below.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Primary wet evergreen hill forests, shaded cane brakes, and mid-elevation stream gorges of the Western Ghats.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Restricted Western Ghats evergreen belts of Tamil Nadu, notably Anamalais (Valparai and Top Slip), Nilgiri slopes, and southern hill tracts.

Seasonality: Rare and localized, recorded mainly between September and April across post-monsoon and early dry months.

Flight & behaviour

Elusive shade-lover that darts rapidly down dim stream corridors and rests under broad leaves or on damp twigs; adults visit Leea indica flowers and occasionally puddle at wet soil.

Field note: Originally described from Kodagu and long treated as a subspecies of Hyarotis microstictum, this Western Ghats endemic is now recognized as a full species.

Caterpillar host plants

Calamus species (Forest rattans)
