Field guide notes for Nymphalidae · Satyrinae (Browns, Bushbrowns, Treebrowns & Rings)

Family overview (Nymphalidae, Brush-footed butterflies): Tamil Nadu’s largest butterfly family (97 checklist species), encompassing tigers, crows, pansies, eggflies, sailers, barons, rajahs, browns, and Tamil Nadu’s state butterfly, the Tamil Yeoman.

Subfamily traits (Satyrinae): Shade-loving brown butterflies marked with concentric underside eyespots (ocelli) that frequently exhibit wet- and dry-season forms.

Typical habitats: Grasslands, forest understorey, bamboo groves, palm groves, and shaded gardens.

Field photography tip: Underside ocelli sizes, brand marks, and discal line shape (straight vs curved) are crucial for species ID. Record both basking upperside and resting underside views, especially for bushbrowns, treebrowns, sailers, and oakleaf butterflies.

Identification marks

Upperwing: Males are uniform rich glossy brown with slight golden-olive sheen and two tiny pale apical dots; females are warm brown with a broken oblique white forewing band, an isolated white bar in space 2, and dark submarginal hindwing spots.

Underwing: Warm brown suffused with pale lilac lines; forewing shows an incurved row of 4-5 blind dark spots, while the hindwing carries 6 yellow-ringed black ocelli in which the apical (space 6) and subtornal (space 2) eyespots are markedly larger than the others.

Lookalikes: Separated from Common Treebrown (Lethe rohria) by having two enlarged hindwing underside ocelli (apical and subtornal) rather than a single oversized apical ocellus, plus lilac-bordered discal lines.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Moist evergreen and semi-evergreen hill forests, shaded bamboo ravines, and humid estate edges.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Western and southern hill ranges including Nilgiris, Mudumalai, Siruvani, Anamalais, Palani Hills, Meghamalai, Sirumalai, and Kalakkad-Mundanthurai.

Seasonality: Recorded mainly between July and January, with scattered records in March-May.

Flight & behaviour

Elusive shade-hugger that flits low through undergrowth and rests on tree trunks or fallen twigs, occasionally feeding on fermenting sap and dung.

Field note: Often called the Two-eyed Treebrown in South India and represented across Peninsular India by subspecies L. d. todara.
