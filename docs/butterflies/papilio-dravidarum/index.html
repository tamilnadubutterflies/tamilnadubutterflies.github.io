Field guide notes for Papilionidae · Papilioninae (True Swallowtails & Birdwings)

Family overview (Papilionidae, Swallowtails): Large to very large, strong-flying butterflies including birdwings, roses, mimes, Mormons, jays, swordtails, and banded peacocks. Many species carry prominent hindwing tails and feed actively while fluttering their wings at flowers.

Subfamily traits (Papilioninae): Includes Graphium jays and swordtails, Pachliopta roses, Papilio swallowtails, and the Southern Birdwing (Troides minos).

Typical habitats: Forest edges, hill streams, gardens, scrub, and evergreen shola margins across Tamil Nadu.

Field photography tip: Note hindwing tail shape, body colour (red vs black), and submarginal spot patterns. Both upperwing (dorsal) and underwing (ventral) photographs help separate mimics, mimes, and closely related dark Papilio species.

Dark white-spotted forest swallowtail endemic to peninsular Western Ghats

Identification marks

Upperwing: Dark velvety brown-black, paler toward the outer forewing; forewing shows a neat row of apical white dots and marginal white spots, while the hindwing carries white discal arrow-spots and marginal crescents without a tail.

Underwing: Warm sepia-brown with sharper white arrow-shaped post-discal spots on the hindwing and small white basal dots.

Lookalikes: Resembles the Common Mime ('clytia' form) or female Euploea crows, but carries pointed arrow-like white discal markings on the hindwing and lacks the Mime's golden-yellow underside spots.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Moist evergreen and semi-evergreen hill forests from foothills to 1,200 m elevation.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Western Ghats belts of Tamil Nadu including Mudumalai Tiger Reserve, Anamalai, Nilgiri slopes, and Kalakkad-Mundanthurai.

Seasonality: Mostly July through January during southwest and northeast monsoon humid spells.

Flight & behaviour

Keeps to shaded forest clearings and shaded coffee estates, flying steadily near understorey shrubs and damp stream gravel.

Field note: First recorded in the TNBS Mudumalai June 2024 survey highlight list and strictly linked to shaded Western Ghats habitats.
