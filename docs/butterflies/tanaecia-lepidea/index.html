Field guide notes for Nymphalidae · Limenitidinae (Sailers, Sergeants, Lascars, Barons & Clipper)

Family overview (Nymphalidae, Brush-footed butterflies): Tamil Nadu’s largest butterfly family (97 checklist species), encompassing tigers, crows, pansies, eggflies, sailers, barons, rajahs, browns, and Tamil Nadu’s state butterfly, the Tamil Yeoman.

Subfamily traits (Limenitidinae): Gliding butterflies with characteristic black-and-white banded wings (Neptis, Athyma) or emerald-olive camouflaged upperwings (Euthalia, Dophla).

Typical habitats: Forest trails, stream banks, orchards, and wooded gardens.

Field photography tip: Check whether the forewing cell streak is broken into spots or continuous, and compare underside ground colour. Record both basking upperside and resting underside views, especially for bushbrowns, treebrowns, sailers, and oakleaf butterflies.

Velvet-brown forest-floor glider bordered with a broad ash-grey band

Identification marks

Upperwing: Velvety dark brown patterned with obscure dark cell loops and bordered along both outer margins by a wide, continuous pale ash-grey terminal band that broadens toward the hindwing tornus; females are larger and slightly paler.

Underwing: Warm cinnamon-ochre to brown (paler on the hindwing) broadly suffused with lilac-grey along the outer borders and marked with wavy dark discal lines.

Lookalikes: Broad ash-grey outer border running continuously across dark brown wings easily separates Grey Count from Common Baron (Euthalia aconthea) and female Gaudy Baron (Euthalia lubentina).

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Moist deciduous and semi-evergreen forests, bamboo brakes, shaded forest paths, and wooded foothills.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Western Ghats forest belts of Tamil Nadu including Mudumalai, Gudalur, Siruvani, Anamalai, and Kalakkad-Mundanthurai.

Seasonality: Recorded mainly from August to February, peaking post-monsoon along damp paths.

Flight & behaviour

Flies low with rapid wingbeats and short flat glides close to the forest floor; feeds eagerly on fallen fermenting fruits, wet soil, and sap.

Field note: Peninsular subspecies Tanaecia lepidea miyana; prefers shaded forest undergrowth where its broad pale grey wing border acts as disruptive camouflage on dappled leaf litter.
