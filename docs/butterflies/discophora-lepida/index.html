Field guide notes for Nymphalidae · Satyrinae (Browns, Bushbrowns, Treebrowns & Rings)

Family overview (Nymphalidae, Brush-footed butterflies): Tamil Nadu’s largest butterfly family (97 checklist species), encompassing tigers, crows, pansies, eggflies, sailers, barons, rajahs, browns, and Tamil Nadu’s state butterfly, the Tamil Yeoman.

Subfamily traits (Satyrinae): Shade-loving brown butterflies marked with concentric underside eyespots (ocelli) that frequently exhibit wet- and dry-season forms.

Typical habitats: Grasslands, forest understorey, bamboo groves, palm groves, and shaded gardens.

Field photography tip: Underside ocelli sizes, brand marks, and discal line shape (straight vs curved) are crucial for species ID. Record both basking upperside and resting underside views, especially for bushbrowns, treebrowns, sailers, and oakleaf butterflies.

Elusive crepuscular bamboo specialist with violet-spotted males and gold-banded females

Identification marks

Upperwing: Stout-bodied deep velvety brown butterfly; males carry three curved rows of pale bluish-violet spots toward the forewing outer half and a dark velvety discal scent brand on the hindwing, while larger females show an oblique bright ochre-yellow forewing band and pale marginal spots.

Underwing: Warm buff-brown intricately striated like a dry bamboo leaf, bisected by a straight dark discal line and carrying two small muted ocelli on the hindwing.

Lookalikes: Unlike Palmking (Amathusia phidippus), lacks huge concentric hindwing eyespots below and tornal tails, and shows brightly spotted or banded upperwings.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Dense bamboo brakes and reed thickets inside evergreen and moist deciduous hill forests.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Moist Western Ghats forests of Tamil Nadu including Nilgiris, Gudalur, Mudumalai, Siruvani, Anamalais, Meghamalai, and Kalakkad-Mundanthurai.

Seasonality: Most often encountered between July and January following monsoon flush of bamboo shoots.

Flight & behaviour

Skulks deep inside shaded bamboo clumps by day and flies swiftly at dawn and dusk, feeding on rotting fruit, tree sap, and animal droppings.

Field note: South Indian and Sri Lankan populations represent a distinct Western Ghats lineage tightly dependent on healthy native bamboo and Ochlandra reed stands.
