Field guide notes for Lycaenidae · Polyommatinae (Grass Blues, Pierrots, Lineblues & Ceruleans)

Family overview (Lycaenidae, Blues and hairstreaks): Small, jewel-like butterflies (96 checklist species) including grass blues, pierrots, lineblues, ceruleans, silverlines, oakblues, royals, flashes, and sunbeams.

Subfamily traits (Polyommatinae): Delicate small blues with distinctive underside spot chains, zebra stripes, or white-and-black pied markings.

Typical habitats: Lawns, grasslands, damp sand, woodland edges, and hill paths across all elevations.

Field photography tip: Macro underside views showing hindwing sub-basal spots and tornal orange eyespots are essential. Sharp macro shots of the hindwing underside bands and spot alignments are usually far more diagnostic than upperside blue.

Shaded understory black-and-white blue with one bold subapical hindwing spot

Identification marks

Upperwing: Tailless and rounded; both sexes are smoky dark brown usually bearing a neat oval white discal patch on the forewing (sometimes reduced or absent in wet-season form dharma) and checkered wing fringes.

Underwing: Clean chalk-white displaying a large, unmistakable jet-black round spot near the hindwing apex (space 7), a small black dot near the inner margin (space 1a), faint broken grey postdiscal lines, and no series of black spots along the forewing costa.

Lookalikes: Separated instantly from Malayan (Megisba malaya) by its clean chalk-white underside lacking a row of four forewing costal spots and three hindwing sub-basal dots, leaving the bold black subapical hindwing spot isolated.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Shaded tropical dry evergreen groves, dense forest understories, bamboo thickets, and shaded orchards.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Occurs in shaded Western and Eastern Ghats forests plus coastal tropical dry evergreen groves in Tamil Nadu, including Guindy, Vandalur, Marakkanam, Point Calimere, Siruvani, Kallar, and Kalakkad-Mundanthurai.

Seasonality: Active year-round in shaded woodlands, peaking from September to March.

Flight & behaviour

Weak, slow, moth-like flutter just feet above shaded leaf litter beneath Glycosmis bushes; rarely leaves forest shade.

Field note: Subspecies Neopithecops zalmora dharma shows seasonal variation where monsoon individuals turn almost completely smoky brown dorsally, losing the white forewing patch.

Caterpillar host plants

Glycosmis pentaphylla (Orangeberry)Glycosmis mauritiana
