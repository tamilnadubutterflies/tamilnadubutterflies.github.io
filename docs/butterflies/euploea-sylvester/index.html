Field guide notes for Nymphalidae · Danainae (Tigers, Crows & Tree Nymphs)

Family overview (Nymphalidae, Brush-footed butterflies): Tamil Nadu’s largest butterfly family (97 checklist species), encompassing tigers, crows, pansies, eggflies, sailers, barons, rajahs, browns, and Tamil Nadu’s state butterfly, the Tamil Yeoman.

Subfamily traits (Danainae): Tough, unpalatable butterflies whose caterpillars feed on milkweeds and dogbanes; central to Tamil Nadu’s seasonal Eastern–Western Ghats migrations.

Typical habitats: Hills, plains, urban parks, and forest corridors throughout Tamil Nadu.

Field photography tip: Count subapical spots and pale streaks in the forewing cell to separate Blue Tiger vs Dark Blue Tiger and the Crow species. Record both basking upperside and resting underside views, especially for bushbrowns, treebrowns, sailers, and oakleaf butterflies.

Deep-brown migratory crow with iridescent blue forewing sheen

Identification marks

Upperwing: Dark chocolate-brown bathed in deep violet-blue gloss on the forewing, bordered by white spots; males carry two prominent parallel velvety brands on the forewing.

Underwing: Dark brown with crisp white marginal and submarginal spot rows.

Lookalikes: Two parallel forewing scent brands (in males) and rich blue sheen separate it from Common Crow.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Moist foothills, hill forests, and cross-state migration corridors.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Western & Eastern Ghats foothills, Mudumalai, Coimbatore, Salem, and Erode corridors.

Seasonality: Peaks during April–May and September–December migration waves.

Flight & behaviour

Glides powerfully alongside Dark Blue and Blue Tigers during seasonal Western–Eastern Ghats migrations.

Field note: Clean pair of pale silky brands on the male forewing reliably confirms field identification against Common Crow.

Caterpillar host plants

Ichnocarpus frutescens
