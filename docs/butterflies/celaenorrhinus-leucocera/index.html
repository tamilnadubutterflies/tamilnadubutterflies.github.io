Field guide notes for Hesperiidae · Pyrginae (Flats, Angles & Grizzled Skippers)

Family overview (Hesperiidae, Skippers): Compact, fast-darting butterflies (83 checklist species) such as awls, flats, angles, aces, bobs, demons, swifts, and grass darts.

Subfamily traits (Pyrginae): Skippers that commonly settle with wings spread flat against leaves or tucked underneath foliage.

Typical habitats: Forest trails, rocky streams, dry open grasslands, and hill clearings.

Field photography tip: Dorsal open-wing photos clearly reveal white hyaline forewing and hindwing spots. Photograph both the upperwing hyaline spots and the underside hindwing spot arcs, essential for distinguishing swifts and aces.

Familiar forest spotted flat with snow-white male antennae and fused discal band

Identification marks

Upperwing: Dark blackish-brown forewing crossed by a broad oblique white hyaline discal band where the cell and space 2 spots fuse cleanly, plus tiny subapical dots; hindwing carries dull orange-yellow spots and chequered cilia.

Underwing: Dark brown repeating the crisp white forewing band and dull ochreous hindwing spots; males display wholly white antennal shafts while females show brown shafts with white clubs.

Lookalikes: Connected white discal band and chequered margins separate it from Malabar Spotted Flat (C. ambareesa) and Tamil Spotted Flat (C. fusca); distinct yellow hindwing spots separate it from Bengal Spotted Flat (C. putra).

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Evergreen and moist deciduous hill forests, shaded estate roads, and forest understorey.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Common across Western Ghats (Nilgiris, Mudumalai, Siruvani, Kallar, Anamalai, Palani Hills, KMTR) and higher Eastern Ghats (Yercaud, Kolli Hills, Pachamalai).

Seasonality: Common year-round in hill tracts, especially abundant from June to January.

Flight & behaviour

Darts rapidly through dappled understorey before tucking flat beneath broad leaves; visits forest flowers early in the morning and mud-puddles along damp trails.

Field note: The gleaming white male antennal shafts are a reliable field clue even in dim forest light when resting beneath foliage.
