Field guide notes for Nymphalidae · Satyrinae (Browns, Bushbrowns, Treebrowns & Rings)

Family overview (Nymphalidae, Brush-footed butterflies): Tamil Nadu’s largest butterfly family (97 checklist species), encompassing tigers, crows, pansies, eggflies, sailers, barons, rajahs, browns, and Tamil Nadu’s state butterfly, the Tamil Yeoman.

Subfamily traits (Satyrinae): Shade-loving brown butterflies marked with concentric underside eyespots (ocelli) that frequently exhibit wet- and dry-season forms.

Typical habitats: Grasslands, forest understorey, bamboo groves, palm groves, and shaded gardens.

Field photography tip: Underside ocelli sizes, brand marks, and discal line shape (straight vs curved) are crucial for species ID. Record both basking upperside and resting underside views, especially for bushbrowns, treebrowns, sailers, and oakleaf butterflies.

Plain brown grass ringlet with four curved underside hindwing eyespots and no white dorsal patch

Identification marks

Upperwing: Uniform dull greyish-brown on both wings; forewing displays a large oval yellow-ringed bi-pupilled subapical ocellus and hindwing carries 2-3 small submarginal ocelli, completely lacking any white upperwing patch.

Underwing: Greyish-white finely covered with dense brown striae and a weak pale brown discal shading; forewing repeats the large bi-pupilled ocellus, while hindwing carries 4 yellow-ringed ocelli in a gentle curve (one apical, three postdiscal-tornal with the anal one often bi-pupilled), reduced to dots in dry season.

Lookalikes: Separated from White Four-ring (Ypthima ceylonica) by lacking the white hindwing patch above, and from Common Three-ring (Ypthima asterope) by having 4 (not 3) hindwing ocelli and no circular brown loop around the forewing ocellus underneath.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Open grasslands, scrub edges, wetland margins, gardens, and foothill clearings.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Statewide in plains, wetlands, and Western and Eastern Ghats foothills including Chennai, Coimbatore, Madurai, Salem, Erode, and Sirumalai.

Seasonality: Common year-round across Tamil Nadu, peaking during August-January.

Flight & behaviour

Weak, hopping grass flier that stays within half a meter of the ground, visiting small herbaceous flowers and basking on sun-warmed grass blades.

Field note: Often flies side-by-side with White Four-ring in Tamil Nadu scrub and grassy margins, making upperwing checks decisive.
