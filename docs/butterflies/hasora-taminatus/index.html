Field guide notes for Hesperiidae · Coeliadinae (Awls, Awlets & Awlkings)

Family overview (Hesperiidae, Skippers): Compact, fast-darting butterflies (83 checklist species) such as awls, flats, angles, aces, bobs, demons, swifts, and grass darts.

Subfamily traits (Coeliadinae): Large, swift skippers active particularly at dawn, dusk, and shaded forest interiors; several possess metallic-green or bright orange markings.

Typical habitats: Dense hill forests, bamboo patches, shaded ravines, and coastal scrub.

Field photography tip: Focus on the underside hindwing white/blue bands and dark tornal spots. Photograph both the upperwing hyaline spots and the underside hindwing spot arcs, essential for distinguishing swifts and aces.

Glossy brown awl flashing a metallic blue-green underside base and white band

Identification marks

Upperwing: Plain dark brown in males lacking any forewing sex brand and bathed in greenish hairs at the wing base; females carry two small pale yellowish hyaline spots on the forewing.

Underwing: Deep brown richly glossed with iridescent metallic blue-green across the basal half, cut sharply by a clean white straight hindwing band and a dark tornal patch; palpi are dull ochreous-brown.

Lookalikes: Metallic blue-green basal sheen on the underside and broader sharply edged white band distinguish it cleanly from Common Banded Awl (Hasora chromus) and Plain Banded Awl (Hasora vitta).

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Evergreen, semi-evergreen, and moist deciduous forest edges, shaded gardens, and foothills.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Widespread across Tamil Nadu Western Ghats (Nilgiris, Mudumalai, Siruvani, Anamalai, KMTR) and Eastern Ghats (Yercaud, Kolli Hills, Pachamalai).

Seasonality: Seen nearly year-round in hill forests, peaking from August to January.

Flight & behaviour

Flies swiftly at dawn, dusk, and under overcast canopy shade, nectaring eagerly at Clerodendrum and Leea flowers before snapping wings shut underneath foliage.

Field note: Nominate subspecies Hasora taminatus taminatus inhabits peninsular India and shares Pongamia and Derris climber hosts with Hasora chromus.
