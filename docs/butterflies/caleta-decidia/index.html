Field guide notes for Lycaenidae · Polyommatinae (Grass Blues, Pierrots, Lineblues & Ceruleans)

Family overview (Lycaenidae, Blues and hairstreaks): Small, jewel-like butterflies (96 checklist species) including grass blues, pierrots, lineblues, ceruleans, silverlines, oakblues, royals, flashes, and sunbeams.

Subfamily traits (Polyommatinae): Delicate small blues with distinctive underside spot chains, zebra stripes, or white-and-black pied markings.

Typical habitats: Lawns, grasslands, damp sand, woodland edges, and hill paths across all elevations.

Field photography tip: Macro underside views showing hindwing sub-basal spots and tornal orange eyespots are essential. Sharp macro shots of the hindwing underside bands and spot alignments are usually far more diagnostic than upperside blue.

Bold black-and-white forest pierrot marked by an angled white band

Identification marks

Upperwing: Dark brownish-black in both sexes crossed by a broad pure white median band angled sharply inward near the forewing costa like an elbow and extending smoothly onto the hindwing.

Underwing: Chalky white with stark dark brown to black patterns: a thick diagonal sub-basal bar from forewing base to mid-costa, an irregular broken discal band, and black submarginal spots lacking metallic blue scales, plus a fine white-tipped tail.

Lookalikes: Separated from Banded Blue Pierrot (Discolampa ethion) by lacking blue sheen on the male upperwing and metallic blue tornal scales below, and from Common Pierrot (Castalius rosimon) by solid bold bands rather than scattered discrete dots.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Moist and dry deciduous hill forests, scrub-forest edges, shaded trails, and stream crossings.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Widespread across Tamil Nadu foothills and hill ranges including Siruvani, Kallar, Mudumalai, Sathyamangalam, Anamalai, Kalakkad-Mundanthurai, Shevaroys, Kolli Hills, Pachamalai, and Gingee.

Seasonality: Common year-round in forested belts, surging after northeast and southwest monsoons.

Flight & behaviour

Flutters weakly through dappled forest undergrowth and congregates prominently in large mud-puddling groups on wet sand and animal scat.

Field note: Larvae feed on young Ziziphus foliage and are occasionally attended by ants; dry-season broods exhibit noticeably broader white dorsal bands.
