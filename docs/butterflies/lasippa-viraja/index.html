Field guide notes for Nymphalidae · Limenitidinae (Sailers, Sergeants, Lascars, Barons & Clipper)

Family overview (Nymphalidae, Brush-footed butterflies): Tamil Nadu’s largest butterfly family (97 checklist species), encompassing tigers, crows, pansies, eggflies, sailers, barons, rajahs, browns, and Tamil Nadu’s state butterfly, the Tamil Yeoman.

Subfamily traits (Limenitidinae): Gliding butterflies with characteristic black-and-white banded wings (Neptis, Athyma) or emerald-olive camouflaged upperwings (Euthalia, Dophla).

Typical habitats: Forest trails, stream banks, orchards, and wooded gardens.

Field photography tip: Check whether the forewing cell streak is broken into spots or continuous, and compare underside ground colour. Record both basking upperside and resting underside views, especially for bushbrowns, treebrowns, sailers, and oakleaf butterflies.

Golden-banded forest glider sailing over Western Ghats stream canopies

Identification marks

Upperwing: Velvety dark brown boldly banded in bright golden-orange; features an unbroken broad forewing cell streak extending well beyond the cell, wide oblique forewing spots, and two broad orange-yellow bands across the hindwing (wider in females).

Underwing: Paler warm orange-yellow with soft cream-tinted bands lightly edged in dusky brown, lacking heavy black borders.

Lookalikes: Much larger with brighter, broader golden-orange bands and an unbroken elongated cell streak than Pantoporia lascars; separated from female Colour Sergeant (Athyma inara) by its slender sailer body and single continuous cell streak.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Wet evergreen, semi-evergreen, and moist deciduous hill forests with cane and bamboo understory.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Western Ghats forests of Tamil Nadu including Mudumalai Tiger Reserve, Siruvani, Anamalai, Valparai, and Kalakkad-Mundanthurai.

Seasonality: Most frequently recorded from October to February and May to July across Western Ghats slopes.

Flight & behaviour

Sails effortlessly at mid-canopy height over forest paths and hilltops; males perch territorially on sunny projecting leaves and visit damp creek edges.

Field note: South Indian race Lasippa viraja kanara; highlighted among forest canopy species during Mudumalai Tiger Reserve butterfly census surveys.
