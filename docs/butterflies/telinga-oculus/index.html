Field guide notes for Nymphalidae · Satyrinae (Browns, Bushbrowns, Treebrowns & Rings)

Family overview (Nymphalidae, Brush-footed butterflies): Tamil Nadu’s largest butterfly family (97 checklist species), encompassing tigers, crows, pansies, eggflies, sailers, barons, rajahs, browns, and Tamil Nadu’s state butterfly, the Tamil Yeoman.

Subfamily traits (Satyrinae): Shade-loving brown butterflies marked with concentric underside eyespots (ocelli) that frequently exhibit wet- and dry-season forms.

Typical habitats: Grasslands, forest understorey, bamboo groves, palm groves, and shaded gardens.

Field photography tip: Underside ocelli sizes, brand marks, and discal line shape (straight vs curved) are crucial for species ID. Record both basking upperside and resting underside views, especially for bushbrowns, treebrowns, sailers, and oakleaf butterflies.

Identification marks

Upperwing: Rich rusty ferruginous-brown; forewing features a huge white-centered black lower ocellus broadly encircled by a luminous orange-red patch extending upward toward the costa plus a tiny subapical dot, while hindwing carries 3-4 small reddish-ringed ocelli.

Underwing: Warm ferruginous-brown turning paler ochre outwardly, crossed by a distinct dark straight discal line and bearing 2 forewing and 7 well-formed white-centered hindwing ocelli that are larger than in T. adolphei.

Lookalikes: Easily separated from Red-eye Bushbrown (Telinga adolphei) and Palni Bushbrown (Telinga davisoni) by its larger wingspan and the vivid, wide orange-red halo flaring across the forewing upperwing.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Montane shola forests, high-elevation evergreen edges, and shaded grassland borders south of the Palghat Gap.

Tamil Nadu distribution: High-altitude Western Ghats south of the Palghat Gap in Tamil Nadu including Anamalais, Palani Hills (Kodaikanal), Meghamalai, and Kalakkad-Mundanthurai.

Seasonality: Recorded year-round in high southern hills, peaking in February-May and August-December.

Flight & behaviour

Glides smoothly through shaded shola understorey and sunlit fern clearings, perching on low foliage and feeding on forest floor organic matter.

Field note: One of the signature montane endemics of the southern Western Ghats, closely tied to undisturbed shola-grassland ecotones.
