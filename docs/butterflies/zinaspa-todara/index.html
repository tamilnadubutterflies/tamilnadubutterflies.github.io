Field guide notes for Lycaenidae · Theclinae (Silverlines, Oakblues, Royals, Flashes & Yamfly)

Family overview (Lycaenidae, Blues and hairstreaks): Small, jewel-like butterflies (96 checklist species) including grass blues, pierrots, lineblues, ceruleans, silverlines, oakblues, royals, flashes, and sunbeams.

Subfamily traits (Theclinae): Often tailed hairstreaks with metallic silver bars (Cigaritis), deep violet-blue upperwings (Arhopala), or swift red-and-slate flashes (Rapala).

Typical habitats: Scrub jungles, forest canopies, mistletoe-bearing trees, and rocky hilltops.

Field photography tip: Capture the hindwing tornal lobes, tail count, and arrangement of underside bands. Sharp macro shots of the hindwing underside bands and spot alignments are usually far more diagnostic than upperside blue.

Acacia-feeding hill blue adorned with fine silver-edged ventral lines

Identification marks

Upperwing: Males are black with a brilliant shining violet-blue discal patch across the cell and lower half of both wings; females retain duller, paler purple-blue over the inner forewing and variable basal areas of the hindwing.

Underwing: Warm pinkish-brown to purplish-grey cleanly traced with fine brown discal and submarginal lines neatly edged on the outer side with glistening silvery-white streaks, a small orange-capped black tornal spot, and a single fine white-tipped tail at vein 2.

Lookalikes: Distinguished from Common Acacia Blue (Surendra quercetorum) by the crisp silver-white edges lining its underside brown bands, single fine tail in both sexes, and purple-blue scaling present on the female upperside.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Moist deciduous and semi-evergreen hill forests, bamboo edges, and Acacia-draped stream valleys.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Western Ghats and higher Eastern Ghats tracts of Tamil Nadu including Nilgiris, Mudumalai, Anamalais, Palani Hills, and Shervaroys.

Seasonality: Recorded mainly from September to April in hill forests.

Flight & behaviour

Brisk flyer around thorny lianas and mid-storey canopies; perches on sunlit edge leaves with wings held closed or half-open in early morning.

Field note: Named after the Toda people of the Nilgiris by Frederic Moore in 1883; the nominate subspecies Zinaspa todara todara is characteristic of the southern Western Ghats.
