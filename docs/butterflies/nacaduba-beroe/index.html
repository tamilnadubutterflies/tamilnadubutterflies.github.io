Field guide notes for Lycaenidae · Polyommatinae (Grass Blues, Pierrots, Lineblues & Ceruleans)

Family overview (Lycaenidae, Blues and hairstreaks): Small, jewel-like butterflies (96 checklist species) including grass blues, pierrots, lineblues, ceruleans, silverlines, oakblues, royals, flashes, and sunbeams.

Subfamily traits (Polyommatinae): Delicate small blues with distinctive underside spot chains, zebra stripes, or white-and-black pied markings.

Typical habitats: Lawns, grasslands, damp sand, woodland edges, and hill paths across all elevations.

Field photography tip: Macro underside views showing hindwing sub-basal spots and tornal orange eyespots are essential. Sharp macro shots of the hindwing underside bands and spot alignments are usually far more diagnostic than upperside blue.

Richly scaled six-lineblue with darkened wing bases and cloudy lines

Identification marks

Upperwing: Males are rich dark opaque violet-blue with narrow dark borders and noticeably darkened wing bases that hide ventral bands from above; females are brown with shiny blue confined to the basal half of the forewing.

Underwing: Darker warm brown with six-lineblue sub-basal lines inside the forewing cell (the lowest sub-basal costal spot in space 11 frequently absent), softer pale bands, weak internal dark striae in males, an orange-crowned black spot in space 2, and a fine white-tipped tail.

Lookalikes: More opaque and darker purple above than Transparent 6-lineblue (Nacaduba kurava), and differs from Rounded 6-lineblue (Nacaduba berenice) by weaker dark internal striae inside the ventral bands.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Evergreen, moist deciduous, and riparian hill forests from foothills to mid-elevations.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Found across Western and Eastern Ghats of Tamil Nadu including Siruvani, Kallar, Mudumalai, Anamalai, Kalakkad-Mundanthurai, Kanyakumari, Shevaroys, and Kolli Hills.

Seasonality: Active most of the year in forested tracts, most numerous between September and March.

Flight & behaviour

Darts along shaded forest paths and gathers in tight clusters on damp sand banks, salt-licks, and wet rocks.

Field note: Southern Indian populations belong to subspecies Nacaduba beroe gythion, whose caterpillars feed on tender shoots and flower buds of woody forest climbers.
