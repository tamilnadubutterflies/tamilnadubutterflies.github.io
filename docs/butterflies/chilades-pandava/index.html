Field guide notes for Lycaenidae · Polyommatinae (Grass Blues, Pierrots, Lineblues & Ceruleans)

Family overview (Lycaenidae, Blues and hairstreaks): Small, jewel-like butterflies (96 checklist species) including grass blues, pierrots, lineblues, ceruleans, silverlines, oakblues, royals, flashes, and sunbeams.

Subfamily traits (Polyommatinae): Delicate small blues with distinctive underside spot chains, zebra stripes, or white-and-black pied markings.

Typical habitats: Lawns, grasslands, damp sand, woodland edges, and hill paths across all elevations.

Field photography tip: Macro underside views showing hindwing sub-basal spots and tornal orange eyespots are essential. Sharp macro shots of the hindwing underside bands and spot alignments are usually far more diagnostic than upperside blue.

Garden cycad blue whose caterpillars feed on sprouting sago-palm fronds

Identification marks

Upperwing: Males are bright lavender-blue with narrow dark outer borders and a small dark tornal spot on the hindwing; females are dark brown with bright blue basal sheen and orange-crowned black marginal spots near the hindwing tornus.

Underwing: Pale buff-brown to grey-brown with crisp white-edged darker bands, four small black sub-basal spots across the hindwing, two prominent black subtornal eyespots in spaces 1b and 2 capped brightly in orange, and a tiny short tail at vein 2.

Lookalikes: Separated from Lime Blue (Chilades lajus) by its fine tail and orange-capped tornal spots, and from Small Cupid (Chilades parrhassius) by larger size, wider seasonal variation, and close fidelity to Cycas plants.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Urban gardens, parks, institutional campuses with ornamental cycads, and wild Cycas hill slopes.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Statewide across cities, towns, and forested Cycas hills of Tamil Nadu from Chennai and Coimbatore to Eastern and Western Ghats slopes.

Seasonality: Abundant year-round wherever cycad shoots flush, especially post-monsoon.

Flight & behaviour

Weak, busy flight circling ornamental cycad crowns; females lay dozens of eggs on tender uncurling fronds and adults visit small garden flowers.

Field note: Obligately ant-associated on cycad flush, where Crematogaster, Camponotus, and Tapinoma ants aggressively defend feeding caterpillars against predators.

Caterpillar host plants

Cycas revoluta (King Sago)Cycas circinalis (Queen Sago)Cycas rumphii
