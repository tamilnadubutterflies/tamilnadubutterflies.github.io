Field guide notes for Nymphalidae · Limenitidinae (Sailers, Sergeants, Lascars, Barons & Clipper)

Family overview (Nymphalidae, Brush-footed butterflies): Tamil Nadu’s largest butterfly family (97 checklist species), encompassing tigers, crows, pansies, eggflies, sailers, barons, rajahs, browns, and Tamil Nadu’s state butterfly, the Tamil Yeoman.

Subfamily traits (Limenitidinae): Gliding butterflies with characteristic black-and-white banded wings (Neptis, Athyma) or emerald-olive camouflaged upperwings (Euthalia, Dophla).

Typical habitats: Forest trails, stream banks, orchards, and wooded gardens.

Field photography tip: Check whether the forewing cell streak is broken into spots or continuous, and compare underside ground colour. Record both basking upperside and resting underside views, especially for bushbrowns, treebrowns, sailers, and oakleaf butterflies.

Large woodland sailer distinguished by chestnut streaks and spots beneath

Identification marks

Upperwing: Deep velvety black marked with bluish-white bands; the narrow forewing cell streak is separated from a short triangular spot beyond the cell, while the hindwing bears a crisp white discal band and narrow pale submarginal crescents.

Underwing: Warm pale buff streaked boldly with rich chestnut-brown and marked by a prominent curved row of large dark chestnut discal spots on the hindwing.

Lookalikes: Larger than Common Sailer (Neptis hylas) and separated from Short-banded Sailer (Phaedyma columella) by its narrower hindwing white band reaching closer to the margin, separated forewing cell streak and outer spot, and bold dark chestnut spots on the underside.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Moist deciduous and evergreen forest edges, wooded stream valleys, cashew groves, and shady rural gardens.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Widespread across Western Ghats foothills and moist hills (Mudumalai, Siruvani, Kallar, Anamalai, KMTR) as well as Eastern Ghats hills like Pachamalai and Kolli Hills.

Seasonality: Active throughout much of the year, peaking from September to January.

Flight & behaviour

Sails higher and more strongly than Common Sailer, circling tree crowns and gliding down to sunlit shrubs, damp paths, and flowering trees.

Field note: Peninsular race Neptis jumbah nalanda; named for the rich chestnut streaks and spots adorning its underwings.
