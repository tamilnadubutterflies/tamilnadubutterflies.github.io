Field guide notes for Nymphalidae · Limenitidinae (Sailers, Sergeants, Lascars, Barons & Clipper)

Family overview (Nymphalidae, Brush-footed butterflies): Tamil Nadu’s largest butterfly family (97 checklist species), encompassing tigers, crows, pansies, eggflies, sailers, barons, rajahs, browns, and Tamil Nadu’s state butterfly, the Tamil Yeoman.

Subfamily traits (Limenitidinae): Gliding butterflies with characteristic black-and-white banded wings (Neptis, Athyma) or emerald-olive camouflaged upperwings (Euthalia, Dophla).

Typical habitats: Forest trails, stream banks, orchards, and wooded gardens.

Field photography tip: Check whether the forewing cell streak is broken into spots or continuous, and compare underside ground colour. Record both basking upperside and resting underside views, especially for bushbrowns, treebrowns, sailers, and oakleaf butterflies.

Velvet-black and chestnut-underwinged sergeant with sailer-mimicking females

Identification marks

Upperwing: Strongly dimorphic: males are velvety jet-black with a divided reddish-ochre to dull white forewing cell streak and a crisp white postdiscal band across both wings; females are larger dark brown with a clean white cell streak and double white bands mimicking Neptis sailers.

Underwing: Males show a vivid bright chestnut-red ground crossed by wide white bands and a pale bluish-grey inner hindwing margin; females are duller ferruginous-brown with crisp double white bands.

Lookalikes: Males are unmistakable for their deep chestnut-red underside and jet-black upperside with divided forewing cell streak; females mimic large Neptis sailers but have a heavier body and the divided cell streak characteristic of Athyma.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Moist evergreen and semi-evergreen hill forests, bamboo-lined stream valleys, and forest clearings.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Western Ghats hill forests of Tamil Nadu including Mudumalai, Siruvani, Kallar, Anamalai, Palani foothills, and Tirunelveli hills.

Seasonality: Recorded most reliably post-monsoon (September to February) with scattered summer sightings.

Flight & behaviour

Rapid, powerful flyer patrolling sunlit stream openings; males puddle eagerly on wet sand and animal droppings while females keep closer to canopy foliage.

Field note: South Indian populations belong to Athyma selenophora kanara; females exhibit classic mimicry of distasteful Neptis sailers in flight.
