Field guide notes for Nymphalidae · Limenitidinae (Sailers, Sergeants, Lascars, Barons & Clipper)

Family overview (Nymphalidae, Brush-footed butterflies): Tamil Nadu’s largest butterfly family (97 checklist species), encompassing tigers, crows, pansies, eggflies, sailers, barons, rajahs, browns, and Tamil Nadu’s state butterfly, the Tamil Yeoman.

Subfamily traits (Limenitidinae): Gliding butterflies with characteristic black-and-white banded wings (Neptis, Athyma) or emerald-olive camouflaged upperwings (Euthalia, Dophla).

Typical habitats: Forest trails, stream banks, orchards, and wooded gardens.

Field photography tip: Check whether the forewing cell streak is broken into spots or continuous, and compare underside ground colour. Record both basking upperside and resting underside views, especially for bushbrowns, treebrowns, sailers, and oakleaf butterflies.

Iridescent olive-green canopy baron spotted in vivid crimson and white

Identification marks

Upperwing: Dark iridescent olive-green; males show crimson forewing cell bars, small white discal spots, and black-rimmed crimson spots on the hindwing; females are larger and duller with a bold oblique black-edged white forewing band and a bluish-green outer flush on the hindwing.

Underwing: Dark purplish-ochre brown with bright crimson basal costal streaks and crisp red spots in both sexes, females displaying a broad pale greenish-blue wash across the hindwing tornus.

Lookalikes: Crimson cell bars and red hindwing spots distinguish it immediately from Common Baron (Euthalia aconthea) and Grey Count (Tanaecia lepidea); far smaller than Redspot Duke (Dophla evelina) and with crimson postdiscal hindwing spots instead of basal rings.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Moist deciduous and semi-evergreen forests, wooded hill gardens, orchard edges, and tree canopies hosting parasitic mistletoes.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Scattered across Western Ghats foothills and hills (Mudumalai, Siruvani, Anamalai, KMTR) as well as wooded Eastern Ghats hills and green corridors.

Seasonality: Most noticeable from August to January, though present year-round near mistletoe-laden trees.

Flight & behaviour

Rapid, powerful flight high around tree canopies; drops down to feed on overripe fruits, tree sap, and damp tracks, basking flat on sunlit leaves.

Field note: South Indian race Euthalia lubentina arasada; caterpillars feed exclusively on woody Loranthaceae mistletoes growing on forest and orchard trees, camouflaged with long feathery lateral spines.
