Field guide notes for Lycaenidae · Theclinae (Silverlines, Oakblues, Royals, Flashes & Yamfly)

Family overview (Lycaenidae, Blues and hairstreaks): Small, jewel-like butterflies (96 checklist species) including grass blues, pierrots, lineblues, ceruleans, silverlines, oakblues, royals, flashes, and sunbeams.

Subfamily traits (Theclinae): Often tailed hairstreaks with metallic silver bars (Cigaritis), deep violet-blue upperwings (Arhopala), or swift red-and-slate flashes (Rapala).

Typical habitats: Scrub jungles, forest canopies, mistletoe-bearing trees, and rocky hilltops.

Field photography tip: Capture the hindwing tornal lobes, tail count, and arrangement of underside bands. Sharp macro shots of the hindwing underside bands and spot alignments are usually far more diagnostic than upperside blue.

Long-tailed forest hairstreak streaming two ribbon-like white tails

Identification marks

Upperwing: Males are rich dark purple-brown across both wings with two white-barred black tornal spots; females are plain dark brown with larger white tornal patches above the long tails.

Underwing: Creamy-white shaded warmly with orange-ochreous toward the forewing apex and outer margin, crossed by fine black discal dashes, black tornal spots crowned with metallic silver-green scales, and two pure white tails at veins 1b and 2 with vein 2 reaching up to 25 mm.

Lookalikes: Much larger and warmer-tinted beneath than Fluffy Tit (Zeltus amasa), with a plain brown-purple upperside and dramatically long ribbon-like tails reaching 20-25 mm.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Wet evergreen, semi-evergreen, and moist deciduous hill forests, shaded trails, and forest-stream openings.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Western Ghats stretches of Tamil Nadu including Nilgiris, Mudumalai, Kallar, Siruvani, Anamalais, Meghamalai, and KMTR.

Seasonality: Present across most of the year in wet forests, most abundant from September to March.

Flight & behaviour

Graceful, swooping flight along shaded forest edges; perches with wings closed on sunlit shrubs where its long white ribbon tails sway gently in air currents.

Field note: Southern Indian subspecies Cheritra freja butleri is a classic Western Ghats forest highlight; those colossal tails break easily when birds strike the false head, sparing the body.
