Field guide notes for Hesperiidae · Hesperiinae (Grass Skippers, Swifts, Aces & Darts)

Family overview (Hesperiidae, Skippers): Compact, fast-darting butterflies (83 checklist species) such as awls, flats, angles, aces, bobs, demons, swifts, and grass darts.

Subfamily traits (Hesperiinae): Fast-flying tawny, brown, or black-and-yellow skippers that typically hold forewings and hindwings at different angles when basking.

Typical habitats: Grasslands, paddy margins, palms, bamboo thickets, and forest undergrowth.

Field photography tip: Zoom into the underside hindwing spot pattern and forewing cell spots to distinguish swifts and darts. Photograph both the upperwing hyaline spots and the underside hindwing spot arcs, essential for distinguishing swifts and aces.

Identification marks

Upperwing: Dark velvety brown forewing marked with four small translucent white spots (two minute dots in the cell and small spots in spaces 2 and 3, plus occasional faint subapical specks); hindwing completely plain dark brown.

Underwing: Pale lilac-grey to vinaceous-buff across the hindwing and forewing apex, bearing a highly distinctive rosette of six small dark brown spots arranged in a neat ring encircling a single central dark dot on the hindwing disc.

Lookalikes: Separated from Indian Palm Bob (Suastus gremius) and Small Palm Bob (Suastus minutus) by the lilac-buff underside bearing a delicate rosette ring around a central spot; separated from Purple Spotted Flitter (Zographetus ogygia) by lacking rufous-ochre ground scaling.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Low- to mid-elevation moist evergreen and semi-evergreen forests, bamboo-rattan gullies, and shaded stream banks.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Western Ghats forests of Tamil Nadu including Siruvani, Anamalais, Nilgiri foothills, and Kalakkad-Mundanthurai.

Seasonality: Uncommon; observed chiefly from August to March along humid forested valleys.

Flight & behaviour

Flies swiftly low through shaded forest undergrowth and perches abruptly on overhanging cane tips or wet rocks in a jet-plane basking stance; visits damp stream sand and bird droppings.

Field note: Caterpillars stitch sections of thorny Calamus rattan leaflets into tight shelters hidden inside damp evergreen ravines.
