Field guide notes for Pieridae · Pierinae (Whites, Orange-tips & Jezebels)

Family overview (Pieridae, Whites and yellows): Medium-to-small butterflies dominated by white, yellow, orange, and black pigments, ranging from garden grass yellows and emigrants to hill-dwelling clouded yellows and forest albatrosses.

Subfamily traits (Pierinae): Includes Delias jezebels, Cepora gulls, Appias albatrosses/puffins, Colotis/Ixias orange-tips, Hebomoia, and Prioneris.

Typical habitats: Spans dry scrub plains (Colotis), urban avenues (Delias), and wet evergreen hills (Appias, Prioneris).

Field photography tip: Capture forewing tip markings and underside hindwing vein patterns. Capture the closed-wing underside when possible, spot counts in Eurema and wingbar markings in Appias and Ixias are key underside clues.

India’s largest pierid with blazing orange-tipped white forewings

Identification marks

Upperwing: Chalky white with brilliant vermilion-orange apical patches edged in black at the forewing tips.

Underwing: Mottled pale bark-brown underside camouflaging the butterfly cleanly against dry leaves when resting.

Lookalikes: Separated from smaller orange-tips (Ixias, Colotis) by its massive swallowtail-like wingspan and pointed forewing apex.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Forest streams, damp mud patches, deciduous hills, and shaded gardens.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Foothills and forests across Western and Eastern Ghats, including Kallar, Siruvani, Kovai Kutralam, and Pachamalai.

Seasonality: Seen throughout the year, peaking post-monsoon.

Flight & behaviour

Very fast, powerful flight above shrubs; congregates on wet sand alongside Graphium jays.

Field note: Largest pierid butterfly in India; its cryptic dry-leaf underside makes it vanish when perched with folded wings.
