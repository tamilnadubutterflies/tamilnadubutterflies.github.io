Field guide notes for Lycaenidae · Theclinae (Silverlines, Oakblues, Royals, Flashes & Yamfly)

Family overview (Lycaenidae, Blues and hairstreaks): Small, jewel-like butterflies (96 checklist species) including grass blues, pierrots, lineblues, ceruleans, silverlines, oakblues, royals, flashes, and sunbeams.

Subfamily traits (Theclinae): Often tailed hairstreaks with metallic silver bars (Cigaritis), deep violet-blue upperwings (Arhopala), or swift red-and-slate flashes (Rapala).

Typical habitats: Scrub jungles, forest canopies, mistletoe-bearing trees, and rocky hilltops.

Field photography tip: Capture the hindwing tornal lobes, tail count, and arrangement of underside bands. Sharp macro shots of the hindwing underside bands and spot alignments are usually far more diagnostic than upperside blue.

Steel-blue glossed silverline with tightly packed ventral silver bars

Identification marks

Upperwing: Males are pale brown with variable orange-red forewing markings and a striking bright plumbeous-blue gloss over the disc of the hindwing; females are larger and plain dark brown without blue sheen, showing a muted orange-red forewing patch and pale orange anal area.

Underwing: Dull greyish-ochreous to leaden buff crossed by closely set, densely packed reddish-brown bands heavily edged in black and lined with metallic silver; the tight band spacing leaves narrow pale interspaces, ending in two tails and an orange tornal spot.

Lookalikes: Separated from Common Silverline (Cigaritis vulcanus) by the male's bright leaden-blue hindwing disc rather than faint violet gloss and by much closer, denser underside bands on a duller greyish ground.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Moist deciduous woodlands, riverine scrub, bamboo margins, and mid-elevation forest clearings.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Mid-elevation Western Ghats foothills and Eastern Ghats ranges of Tamil Nadu including Nilgiris lower slopes, Kallar, Siruvani, Anamalais, Palani Hills, and Shervaroys.

Seasonality: Mainly post-monsoon and winter (September to March), with scattered records in summer months.

Flight & behaviour

Darting low-strata flyer that perches alertly on sunlit grass blades and forest-edge shrubs, occasionally mud-puddling along damp forest tracks.

Field note: Often shares sunny forest clearings with Cigaritis vulcanus and Cigaritis lohita; larvae shelter beneath bark crevices tended by arboreal Crematogaster ants.
