Field guide notes for Nymphalidae · Limenitidinae (Sailers, Sergeants, Lascars, Barons & Clipper)

Family overview (Nymphalidae, Brush-footed butterflies): Tamil Nadu’s largest butterfly family (97 checklist species), encompassing tigers, crows, pansies, eggflies, sailers, barons, rajahs, browns, and Tamil Nadu’s state butterfly, the Tamil Yeoman.

Subfamily traits (Limenitidinae): Gliding butterflies with characteristic black-and-white banded wings (Neptis, Athyma) or emerald-olive camouflaged upperwings (Euthalia, Dophla).

Typical habitats: Forest trails, stream banks, orchards, and wooded gardens.

Field photography tip: Check whether the forewing cell streak is broken into spots or continuous, and compare underside ground colour. Record both basking upperside and resting underside views, especially for bushbrowns, treebrowns, sailers, and oakleaf butterflies.

Boldly black-and-white sergeant marked by black dots inside hindwing spots

Identification marks

Upperwing: Velvety blackish-brown with crisp snow-white bands; the forewing white cell streak is cleanly divided into four segments ending in an arrowhead-shaped outer piece, and the hindwing postdiscal white spots each enclose a neat black inner dot; sexes share the same pattern.

Underwing: Rich golden-ochre ground colour bearing the dorsal white bands boldly outlined in crisp black, retaining the diagnostic black dots inside each white hindwing postdiscal spot.

Lookalikes: Separated from all other Tamil Nadu sergeants and sailers by the four-part forewing cell streak, black-dotted white postdiscal hindwing spots, and bright golden-ochre black-outlined underside.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Open forest clearings, scrub-bordered hills, woodland trails, and foothill gardens.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Widespread in Western and Eastern Ghats foothills and mid-elevations across Mudumalai, Nilgiris, Anamalai, Pachamalai, Yercaud, and Kolli Hills.

Seasonality: Common year-round in hill forests and foothills, peaking October to February.

Flight & behaviour

Alternates brisk stiff-winged glides with quick flaps; perches conspicuously with flat-spread wings on exposed sunlit twigs and visits damp soil.

Field note: Unlike strongly dimorphic sergeants such as Staff and Colour Sergeants, both sexes share virtually the same crisp black-and-white livery, females showing a double row of lateral black dots on the abdomen.
