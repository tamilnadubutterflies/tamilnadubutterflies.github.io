Field guide notes for Lycaenidae · Theclinae (Silverlines, Oakblues, Royals, Flashes & Yamfly)

Family overview (Lycaenidae, Blues and hairstreaks): Small, jewel-like butterflies (96 checklist species) including grass blues, pierrots, lineblues, ceruleans, silverlines, oakblues, royals, flashes, and sunbeams.

Subfamily traits (Theclinae): Often tailed hairstreaks with metallic silver bars (Cigaritis), deep violet-blue upperwings (Arhopala), or swift red-and-slate flashes (Rapala).

Typical habitats: Scrub jungles, forest canopies, mistletoe-bearing trees, and rocky hilltops.

Field photography tip: Capture the hindwing tornal lobes, tail count, and arrangement of underside bands. Sharp macro shots of the hindwing underside bands and spot alignments are usually far more diagnostic than upperside blue.

Giant metallic-blue oakblue living in weaver-ant territories

Identification marks

Upperwing: Males flash brilliant deep metallic violet-blue bordered only by a hairline black margin; females show bright sky-to-purple blue restricted to basal wing discs framed by very broad dark black-brown margins.

Underwing: Silvery lilac-grey to pale pinkish-brown marked with bold chocolate-brown spots and angled discal bands edged in pale grey, a neat pair of square spots at right angles in spaces 4 and 5 of the hindwing, bright metallic green scales at the tornus, and a stout white-tipped tail.

Lookalikes: Larger and brighter blue dorsally than Centaur Oakblue (Arhopala centaurus), with paler pinkish-grey undersides lacking metallic green rims inside the forewing cell; much larger and cleaner-marked than Kanara Oakblue (Arhopala alea).

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Moist and dry deciduous forests, riverine gallery woodland, shaded hill valleys, and wooded avenues.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Common in Western Ghats foothills (Kallar, Mudumalai, Siruvani, Anamalais, KMTR) and recorded across Eastern Ghats ranges including Kalvarayan, Shervarayan (700-800 m), Pachamalai, and Sirumalai.

Seasonality: Active across most of the year with strong post-monsoon numbers from September to March.

Flight & behaviour

High-perching forest canopy flyer that descends to lower twigs in morning sun, basks briefly with partially spread wings, and feeds on tree sap or damp mineral patches.

Field note: Subspecies Arhopala amantes amantes maintains an obligate partnership with Asian weaver ants (Oecophylla smaragdina), whose aggressive patrols shield larvae on host foliage.
