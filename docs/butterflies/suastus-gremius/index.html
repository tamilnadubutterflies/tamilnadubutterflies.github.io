Field guide notes for Hesperiidae · Hesperiinae (Grass Skippers, Swifts, Aces & Darts)

Family overview (Hesperiidae, Skippers): Compact, fast-darting butterflies (83 checklist species) such as awls, flats, angles, aces, bobs, demons, swifts, and grass darts.

Subfamily traits (Hesperiinae): Fast-flying tawny, brown, or black-and-yellow skippers that typically hold forewings and hindwings at different angles when basking.

Typical habitats: Grasslands, paddy margins, palms, bamboo thickets, and forest undergrowth.

Field photography tip: Zoom into the underside hindwing spot pattern and forewing cell spots to distinguish swifts and darts. Photograph both the upperwing hyaline spots and the underside hindwing spot arcs, essential for distinguishing swifts and aces.

Sturdy brown bob patterned with bold black spots on a grey underside

Identification marks

Upperwing: Warm dark brown forewing carrying square pale-yellow spots.

Underwing: Smooth pale purplish-grey speckled prominently with sharp black dots across the hindwing.

Lookalikes: Distinctive sharp jet-black spots dotted across a smooth pale grey hindwing underside make Indian Palm Bob straightforward to identify.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Coconut plantations, urban palm avenues, coastal parks, and scrub.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Common statewide across coastal towns, plains, and foothills.

Seasonality: Abundant year-round in palm-growing belts.

Flight & behaviour

Quick darting flight around palm shoots; visits Lantana and Ixora flowers in gardens.

Field note: Commonly encountered across urban coconut avenues as well as rural palmyra groves.
