Field guide notes for Nymphalidae · Satyrinae (Browns, Bushbrowns, Treebrowns & Rings)

Family overview (Nymphalidae, Brush-footed butterflies): Tamil Nadu’s largest butterfly family (97 checklist species), encompassing tigers, crows, pansies, eggflies, sailers, barons, rajahs, browns, and Tamil Nadu’s state butterfly, the Tamil Yeoman.

Subfamily traits (Satyrinae): Shade-loving brown butterflies marked with concentric underside eyespots (ocelli) that frequently exhibit wet- and dry-season forms.

Typical habitats: Grasslands, forest understorey, bamboo groves, palm groves, and shaded gardens.

Field photography tip: Underside ocelli sizes, brand marks, and discal line shape (straight vs curved) are crucial for species ID. Record both basking upperside and resting underside views, especially for bushbrowns, treebrowns, sailers, and oakleaf butterflies.

Unmistakable Western Ghats reed-bamboo specialist flushed with violet-mauve bands

Identification marks

Upperwing: Deep dusky fuscous-brown glossed with subtle violet; uniquely rounded forewing carries a curved crescentic pale violet-mauve band enclosing three small oblique white dots, and the hindwing extends into a sharp tail-like angle at vein 4. Females are slightly broader-winged with a softer mauve band.

Underwing: Warm pale ochreous-brown densely etched with fine dark brown striae and tiny obsolete submarginal ocelli, resembling dry reed foliage when perched.

Lookalikes: Unmistakable in Tamil Nadu by its convex forewing margin, mauve white-spotted forewing crescent, and pointed hindwing projection.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Undisturbed wild Ochlandra reed brakes inside wet evergreen hill forests of the southern Western Ghats.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Strictly southern Western Ghats of Tamil Nadu including Kalakkad-Mundanthurai, Kanyakumari hills, Meghamalai, and southern Anamalais.

Seasonality: Localized and seasonal, peaking after southwest and northeast monsoon rains from August to December.

Flight & behaviour

Weak, bouncing shade-dweller that flits rapidly between dense Ochlandra reed stems and rests hidden low near damp forest floors.

Field note: A monotypic genus endemic strictly to the southern Western Ghats; survival depends directly on conserving native Ochlandra reed habitats.
