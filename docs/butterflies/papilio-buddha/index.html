Field guide notes for Papilionidae · Papilioninae (True Swallowtails & Birdwings)

Family overview (Papilionidae, Swallowtails): Large to very large, strong-flying butterflies including birdwings, roses, mimes, Mormons, jays, swordtails, and banded peacocks. Many species carry prominent hindwing tails and feed actively while fluttering their wings at flowers.

Subfamily traits (Papilioninae): Includes Graphium jays and swordtails, Pachliopta roses, Papilio swallowtails, and the Southern Birdwing (Troides minos).

Typical habitats: Forest edges, hill streams, gardens, scrub, and evergreen shola margins across Tamil Nadu.

Field photography tip: Note hindwing tail shape, body colour (red vs black), and submarginal spot patterns. Both upperwing (dorsal) and underwing (ventral) photographs help separate mimics, mimes, and closely related dark Papilio species.

Iridescent green-banded endemic swallowtail of the Western Ghats

Identification marks

Upperwing: Velvety black outer wings with an exceptionally broad metallic blue-green basal and median sheen that shifts from emerald to turquoise in sunlight.

Underwing: Dark brown dusted with fine scales and pale submarginal crescents on the hindwing.

Lookalikes: Differs from Common Banded Peacock by its much broader blue-green wing base and narrower black outer half on the forewing.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Dense evergreen and semi-evergreen hill forests, river valleys, and high canopy.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Western Ghats ranges of Tamil Nadu including Mudumalai Tiger Reserve, Anamalai, Nilgiris, and Tirunelveli hills.

Seasonality: Mainly June to December, peaking during southwest and northeast monsoons.

Flight & behaviour

Canopy flyer that swoops down along forest streams and occasionally puddles on damp gravel in Mudumalai and Anamalai.

Field note: Highlight sighting of the June 2024 TNBS Mudumalai Monsoon Butterfly Survey.

Caterpillar host plants

Zanthoxylum rhetsa
