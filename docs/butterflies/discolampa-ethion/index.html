Field guide notes for Lycaenidae · Polyommatinae (Grass Blues, Pierrots, Lineblues & Ceruleans)

Family overview (Lycaenidae, Blues and hairstreaks): Small, jewel-like butterflies (96 checklist species) including grass blues, pierrots, lineblues, ceruleans, silverlines, oakblues, royals, flashes, and sunbeams.

Subfamily traits (Polyommatinae): Delicate small blues with distinctive underside spot chains, zebra stripes, or white-and-black pied markings.

Typical habitats: Lawns, grasslands, damp sand, woodland edges, and hill paths across all elevations.

Field photography tip: Macro underside views showing hindwing sub-basal spots and tornal orange eyespots are essential. Sharp macro shots of the hindwing underside bands and spot alignments are usually far more diagnostic than upperside blue.

Identification marks

Upperwing: Males glow deep dark blue-purple overlaid by a broad shared white median band across both wings and wide black borders; females are black-brown with the crisp white median band and no dorsal blue iridescence.

Underwing: Snowy white neatly banded with two curved black sub-basal bars, broken black postdiscal bars, submarginal black spots dusted near the hindwing tornus with faint metallic bluish-green scales, and a fine white-tipped tail.

Lookalikes: Males are unmistakable with their dark iridescent blue gloss over a crisp white median band; females differ from Angled Pierrot (Caleta decidia) by parallel curved sub-basal underside bars and metallic bluish tornal speckling.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Shaded evergreen and moist deciduous forest streams, foothill trails, and bamboo corridors.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Well distributed in Tamil Nadu hill forests including Siruvani, Kallar, Mudumalai, Anamalai, Palani Hills, Kalakkad-Mundanthurai, Kanyakumari, Sirumalai, Shevaroys, and Kolli Hills.

Seasonality: Recorded throughout the year in moist hill forests, peaking from August to January.

Flight & behaviour

Weak fluttering flight inside shaded canopy gaps; males puddle eagerly beside forest streams and flash violet-blue when wings tilt to sunlight.

Field note: Closely tied to Ziziphus climbers along shaded stream banks; caterpillars chew characteristic troughs on young jujube leaves and are visited by ants.
