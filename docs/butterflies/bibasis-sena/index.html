Field guide notes for Hesperiidae · Coeliadinae (Awls, Awlets & Awlkings)

Family overview (Hesperiidae, Skippers): Compact, fast-darting butterflies (83 checklist species) such as awls, flats, angles, aces, bobs, demons, swifts, and grass darts.

Subfamily traits (Coeliadinae): Large, swift skippers active particularly at dawn, dusk, and shaded forest interiors; several possess metallic-green or bright orange markings.

Typical habitats: Dense hill forests, bamboo patches, shaded ravines, and coastal scrub.

Field photography tip: Focus on the underside hindwing white/blue bands and dark tornal spots. Photograph both the upperwing hyaline spots and the underside hindwing spot arcs, essential for distinguishing swifts and aces.

Chocolate-brown awl crowned by a fiery orange abdomen tip and red hindwing fringe

Identification marks

Upperwing: Unspotted velvety dark chocolate-brown across both wings, accented by a bright carmine-orange hindwing tornal fringe (cilia) and a vivid orange abdominal tip.

Underwing: Warm brown with a large buff-white patch along the inner forewing margin and a broad pure-white discal band with a soft outer edge across the hindwing; third palpal segment is slender and erect.

Lookalikes: Differs from Common Banded Awl (Hasora chromus) and White Banded Awl (Hasora taminatus) by its bright orange-tipped abdomen, carmine-red hindwing cilia, and wide buff-white inner forewing patch underneath.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Moist deciduous and evergreen foothill forests, wooded stream corridors, and bamboo-shaded ravines.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Western Ghats tracts of Tamil Nadu including Nilgiris, Mudumalai, Kallar, Siruvani, Anamalai, and Kalakkad-Mundanthurai.

Seasonality: Recorded mostly during monsoon and post-monsoon months from June to December, with occasional dry-season sightings.

Flight & behaviour

Darts rapidly along shaded trails at dawn, dusk, and in overcast weather, settling upright with closed wings beneath broad leaves and visiting bird droppings or forest flowers.

Field note: Often placed in the genus Bibasis on Indian checklists; the bright orange abdominal tip shows clearly even when the butterfly perches with closed wings.
