Field guide notes for Lycaenidae · Theclinae (Silverlines, Oakblues, Royals, Flashes & Yamfly)

Family overview (Lycaenidae, Blues and hairstreaks): Small, jewel-like butterflies (96 checklist species) including grass blues, pierrots, lineblues, ceruleans, silverlines, oakblues, royals, flashes, and sunbeams.

Subfamily traits (Theclinae): Often tailed hairstreaks with metallic silver bars (Cigaritis), deep violet-blue upperwings (Arhopala), or swift red-and-slate flashes (Rapala).

Typical habitats: Scrub jungles, forest canopies, mistletoe-bearing trees, and rocky hilltops.

Field photography tip: Capture the hindwing tornal lobes, tail count, and arrangement of underside bands. Sharp macro shots of the hindwing underside bands and spot alignments are usually far more diagnostic than upperside blue.

Three-tailed blue-and-white forest gem showing a crisp white ventral band

Identification marks

Upperwing: Bright shining sky-to-royal blue over the inner wings broadly bordered in black across the forewing outer half and costa, punctuated by a clean white central patch on the forewing; females are duller blue with a larger white discal patch.

Underwing: Warm ochreous-brown crossed cleanly across both wings by a straight, dark-edged snow-white discal band that continues to vein 1a on the hindwing, complemented by metallic green-blue tornal scales and three white-tipped black tails at veins 1b, 2, and 3.

Lookalikes: Unmistakable in Tamil Nadu by the stark white forewing discal spot above combined with three tails and a crisp straight white band across a cinnamon-ochreous underside.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Evergreen and moist deciduous hill forests, wooded clearings, and shaded stream corridors.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Tamil Nadu Western Ghats ranges including Nilgiris, Mudumalai, Siruvani, Anamalais, Palani Hills, and Kalakkad-Mundanthurai, plus moist Eastern Ghats tracts.

Seasonality: Observed mainly from September to March in moist hill ranges.

Flight & behaviour

Quick, erratic flier that perches high on sunlit tree-edge leaves and drops briefly to damp sand or low shrubs during morning sun.

Field note: Represented in southern India and Sri Lanka by subspecies Horaga onyx cingalensis; its three tails and white band create an effective false-head decoy.
