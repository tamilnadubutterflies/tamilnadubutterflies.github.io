Field guide notes for Hesperiidae · Coeliadinae (Awls, Awlets & Awlkings)

Family overview (Hesperiidae, Skippers): Compact, fast-darting butterflies (83 checklist species) such as awls, flats, angles, aces, bobs, demons, swifts, and grass darts.

Subfamily traits (Coeliadinae): Large, swift skippers active particularly at dawn, dusk, and shaded forest interiors; several possess metallic-green or bright orange markings.

Typical habitats: Dense hill forests, bamboo patches, shaded ravines, and coastal scrub.

Field photography tip: Focus on the underside hindwing white/blue bands and dark tornal spots. Photograph both the upperwing hyaline spots and the underside hindwing spot arcs, essential for distinguishing swifts and aces.

Elusive green-streaked awlet active at dawn and dusk in wet hill forests

Identification marks

Upperwing: Males are pale vinaceous-brown with yellowish interspace streaks and cilia; females are glossy bronzy-green shading to indigo-blue toward the apex, with yellow-banded black abdomens.

Underwing: Dark brown densely patterned with pale greyish-green streaks between every vein, broad pale inner forewing border, and bright orange-yellow underside thorax, palpi, and legs.

Lookalikes: Unmistakable underside pattern of pale greenish interspace rays cleanly separates it from Orange Striped Awlet (Burara jaina) and from the black-veined green underside of Indian Awlking (Choaspes benjaminii).

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Dense wet evergreen forests, shola borders, and epiphytic climber canopies at mid- to high elevations.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Localized in high-rainfall Western Ghats pockets of Tamil Nadu including Nilgiris, Valparai, Anamalai, and Tirunelveli hills.

Seasonality: Recorded mainly between June and December during wet monsoon conditions.

Flight & behaviour

Strong, swift crepuscular flier emerging briefly at dawn and twilight to visit canopy blossoms or damp streamside rocks, resting closed-winged in deep shade by day.

Field note: Represented in southern India by subspecies Burara gomata kanara, whose larvae shelter inside rolled leaves of Schefflera vines.
