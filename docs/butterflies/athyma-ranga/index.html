Field guide notes for Nymphalidae · Limenitidinae (Sailers, Sergeants, Lascars, Barons & Clipper)

Family overview (Nymphalidae, Brush-footed butterflies): Tamil Nadu’s largest butterfly family (97 checklist species), encompassing tigers, crows, pansies, eggflies, sailers, barons, rajahs, browns, and Tamil Nadu’s state butterfly, the Tamil Yeoman.

Subfamily traits (Limenitidinae): Gliding butterflies with characteristic black-and-white banded wings (Neptis, Athyma) or emerald-olive camouflaged upperwings (Euthalia, Dophla).

Typical habitats: Forest trails, stream banks, orchards, and wooded gardens.

Field photography tip: Check whether the forewing cell streak is broken into spots or continuous, and compare underside ground colour. Record both basking upperside and resting underside views, especially for bushbrowns, treebrowns, sailers, and oakleaf butterflies.

Uncommon wet-forest sergeant with white discal bands cut by black veins

Identification marks

Upperwing: Smoky black-brown (glossed faintly metallic blue in fresh males) lacking a solid white forewing cell streak, instead showing faint greyish cell smudges and broad white discal bands conspicuously divided by thick black veins; females carry broader white patches.

Underwing: Smoky olive-brown with diffuse greyish-white discal patches crossed by dark veins, bluish-grey cell streaks, and narrow pale submarginal lunules.

Lookalikes: Distinct from Common Sergeant (Athyma perius) and Staff Sergeant (Athyma selenophora) by lacking a clean white or red-tinged forewing cell streak and having broad white discal patches heavily veined in black.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Wet evergreen and semi-evergreen hill forests, wooded ravines, and shaded stream corridors.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Uncommon resident of Western Ghats hill forests in Tamil Nadu, recorded from Nilgiris, Anamalai, Valparai, Siruvani, and Kalakkad-Mundanthurai.

Seasonality: Scarce and seasonal, encountered mainly from September to January and April to June.

Flight & behaviour

Fast canopy and mid-story glider that descends to wet forest tracks for mineral puddling and rests flat on overhanging tree leaves.

Field note: Represented in southern India by the scarce Western Ghats race Athyma ranga karwara; closely tied to intact Oleaceae host trees in moist forest tracts.
