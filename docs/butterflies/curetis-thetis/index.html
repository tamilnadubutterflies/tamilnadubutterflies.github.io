Field guide notes for Lycaenidae · Curetinae (Sunbeams)

Family overview (Lycaenidae, Blues and hairstreaks): Small, jewel-like butterflies (96 checklist species) including grass blues, pierrots, lineblues, ceruleans, silverlines, oakblues, royals, flashes, and sunbeams.

Subfamily traits (Curetinae): Robust blues with brilliant silvery-white undersides contrasting against bright coppery-orange (males) or white (females) upperwings.

Typical habitats: Moist forests, riverbanks, and shaded forest edges where males bask high on foliage.

Field photography tip: Look at the direction of the dark underside discal line to separate Indian, Angled, and Siva Sunbeams. Sharp macro shots of the hindwing underside bands and spot alignments are usually far more diagnostic than upperside blue.

Coppery-orange and silver-white sunbeam of wooded edges and scrub

Identification marks

Upperwing: Males flash brilliant fiery coppery-orange framed in black; females are dark brown with crisp white central patches.

Underwing: Gleaming unmarked satiny silver-white on both sexes, making resting butterflies look like silvery leaves.

Lookalikes: Cleaner straighter underside post-discal dot row and slightly rounded forewing tip help separate it from Angled Sunbeam (Curetis acuta).

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Forest streams, coastal scrub, wooded parks, and riverbanks.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Widespread across coastal, plains, and hill forests of Tamil Nadu.

Seasonality: Year-round, common in winter and post-monsoon.

Flight & behaviour

Strong, rapid flight; basks with partially opened copper wings on sunlit foliage.

Field note: Dazzling silvery-white underside reflects light starkly while its orange upperwing flashes in rapid flight.
