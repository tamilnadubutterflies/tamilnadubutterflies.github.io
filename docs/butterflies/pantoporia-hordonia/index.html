Field guide notes for Nymphalidae · Limenitidinae (Sailers, Sergeants, Lascars, Barons & Clipper)

Family overview (Nymphalidae, Brush-footed butterflies): Tamil Nadu’s largest butterfly family (97 checklist species), encompassing tigers, crows, pansies, eggflies, sailers, barons, rajahs, browns, and Tamil Nadu’s state butterfly, the Tamil Yeoman.

Subfamily traits (Limenitidinae): Gliding butterflies with characteristic black-and-white banded wings (Neptis, Athyma) or emerald-olive camouflaged upperwings (Euthalia, Dophla).

Typical habitats: Forest trails, stream banks, orchards, and wooded gardens.

Field photography tip: Check whether the forewing cell streak is broken into spots or continuous, and compare underside ground colour. Record both basking upperside and resting underside views, especially for bushbrowns, treebrowns, sailers, and oakleaf butterflies.

Dainty orange-banded forest-edge lascar with continuous grey marginal lines

Identification marks

Upperwing: Dark brownish-black crossed by bright orange bands; the indented orange forewing cell streak merges smoothly into the upper discal band, and the outer margin bears a continuous pale grey submarginal line broader than the fine orange line outside it.

Underwing: Pale creamy-yellow with fine dark brown band edges and pearly sheen in wet-season morphs, shifting to clouded ochre-brown with blurred markings in dry-season broods.

Lookalikes: Very close to Extra Lascar (Pantoporia sandaka), from which it is separated by the continuous, broader grey forewing submarginal line (versus broken grey dots in sandaka), unnotched upper cell-bar edge, and absence of a shiny pale hindwing speculum in males.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Forest clearings, scrubby foothills, secondary growth, bamboo thickets, and garden fringes.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Widespread across Western and Eastern Ghats foothills and wooded plains, including Mudumalai, Siruvani, Kallar, Anamalai, Pachamalai, Kolli, and Javadi hills.

Seasonality: Common year-round, with peak numbers from August to January and drier seasonal morphs emerging January to April.

Flight & behaviour

Flutters swiftly near scrub canopies with short sailing glides, basking flat on sunlit bushes and probing Lantana and Chromolaena blossoms.

Field note: Dry-season P. hordonia morphs develop expanded orange upperwing bands and clouded undersides that can closely resemble wet-season P. sandaka.
