Field guide notes for Lycaenidae · Theclinae (Silverlines, Oakblues, Royals, Flashes & Yamfly)

Family overview (Lycaenidae, Blues and hairstreaks): Small, jewel-like butterflies (96 checklist species) including grass blues, pierrots, lineblues, ceruleans, silverlines, oakblues, royals, flashes, and sunbeams.

Subfamily traits (Theclinae): Often tailed hairstreaks with metallic silver bars (Cigaritis), deep violet-blue upperwings (Arhopala), or swift red-and-slate flashes (Rapala).

Typical habitats: Scrub jungles, forest canopies, mistletoe-bearing trees, and rocky hilltops.

Field photography tip: Capture the hindwing tornal lobes, tail count, and arrangement of underside bands. Sharp macro shots of the hindwing underside bands and spot alignments are usually far more diagnostic than upperside blue.

Cream-brown underside royal bearing a fine sinuous red-brown discal line

Identification marks

Upperwing: Males are deep glossy royal-blue with wide black apical and outer borders on the forewing; females are paler sky-blue with narrower, smoothly curved dark brown apical borders and dark marginal spots along the hindwing.

Underwing: Warm creamy-brown crossed on both wings by a crisp, narrow red-brown discal line that turns sharply angular toward the hindwing inner margin, accompanied by two orange-crowned black tornal spots dusted in blue-grey scales and two white-tipped tails including a broadened inner tail.

Lookalikes: Warm creamy-brown underside with a crisp red-brown discal line distinguishes it cleanly from the dove-grey Peacock Royal (Tajuria cippus) and the boldly two-toned white-and-brown Banded Royal (Rachana jalindra).

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Moist deciduous, semi-evergreen, and evergreen hill forests, shola edges, and wooded valleys.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Western Ghats hill ranges of Tamil Nadu including Nilgiris, Mudumalai, Siruvani, Anamalais, Palani Hills, Meghamalai, and KMTR.

Seasonality: Flies mostly from September to March across mid-to-high elevations.

Flight & behaviour

Fast canopy flyer that frequently hill-tops on wooded peaks and visits sunlit blossom clusters or damp forest-stream sand.

Field note: Males possess a specialized vein fold and hair tuft near space 1a on the hindwing; caterpillars feed on mistletoe flowers and tender leaves high in forest trees.
