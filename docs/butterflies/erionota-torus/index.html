Field guide notes for Hesperiidae · Hesperiinae (Grass Skippers, Swifts, Aces & Darts)

Family overview (Hesperiidae, Skippers): Compact, fast-darting butterflies (83 checklist species) such as awls, flats, angles, aces, bobs, demons, swifts, and grass darts.

Subfamily traits (Hesperiinae): Fast-flying tawny, brown, or black-and-yellow skippers that typically hold forewings and hindwings at different angles when basking.

Typical habitats: Grasslands, paddy margins, palms, bamboo thickets, and forest undergrowth.

Field photography tip: Zoom into the underside hindwing spot pattern and forewing cell spots to distinguish swifts and darts. Photograph both the upperwing hyaline spots and the underside hindwing spot arcs, essential for distinguishing swifts and aces.

Huge twilight banana skipper with rounded forewings and bright crimson eyes

Identification marks

Upperwing: Very large dark chocolate-brown skipper with markedly rounded forewing outer margins and three broad, well-separated semi-translucent yellow-ochre spots on the forewing (one quadrate cell spot and two large discal spots in spaces 2 and 3); hindwing unmarked with pale tornal fringes.

Underwing: Smooth pale greyish-brown across the hindwing and outer forewing, repeating the three large yellow forewing spots without bands or dots on the hindwing; compound eyes glow ruby-red in life.

Lookalikes: Slightly smaller than Giant Redeye (Gangara thyrsis) and distinguished by rounded forewing termen and smooth unmarked greyish-brown hindwing underside lacking pale blue-grey scaling; vastly larger than Common Redeye (Matapa aria).

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Banana plantations, homestead gardens, moist forest edges, foothill farms, and shaded palm-rich valleys.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Widespread across Tamil Nadu banana zones and hills including Coimbatore, Kallar, Pollachi, Nilgiri slopes, Madurai, and Kanyakumari.

Seasonality: Active year-round across banana-growing belts and foothills, peaking during monsoon and post-monsoon months.

Flight & behaviour

Strongly crepuscular, sheltering motionless beneath banana fronds by day and flying powerfully at dawn and dusk to nectar at banana flowers, Lantana, and Ixora; occasionally suns briefly in early morning.

Field note: Larvae cut and roll thick tubular shelters from Musa leaf margins and dust themselves with powdery white wax; the convex forewing termen separates it from the Southeast Asian Erionota thrax.
