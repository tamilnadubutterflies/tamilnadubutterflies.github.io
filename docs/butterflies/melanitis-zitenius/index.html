Field guide notes for Nymphalidae · Satyrinae (Browns, Bushbrowns, Treebrowns & Rings)

Family overview (Nymphalidae, Brush-footed butterflies): Tamil Nadu’s largest butterfly family (97 checklist species), encompassing tigers, crows, pansies, eggflies, sailers, barons, rajahs, browns, and Tamil Nadu’s state butterfly, the Tamil Yeoman.

Subfamily traits (Satyrinae): Shade-loving brown butterflies marked with concentric underside eyespots (ocelli) that frequently exhibit wet- and dry-season forms.

Typical habitats: Grasslands, forest understorey, bamboo groves, palm groves, and shaded gardens.

Field photography tip: Underside ocelli sizes, brand marks, and discal line shape (straight vs curved) are crucial for species ID. Record both basking upperside and resting underside views, especially for bushbrowns, treebrowns, sailers, and oakleaf butterflies.

Largest Indian evening brown with hooked forewing tips and a broad ochre subapical patch

Identification marks

Upperwing: Rich warm brown bearing a sharply produced, sickle-like forewing apex; displays two dark subapical spots bordered broadly on the inner side by a bright orange-ochre patch that is especially wide and vivid in females.

Underwing: Wet-season morph shows pale striated brown wings with small neat white-pupilled submarginal ocelli (3-4 on forewing, 6 on hindwing); dry-season morph resembles a blotched, fungus-spotted dead leaf with obsolete eyespots.

Lookalikes: Substantially larger and more strongly falcate than Common Evening Brown (Melanitis leda) and Dark Evening Brown (Melanitis phedima), with a broader ochre-yellow patch extending well toward the forewing costa.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Dense evergreen and moist deciduous hill forests, tall bamboo stands, and shaded ravines.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Moist Western Ghats hill forests of Tamil Nadu including Nilgiris, Gudalur, Siruvani, Anamalais, Meghamalai, and Kalakkad-Mundanthurai.

Seasonality: Scarcer than other evening browns; encountered mostly during July-January with dry-season morphs lingering into April.

Flight & behaviour

Flushes explosively from leaf litter when disturbed and settles quickly with wings tightly shut, flying purposefully in deep shade and twilight.

Field note: Restricted to healthier forest interiors in Tamil Nadu compared to the garden-tolerant Common Evening Brown.
