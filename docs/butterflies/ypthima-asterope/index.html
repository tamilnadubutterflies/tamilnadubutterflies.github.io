Field guide notes for Nymphalidae · Satyrinae (Browns, Bushbrowns, Treebrowns & Rings)

Family overview (Nymphalidae, Brush-footed butterflies): Tamil Nadu’s largest butterfly family (97 checklist species), encompassing tigers, crows, pansies, eggflies, sailers, barons, rajahs, browns, and Tamil Nadu’s state butterfly, the Tamil Yeoman.

Subfamily traits (Satyrinae): Shade-loving brown butterflies marked with concentric underside eyespots (ocelli) that frequently exhibit wet- and dry-season forms.

Typical habitats: Grasslands, forest understorey, bamboo groves, palm groves, and shaded gardens.

Field photography tip: Underside ocelli sizes, brand marks, and discal line shape (straight vs curved) are crucial for species ID. Record both basking upperside and resting underside views, especially for bushbrowns, treebrowns, sailers, and oakleaf butterflies.

Sun-loving dryland ringlet sporting three underside hindwing eyespots and a ringed forewing loop

Identification marks

Upperwing: Pale greyish-brown; forewing features a prominent bi-pupilled black subapical ocellus enclosed inside a broad pale-bordered brown ring-loop, while hindwing carries 1-2 small dark postdiscal spots.

Underwing: Pale whitish-grey densely dusted with fine brown striae; forewing ocellus sits inside a sharp circular brown loop, and hindwing displays 3 small yellow-ringed ocelli (one apical, two near the tornus) that shrink to tiny dots in dry weather.

Lookalikes: Separated from Common Four-ring (Ypthima huebneri) by having only 3 (not 4) hindwing underside ocelli and a distinct dark circular loop bordering the forewing ocellus underneath.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Dry open scrublands, fallow fields, rocky grasslands, and sunny foothill paths.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Widespread across dry plains and rain-shadow foothills of Tamil Nadu including Coimbatore, Erode, Madurai, Tirunelveli, Ramanathapuram, Salem, and Chengalpattu.

Seasonality: Found year-round in dry plains and foothills, peaking during October-February.

Flight & behaviour

Weak, jerky flier hugging bare ground and sparse grass tufts, basking flat in hot sun and visiting tiny Tridax and weed blossoms.

Field note: One of the widest-ranging ringlets globally, extending from Africa across West Asia into India's driest Tamil Nadu thorn scrub.
