Field guide notes for Nymphalidae · Nymphalinae (Pansies, Eggflies, Admirals & Oakleaf)

Family overview (Nymphalidae, Brush-footed butterflies): Tamil Nadu’s largest butterfly family (97 checklist species), encompassing tigers, crows, pansies, eggflies, sailers, barons, rajahs, browns, and Tamil Nadu’s state butterfly, the Tamil Yeoman.

Subfamily traits (Nymphalinae): Familiar, colourful butterflies featuring conspicuous eyespots, seasonal morphs, and dramatic dead-leaf underside camouflage.

Typical habitats: Gardens, wetlands, agricultural bunds, scrublands, and hill forests.

Field photography tip: In Junonia pansies and Hypolimnas eggflies, both dorsal colour patches and ventral patterns aid rapid ID. Record both basking upperside and resting underside views, especially for bushbrowns, treebrowns, sailers, and oakleaf butterflies.

Hill-forest bark mimic flashing an electric turquoise band across dark wings

Identification marks

Upperwing: Deep indigo-black crossed across both wings by a broad gleaming turquoise-blue postdiscal band, with a crisp white subapical forewing bar and jagged, scalloped margins; females are slightly larger with a broader blue band.

Underwing: Dark charcoal-brown densely covered in fine wavy black bark-like striations and a paler median band, with strongly hooked forewing tips in dry-season morphs.

Lookalikes: Unmistakable in Tamil Nadu: far darker indigo ground and broader continuous turquoise band across both wings than Blue Pansy (Junonia orithya), and lacks the brown leaf-tail of Blue Oakleaf (Kallima horsfieldi).

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Shaded evergreen and shola forests, mossy stream banks, wooded ghats roads, and hill-station gardens above 800 m.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Western Ghats hill ranges (Nilgiris, Coonoor, Mudumalai, Siruvani, Valparai, Kodaikanal, KMTR) and high Eastern Ghats hills like Shervarayan and Kolli Hills.

Seasonality: Recorded year-round in cool hill forests, most numerous from September to January.

Flight & behaviour

Darting territorial flyer that rests head-downward on tree trunks, wet rocks, and damp paths, snapping wings shut to vanish against dark bark and feeding on tree sap and overripe fruit.

Field note: Southern Indian subspecies Kaniska canace viridis; its bark-textured underwing and angular wing outline make perched individuals virtually invisible on wet tree trunks.
