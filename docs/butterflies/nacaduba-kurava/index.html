Field guide notes for Lycaenidae · Polyommatinae (Grass Blues, Pierrots, Lineblues & Ceruleans)

Family overview (Lycaenidae, Blues and hairstreaks): Small, jewel-like butterflies (96 checklist species) including grass blues, pierrots, lineblues, ceruleans, silverlines, oakblues, royals, flashes, and sunbeams.

Subfamily traits (Polyommatinae): Delicate small blues with distinctive underside spot chains, zebra stripes, or white-and-black pied markings.

Typical habitats: Lawns, grasslands, damp sand, woodland edges, and hill paths across all elevations.

Field photography tip: Macro underside views showing hindwing sub-basal spots and tornal orange eyespots are essential. Sharp macro shots of the hindwing underside bands and spot alignments are usually far more diagnostic than upperside blue.

Frosted six-lineblue whose underside stripes show faintly through the wings

Identification marks

Upperwing: Males are bright pale frosted violet-blue with thin dark borders, translucent enough that the underside bands show subtly from above; females are dark brown with a pale iridescent blue discal patch turning whitish on the outer forewing.

Underwing: Pale greyish-brown with a full six-lineblue pattern including a pair of sub-basal white lines across the forewing cell, crisp white-edged postdiscal bands, weak dark internal striae in males, broad lunulate submarginal spots, an orange-capped black tornal spot in space 2, and a thin tail.

Lookalikes: Separated from Opaque 6-lineblue (Nacaduba beroe) by paler, semi-translucent wings and complete sub-basal costal spots; differs from 4-lineblues by possessing sub-basal white lines inside the forewing cell.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Evergreen, semi-evergreen, and moist deciduous hill forests, forest gardens, and stream edges.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Widespread across Tamil Nadu hills including Nilgiris, Siruvani, Kallar, Mudumalai, Anamalai, Palani Hills, Kalakkad-Mundanthurai, Shevaroys, Kolli Hills, and Pachamalai.

Seasonality: Common year-round in wet hill forests, peaking post-monsoon from September to March.

Flight & behaviour

Flies rapidly in bright clearings around flowering shrubs and puddles abundantly on wet soil alongside Ceruleans and Prosotas lineblues.

Field note: Females in southern India (subspecies canaraica) frequently display a striking whitish blur across the forewing blue disc, helping field separation from darker female congeners.
