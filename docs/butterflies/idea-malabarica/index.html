Field guide notes for Nymphalidae · Danainae (Tigers, Crows & Tree Nymphs)

Family overview (Nymphalidae, Brush-footed butterflies): Tamil Nadu’s largest butterfly family (97 checklist species), encompassing tigers, crows, pansies, eggflies, sailers, barons, rajahs, browns, and Tamil Nadu’s state butterfly, the Tamil Yeoman.

Subfamily traits (Danainae): Tough, unpalatable butterflies whose caterpillars feed on milkweeds and dogbanes; central to Tamil Nadu’s seasonal Eastern–Western Ghats migrations.

Typical habitats: Hills, plains, urban parks, and forest corridors throughout Tamil Nadu.

Field photography tip: Count subapical spots and pale streaks in the forewing cell to separate Blue Tiger vs Dark Blue Tiger and the Crow species. Record both basking upperside and resting underside views, especially for bushbrowns, treebrowns, sailers, and oakleaf butterflies.

Giant translucent white-and-black glider endemic to Western Ghats wet forests

Identification marks

Upperwing: Broad papery white wings veiled with charcoal-black veins and bold irregular black blotches and spots.

Underwing: Identical translucent white-and-black pattern visible cleanly from below as it floats through the canopy.

Lookalikes: Unmistakable by size, papery slow flight, and translucent black-spotted white wings.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: High-rainfall evergreen forests, shaded ravines, and undisturbed Western Ghats river valleys.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Wet Western Ghats enclaves in Tamil Nadu: Nilgiris, Anamalai/Valparai, Silent Valley borders, and Kalakkad-Mundanthurai.

Seasonality: Seen throughout the year in wet evergreen pockets.

Flight & behaviour

Floats gently like drifting tissue paper high among canopy lianas, occasionally descending to nectar at Ixora or Clerodendrum.

Field note: Listed as Near Threatened globally and relies strictly on intact, unlogged wet evergreen valley forests.

Caterpillar host plants
