Field guide notes for Nymphalidae · Nymphalinae (Pansies, Eggflies, Admirals & Oakleaf)

Family overview (Nymphalidae, Brush-footed butterflies): Tamil Nadu’s largest butterfly family (97 checklist species), encompassing tigers, crows, pansies, eggflies, sailers, barons, rajahs, browns, and Tamil Nadu’s state butterfly, the Tamil Yeoman.

Subfamily traits (Nymphalinae): Familiar, colourful butterflies featuring conspicuous eyespots, seasonal morphs, and dramatic dead-leaf underside camouflage.

Typical habitats: Gardens, wetlands, agricultural bunds, scrublands, and hill forests.

Field photography tip: In Junonia pansies and Hypolimnas eggflies, both dorsal colour patches and ventral patterns aid rapid ID. Record both basking upperside and resting underside views, especially for bushbrowns, treebrowns, sailers, and oakleaf butterflies.

Swift golden-tawny forest flyer disguised as a crisp dry leaf at rest

Identification marks

Upperwing: Rich golden-tawny shading into a broad jet-black forewing apex dotted with small white subapical spots, a pointed falcate forewing tip, and a short hindwing tail; females are slightly paler tawny with a broader pale subapical band.

Underwing: Remarkable dead-leaf camouflage ranging from warm autumn-ochre to deep purplish-brown, marked with a dark central midrib line from forewing tip to hindwing tail and scattered silvery or fungal-like spots that vary across seasons and individuals.

Lookalikes: Dorsal orange-and-black wings might briefly suggest Cruiser (Vindula erota) or Rustic (Cupha erymanthis) in flight, but the pointed falcate forewing apex, short hindwing tail, and dead-leaf midrib underside set Autumn Leaf completely apart (and Blue Oakleaf Kallima horsfieldi is indigo-blue above).

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Evergreen and moist semi-evergreen forests, shaded stream banks, shaded hill gardens, and humid forest clearings.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Western Ghats wet forest belt of Tamil Nadu, including Mudumalai, Nilgiri slopes, Siruvani, Kovai Kutralam, Anamalai, and Kalakkad-Mundanthurai.

Seasonality: Recorded most consistently from August to February across humid hill forests.

Flight & behaviour

Flies swiftly along shaded jungle trails before snapping down vertically onto twigs or undersides of leaves with closed wings, vanishing instantly as a dead leaf; males also visit damp sand and tree sap.

Field note: Peninsular India hosts the endemic race Doleschallia bisaltide malabarica; exhibits both seasonal polyphenism and individual underside variation so no two perched leaves look identical.
