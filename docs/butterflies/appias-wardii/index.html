Field guide notes for Pieridae · Pierinae (Whites, Orange-tips & Jezebels)

Family overview (Pieridae, Whites and yellows): Medium-to-small butterflies dominated by white, yellow, orange, and black pigments, ranging from garden grass yellows and emigrants to hill-dwelling clouded yellows and forest albatrosses.

Subfamily traits (Pierinae): Includes Delias jezebels, Cepora gulls, Appias albatrosses/puffins, Colotis/Ixias orange-tips, Hebomoia, and Prioneris.

Typical habitats: Spans dry scrub plains (Colotis), urban avenues (Delias), and wet evergreen hills (Appias, Prioneris).

Field photography tip: Capture forewing tip markings and underside hindwing vein patterns. Capture the closed-wing underside when possible, spot counts in Eurema and wingbar markings in Appias and Ixias are key underside clues.

Endemic Western Ghats albatross bearing broad black dorsal wing tips

Identification marks

Upperwing: Males are bright white with a broad jet-black forewing outer border containing small white subapical spots and a toothed black hindwing edge; females are white with wide dusky borders.

Underwing: Pearly white with pale yellow hindwings and soft ochre-yellow washing along the forewing tip.

Lookalikes: Males show much broader, white-spotted black forewing borders than Common Albatross (Appias albina) and lack Chocolate Albatross's dark brown underside rim.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Higher-elevation wet evergreen forests, montane sholas, and bamboo hill streams.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Localized in Nilgiris, Mudumalai higher slopes, Anamalai hills, Palani Hills, and southern Western Ghats.

Seasonality: Recorded mostly from September through March across wet hill ranges.

Flight & behaviour

Fast canopy and stream-corridor flyer; males congregate densely at muddy forest seepages during sunny intervals.

Field note: A Western Ghats endemic protected under Indian wildlife law and often misidentified as Lesser Albatross without careful border checks.
