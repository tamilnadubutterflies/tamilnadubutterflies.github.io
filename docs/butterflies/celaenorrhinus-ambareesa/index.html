Field guide notes for Hesperiidae · Pyrginae (Flats, Angles & Grizzled Skippers)

Family overview (Hesperiidae, Skippers): Compact, fast-darting butterflies (83 checklist species) such as awls, flats, angles, aces, bobs, demons, swifts, and grass darts.

Subfamily traits (Pyrginae): Skippers that commonly settle with wings spread flat against leaves or tucked underneath foliage.

Typical habitats: Forest trails, rocky streams, dry open grasslands, and hill clearings.

Field photography tip: Dorsal open-wing photos clearly reveal white hyaline forewing and hindwing spots. Photograph both the upperwing hyaline spots and the underside hindwing spot arcs, essential for distinguishing swifts and aces.

Endemic Western Ghats spotted flat dusted olive-gold with separated forewing spots

Identification marks

Upperwing: Dark olive-brown richly dusted with golden-olive scales; forewing spots are pale creamy-yellow and separated rather than fused, while the hindwing carries irregular rows of dull yellow spots and chequered cilia.

Underwing: Warm brown with golden-yellow scaling at wing bases, repeating the well-separated pale forewing spots and distinct orange-yellow hindwing spot rows; antennal shaft dark and chequered.

Lookalikes: Separated from Common Spotted Flat (Celaenorrhinus leucocera) and Bengal Spotted Flat (Celaenorrhinus putra) by its heavy olive-yellow dusting, well-separated discal spots, and spotted upper hindwing.

Habitat & Tamil Nadu range

Habitats: Moist deciduous, semi-evergreen, and evergreen forests, shaded hill roads, and Strobilanthes undergrowth.

Tamil Nadu distribution: Western Ghats ranges of Tamil Nadu including Nilgiris, Mudumalai, Siruvani, Kallar, Anamalai, Palani Hills, and Kalakkad-Mundanthurai.

Seasonality: Active most of the year in wet hills, peaking July to December.

Flight & behaviour

Fast shaded flier that rests flat with outspread wings beneath leaves, on mossy rocks, or inside culverts, emerging in morning sun to feed at Impatiens and Strobilanthes.

Field note: A Western Ghats and peninsular Indian endemic often found resting in cool stone culverts and rock overhangs during hot afternoons.
